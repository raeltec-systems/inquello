# Inquello — product brief

**Purpose and confirmed audience:** align the internal product and design team on the product direction before detailed UX and architecture. Concept and brand exploration exists; detailed experience validation remains ahead.

## Product and problem

Inquello is an agent workbench for audit and assurance: bring existing work, ask for an outcome, and inspect, revise and deliver the result. It is intended to give auditors the broad working experience Codex gives developers, adapted to their methods, evidence, artifacts and professional responsibilities.

The problem hypothesis is that auditors spend avoidable effort gathering context, transferring information among files and systems, repeating analysis, reconstructing evidence for review and keeping related outputs current. Inquello aims to reduce that total effort while preserving accuracy, traceability, confidentiality and human judgment. These benefits require validation with practitioners; the research does not establish measured savings or market demand.

## People and experience

The first validation setting is one organisation's audit team. The wider product serves internal audit, external audit, advisory and IT/IS audit across industries and methodologies. Practitioners prepare work; human reviewers challenge evidence and conclusions; leads coordinate assignments; specialists perform technical analysis; administrators and method owners govern access and reusable guidance. These responsibilities need not map to fixed job titles.

A practitioner starts with a question, folder, workbook, procedure or engagement. Inquello investigates, analyses, calculates, drafts and revises through permitted tools, with specialist delegation when useful. Sources, calculations, uncertainty and changes stay inspectable. The practitioner can edit the same supported saved files in Excel, Word or other applications and continue the task in Inquello. Review, professional approval and confirmed delivery remain distinct.

Method adoption begins with **explain → demonstrate → reuse → review → refine**, using instructions, examples, skills, tools, agents, configuration and memory. A formal engagement or universal control-testing model is not required. Technical users retain code, query and shell capabilities. The intended access surfaces span desktop, CLI, application-adjacent assistance and mobile/remote steering; detailed client support and deployment remain to be designed.

## Scope and direction

The accepted foundation is a Codex-derived workbench, essentially a fork adapted for auditors. **V1 targets the full researched capability mirror and required audit adaptations**, including automation and continuous assurance. This describes the complete V1 target, not a claim that every capability ships in its first release. Sequencing must name deferrals; deferred capabilities remain unmet scope.

| Phase | Record authority and scope |
| --- | --- |
| **V1 — external records** | Work with existing authoritative audit systems and supported files. Retain task history, evidence references, human review assistance, collaboration, monitoring and authorised external delivery. Remote updates are deliberate, reviewable and verified. |
| **V2 — native records** | Add authoritative audit records, relationships, attribution, version history, controlled amendments and applicable retention, recovery and export. Record-mode choice sits at organisation/workspace level. |

By default, the assistant works within the scope of a user-started task; preauthorised unattended work remains in scope. Actual organisational policy governs processing. No exclusive provider or deployment topology is selected; Bedrock and Azure Foundry/Azure OpenAI remain among the researched routes. Native audit-record foundations belong to V2 and are not prerequisites for V1. Broader portfolio, staffing and workflow-management additions remain unprioritised candidates.

## Why this approach

The intended distinction is the combination of broad agent execution, adaptable methods, continuity in existing artifacts and reviewable work across systems. Users should be able to teach the assistant their way of working without rebuilding every task inside a fixed audit workflow. This is a differentiation hypothesis to test against current tools and working practices, not an established competitive advantage.

## Success and next evidence

Success means **accepted work with less total effort**, while maintaining or improving accuracy, traceability, confidentiality and practitioner control. Measure preparation, review, corrections, retries and delivery together, alongside tool/model costs and recovery outcomes. Set numerical thresholds from the selected team's baseline.

The research provides proposed validation cases: an Excel reconciliation, a Word workpaper, an access review with controlled external delivery, and reuse of a taught procedure. Add external-audit/advisory cases and presentation round trips to test practice breadth and artifact fidelity. These cases guide validation; they do not reduce the V1 scope. The next step is to select participants, permitted examples and integration destinations, then compare the complete workflow with current practice. Product tests and practitioner validation have not yet been performed.

## Open decisions and dependencies

Guest participation, retained supporting evidence, cross-engagement recall, method distribution, reviewer roles and cost limits remain open. Team-only collaboration, necessary permitted extracts, approved carry-forward and combined task/team budgets are documented recommendations, not accepted decisions. Initial integrations, format/client support, release sequencing and quantitative targets also need selection.

Existing research identifies candidate reusable components but does not establish reuse of every desktop or hosted capability. Each capability needs a verified component or service route, appropriate access and actual organisational policy. Inquello remains the working name; naming and visual exploration do not establish final clearance or approved UX.
