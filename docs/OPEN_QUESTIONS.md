## First choices that steer the next research

### Q1. Who is the first real version for?

- **A — Your personal workbench.** Optimise for your work and your approved accounts; review and sharing with other people can still be studied. Lowest initial organisational scope.
- **B — One organisation's audit team.** Shared work, review roles, administration, continuity, and team data policies become first-order needs.
- **C — A product offered to multiple independent customer organisations.** Add customer separation, deployment/commercial terms, support, and broader administration needs from the outset.

**Selected: B — one organisation's audit team.** This supersedes the earlier recommendation A. It sets the first validation context, not the product's permanent audience. A team may still serve several clients and need confidentiality boundaries between assignments. Team administration, sharing and review now need explicit investigation; exact human/guest roles under Q6 remain open. Authentication and service eligibility must be checked for the intended delivery model.

### Q2. How much of the auditor's work should we validate first?

- **A — Start with individual tasks and outputs.** Validate research, data analysis, workpapers, advice, and reports first; formal engagements and both record modes remain in the accepted product horizon.
- **B — Standalone tasks plus formal engagements and follow-up.** Validate how an auditor can start small or manage a linked body of audit work in either record mode.
- **C — The wider audit function as well.** Extend the initial research to portfolio/annual planning, staff capacity, budgets, quality programmes, and management reporting.

**Recommendation: B.** It tests ad hoc work and end-to-end engagements together. This selects initial validation coverage; it does not remove established product goals. C would also test whether broader audit-function management belongs in the product perimeter.

### Q3. Where should the external-versus-built-in record choice apply?

- **A — Per organisation or workspace.** One authority model throughout that environment; simpler, but less flexible across assignments.
- **B — Per engagement, within organisation policy.** Each engagement declares its authoritative audit record system; standalone tasks declare a destination when needed.
- **C — Per record type or workflow stage.** For example, external planning with native workpapers and external findings; most flexible, with more ownership/conflict complexity.

**Selected: A — per organisation or workspace.** This supersedes the earlier recommendation B. V1 uses external records; V2 adds built-in mode and the mode-selection capability. Engagement-level mode switching is not the selected default. Transactional source systems can stay external in either mode. The exact organisation/workspace precedence and migration into built-in records are V2 design questions; lineage and retained approvals must be considered then.

### Q4. Beyond shared files, what should the platform do to remote audit records?

**Clarified baseline:** the product should work on supported saved artifacts that the auditor can also use in Excel, Word, PowerPoint, and other suitable editors. This path does not require an API connection to each editor. The choices below concern remote platform records and additional integration; they do not gate assistance with shared files.

- **A — Read/import and export work packages.** The auditor completes the final handoff; few integration side effects.
- **B — Read plus deliberate, reviewable writeback.** Prepare changes, show the intended destination, apply authorised updates, and verify the resulting external state.
- **C — Continuous two-way synchronisation.** Keep records aligned automatically, with explicit conflict, retry, deletion, and reconciliation rules.

**Selected: B — deliberate, reviewable writeback**, subject to actual access mechanisms and permissions. Import/export remains a useful fallback. This choice concerns audit work/records; it does not implicitly authorise modifying source financial or operational transactions, and no real writeback is authorised in this research phase.

## Product behavior preferences

### Q5. How should conversation and visible work fit together?

**Direction resolved by the user, October 26:** preserve Codex's underlying conversational workspace and inspectable work, redesigning relevant concepts and surfaces for auditors. The auditor can review work in the product or continue in their preferred external tools while receiving assistance.

This resolves the earlier experience choice broadly in the direction of B. Exact layouts, supported previews, editing depth, and review interactions remain research/design work. It does not imply rebuilding every external editor within the product.

### Q6. Who should collaborate inside the product?

- **A — The auditor privately, with outputs shared elsewhere.** The platform is a personal assistant and work area.
- **B — The audit team, including human reviewers.** Shared work and role-aware review; stakeholders can exchange evidence through existing tools.
- **C — Audit teams plus management, clients, and evidence providers.** Limited guest/request/response access as well as full auditor roles.

**Remaining recommendation: B for the selected team validation setting.** This is a proposal, not a recorded selection for Q6. Study guest/stakeholder access before promising complete in-platform collaboration. Agent reviewers do not replace the chosen human review chain.

### Q7. What should be the default level of agent autonomy?

- **A — Recommend and draft.** Humans initiate consequential execution or record changes.
- **B — Execute a scoped user-started task.** Agents can analyse and prepare/edit work within agreed boundaries; consequential actions and professional approvals have defined checkpoints.
- **C — Perform preauthorised recurring work unattended.** Agents run schedules, report failures, and escalate exceptions under a bounded mandate.

**Selected: B — scoped user-started execution as the default.** C remains in the full capability research as an explicitly enabled option. A recurring run finishing successfully does not itself approve a conclusion or close an audit finding. The decision sets a default, not the permanent ceiling of agent capability.

### Q8. How should methods and ways of working be customised?

- **A — Freeform instructions and templates.** Maximum ease of experimentation; consistent application and change control are harder.
- **B — Versioned method packs and reusable procedures, with controlled overrides.** Teams set terminology, methods, roles, criteria, templates, and permitted adaptations; each engagement records the version it uses.
- **C — A general workflow builder.** Users create their own objects, states, fields, rules, and automations; greatest flexibility and configuration burden.

**User direction: start from how Codex currently supports users' methodologies.** Investigate task context, persistent instructions, skills and references, specialist agents, tools, configuration, enforcement and memory before proposing the auditor equivalent. The earlier recommendation B is not an accepted design. Any method packaging should follow this evidence and preserve freeform work. Licensing of supplied third-party methodology remains a separate evidence question.

**Research result:** [CODEX_METHODOLOGY_STUDY.md](CODEX_METHODOLOGY_STUDY.md) now documents that baseline, with pinned source traces and proposed audit adaptations. Codex combines these mechanisms; they are not mutually exclusive product choices. Method governance, human roles and cross-engagement memory policy still require team evidence.

### Q9. What should accompany a result that someone relies on?

- **A — Source links and references.** Minimises copies, but links can change or disappear.
- **B — Necessary snapshots/extracts, capture metadata, and derivations, plus source links.** Retain enough permitted material and analysis to explain/reperform the relied-on result.
- **C — Copies of all source material brought into the task.** Broad replay context, with larger confidentiality, storage, deletion, and retention obligations.

**Recommendation: B**, governed by the practitioner's actual policy and source permissions. External-record mode also needs this work-product evidence; its approved destination may hold the final package. A copy or hash does not by itself prove authenticity, completeness, or evidence sufficiency.

### Q10. What context may carry across engagements?

- **A — Keep engagement knowledge separate.** New assignments start from general instructions and explicit inputs.
- **B — Reuse approved organisational knowledge and reviewed carry-forward material.** Keep provenance, versions, scope, and current permissions visible.
- **C — Automatically retrieve or learn across authorised engagements.** Greater convenience, requiring stronger relevance, confidentiality, correction, and withdrawal controls.

**Recommendation: B.** Distinguish reusable methods from client evidence, last year's conclusions, personal memory, and material whose access has since been revoked.

### Q11. What processing boundary must the future product respect?

- **A — Local working files/execution with approved remote AI services.** Desktop work can stay local while selected context goes to a model provider.
- **B — Managed cloud workspaces and approved AI processing regions.** Central access and continuity, subject to the organisation's actual data policies.
- **C — Private/on-premises execution and inference requirements.** Research whether the necessary capabilities and operating effort can be supported within that boundary.

**Agreed decision principle: follow actual organisational data policy.** A/B/C remain unselected pending that evidence. The folder-only instruction governs this research installation, not the future deployment topology. Investigate files, model inputs, connector calls, logs, backups and scheduled runs separately. Azure Foundry and Bedrock are provider candidates, not automatic answers to those location and access questions.

### Q12. What should the next research pass produce?

**Immediate sequence resolved by the user, October 26:** first produce an exhaustive functional mapping of Codex as it is to an auditor experience. For every capability, show how an auditor would use it and which enhancements make it audit-appropriate. Then plan the next V2 layer of additional functionality. Continuous assurance is only one example within the full scope.

The [feature overview](CODEX_AUDITOR_FEATURE_MAP.md), [detailed mirror](CODEX_AUDITOR_FEATURE_MIRROR.md) and [worked examples](CODEX_AUDITOR_WORKED_EXAMPLES.md) deliver this mapping. Demonstrations, practitioner walkthroughs and controlled workflow evidence remain later verification options. The earlier recommendation to start three workflow packs does not supersede the user's stated sequence. Neither implementation nor contacting others, purchasing access or using confidential material is authorised by this research request.

## Choices that can wait for representative examples

### Q13. Where should auditors directly edit their working outputs?

**Interoperability intent resolved by the user, October 26:** auditors should be able to review supported artifacts in the workbench and continue working on the same saved artifacts in Excel, Word, PowerPoint, or other suitable tools. Assistance should continue around that shared body of work. Existing files do not have to become proprietary native documents, and full embedded Office editors have not been requested.

The earlier native-versus-external alternatives are superseded by this stated intent. Remaining evidence work: format fidelity, formula/calculation behavior, references, comments, formatting, versions, review information, save/reopen behavior, and detection of intervening edits. Reading saved files, understanding live unsaved application state, and updating remote platform records are separate capabilities. Both record modes remain established independently of the editor a practitioner uses.

### Q14. Which data workload should the research prove first?

- **A — Documents and ordinary spreadsheets.** Evidence reading, extraction, reconciliation, working papers, and reports.
- **B — Large structured populations and systems data.** Database extracts/queries, complete-population testing, repeated analytics, and monitoring.
- **C — One representative case from each.** Study the transition between document evidence and larger structured analysis.

**Recommendation: C.** This preserves the breadth of the auditor tool while exposing different constraints. Actual file sizes, row counts, formats, network access, and permitted query locations remain evidence to collect, not numbers to invent.

### Q15. What does “everything Codex can do” mean for later delivery?

**Foundation and ambition resolved by the user, October 26:** essentially a fork of Codex, preserving its underlying experience and broad capabilities while redesigning appropriate aspects for auditors. The narrower-product alternative is no longer an unresolved default choice. Research should account for each capability and identify what is retained, adapted, extended, or still unverified.

This does not select an immediate parity deadline, a release sequence, or a particular implementation route. Public component availability, reuse eligibility, and services needed for particular capabilities remain evidence questions. Source boundaries should inform how to achieve the direction rather than silently narrow it. Any proposed scope change or omission should be made explicit in later planning.

**Further clarification, October 26:** first map the full Codex-to-auditor equivalent and its required audit enhancements, then plan V2 additions. This sets research/planning order, not an implementation deadline or permission to build. Required changes to formats, evidence references, task context or event adapters are part of making the direct equivalent work; they are not automatically deferred to V2.

### Q16. How should users control running costs?

- **A — Estimated cost and a limit for each substantial task.** Show likely consumption; seek a revised budget before exceeding the agreed limit.
- **B — A monthly user/team budget with warnings and quotas.** Simpler ongoing governance, with less control over the cost of an individual run.
- **C — Both task limits and an overall user/team budget.** Bound individual runs and cumulative consumption, with explicit handling when either allowance runs out.

**Remaining recommendation: C for the selected team validation setting.** This is a proposal, not a recorded selection for Q16. Turnaround versus cost is a separate preference to evaluate against actual workloads. Measure cost per accepted deliverable, including reviewer correction and retries, before proposing monetary amounts or assuming a subscription pays for embedded use.
