## GIAS ledger candidates

For each GIAS entry: source A1; publisher IIA; publication 2024-01-09; accessed 2026-11-05; class professional-standard assertion. Confidence is high in textual attribution, not in universal implementation.

- **GIAS-STRUCTURE — verified**, pp.7–8, glossary pp.12–13. Requirements are mandatory practices. Considerations describe common/preferred implementation practices. Evidence examples are neither requirements nor exhaustive ways to demonstrate conformance. Glossary distinguishes unconditional “must,” optional “may,” and preferred but nonrequired “should.” Do not promote an illustrative artifact into a mandatory standard.
- **GIAS-9.3 — verified**, p.65 (considerations continue p.66). CAE establishes, evaluates and updates methodologies and trains auditors in them. Considerations allow differing form/detail based on organizational circumstances, including individual documents, a manual, or audit-management software. No particular software product, data model, or template is mandated.
- **GIAS-9.4 — verified**, Requirements pp.66–67. Audit plan supports organizational objectives and is based on documented assessment of strategies, objectives and risks, informed by board and senior-management input and CAE understanding. Assessment occurs at least annually. Plan identifies needed resources and is dynamic. CAE communicates resource impacts, omitted high-risk assurance coverage rationale, competing demands and access/scope restrictions. Board approves plan and significant changes after discussion with board and senior management. Audit universe is an approach in Considerations, not a required artifact.
- **GIAS-12.3 — verified**, Requirements p.90; considerations pp.90–91. CAE establishes supervision/quality/competency methodologies; CAE or supervisor guides auditors, checks program completion and supporting workpapers. Supervision varies with maturity, auditor experience and engagement complexity. Qualified delegation is expressly permitted, with ultimate CAE responsibility retained. Supervision evidence must be documented and retained.
- **GIAS-ITERATION — verified**, Domain V p.92. The numbered presentation is not a mandatory linear sequence: engagement work can overlap and iterate; planning/risk assessment may continue during performance. Both assurance and advice follow Standards except individual stated exceptions. Advisory work does not authorize management responsibility. Appropriate responsibilities may be delegated to qualified internal-audit professionals while CAE retains ultimate accountability.
- **GIAS-13.2 — verified**, Requirements p.95. Understand the activity and assess/prioritize relevant risks, including fraud, using relevant information and management criteria. A formal documented risk assessment may be unnecessary for advisory services depending on stakeholder agreement. Previously identified risks can be reviewed and updated rather than assessed from scratch.
- **GIAS-13.3 — verified**, Requirements p.98. Document objectives and scope for each engagement; scope establishes boundaries sufficient to meet objectives. Consider assurance/advisory distinction. Discuss scope limitations with management and elevate unresolved limits to board through CAE methodology. Preserve flexibility to change objectives/scope; CAE approves these and changes. Considerations distinguish primarily auditor-set assurance scope from usually jointly set advisory scope.
- **GIAS-13.4 — verified**, Requirements p.100. Identify relevant evaluation criteria. Use adequate board/management criteria; discuss appropriate alternatives when inadequate. Depending on stakeholder agreement, advisory work may not need evaluation criteria.
- **GIAS-13.5 — verified**, Requirements pp.101–102. Identify resource types/quantity considering complexity, time frame and appropriateness/sufficiency of financial, human and technological resources. Discuss deficiencies with CAE to obtain resources.
- **GIAS-13.6 — verified**, Requirements p.103. Document a work program based on planning information and applicable engagement risk assessment. Identify evaluation criteria, tasks, methodologies/tools and assigned auditors. CAE review/approval precedes implementation and occurs promptly for subsequent changes. Considerations pp.103–104 discuss risk-control-test linkage, optional task completion/review fields and, if sampling is used, methodology, population, size and projectability.
- **GIAS-14.1 — verified**, Requirements pp.104–105. Gather and evaluate relevant, reliable and sufficient information supporting reasonable findings/conclusions. Sufficiency includes another prudent, informed and competent person being able to repeat work and reach the same conclusions. Determine additional collection when evidence is inadequate and whether inability to obtain relevant evidence is itself a finding.
- **GIAS-14.2 — verified**, Requirements p.106. Analyze evidence against criteria to determine condition and potential differences/findings. Evaluate whether more analysis is needed; required extra analysis entails adjusted and CAE-approved program. Advisory evidence gathering to develop findings may be unnecessary by stakeholder agreement.
- **GIAS-14.3 — verified**, Requirements pp.107–108. Evaluate each potential finding's significance; collaborate with management on root causes when possible, effects and significance. Consider likelihood/impact, report significant risks and prioritize findings using CAE methodology. Other risks' reporting depends on circumstances/methodology. Considerations p.108 give optional templates; this does not turn all listed template fields into unconditional gates.
- **GIAS-14.4 — verified**, Requirements p.109. Determine whether to recommend, request management plans, or agree actions collaboratively; intended outcomes include addressing gaps, risks and causes and improving activity. Discuss recommendations with management. Resolve disagreements using an established methodology allowing both positions/rationales.
- **GIAS-14.5 — verified**, Requirements p.110. Conclusion summarizes results against engagement and management objectives and auditor judgment on aggregate significance. Assurance conclusions additionally address governance/risk/control effectiveness, including effective processes. This is not merely a count or average of findings.
- **GIAS-14.6 — verified**, Requirements p.111. Documentation supports results and permits informed repetition with the same results; auditors and supervisor review accuracy/relevance/completeness, and CAE reviews/approves. Retention follows relevant law/regulation and function/organization policy. Considerations pp.111–112 allow templates or software and illustrate workpapers; no universally prescribed storage architecture follows.
- **GIAS-15.1 — verified**, Requirements p.113. Every final communication includes objectives, scope, conclusions and applicable recommendations/plans. Assurance adds findings/significance/prioritization, scope limits and effectiveness conclusion. Include responsible individuals and planned action dates, recognize pre-report actions, meet communication quality requirements and obtain CAE approval before issue. CAE disseminates to appropriate parties. Disclose relevant nonconformance and its reasons/impact. Considerations pp.113–114 permit varied formats/audiences.
- **GIAS-15.2 — verified**, Requirements pp.114–115. Confirm management implementation through established methodology: progress inquiries, risk-based follow-up and tracking-system status updates. Depth considers finding significance. For missed dates obtain/document management explanation and discuss with CAE; CAE assesses whether delay/inaction accepts excessive risk. Considerations p.115 permit software, spreadsheets or other systems and selective risk-based follow-up.
- **GIAS-11.5 — verified**, Requirements p.84. CAE discusses management acceptance beyond appetite/tolerance with senior management and escalates unresolved matters to board. Resolving the risk is not CAE responsibility.

## Overclaim adjudications

- **UNIVERSAL-RCM — overturned as a GIAS requirement.** A1 13.2 Considerations pp.96–97 describes a risk/control matrix as a possible technique and conditions its development on use. 13.6 p.103 permits linking risks/controls/tests. Neither establishes mandatory universal RCM format.
- **UNIVERSAL-5CS/TEMPLATE — overturned as a GIAS requirement.** A1 14.2–14.4 require evaluation and appropriate action, but 14.3 p.108 presents template use as optional. No compulsory artifact called “5Cs” was established. A practitioner mnemonic can help without being the standard.
- **ROOT-CAUSE-EVERY-FINDING — overturned if unconditional.** A1 14.3 p.107 expressly qualifies identification by feasibility (“when possible”). Keep collaboration/attempt and professional judgment distinct from requiring a known root cause for every case.
- **FIXED-SAMPLE / 5–20%-NORM — unverified as practice; overturned as attribution to these GIAS provisions.** A1 13.6 pp.103–104 and 14.6 p.112 discuss documenting methodology/population/size/selection, not a fixed size or percentage. Full supplied-text searches for sampling/sample-size plus close reading of relevant sections found no support for a 5–20% norm. This is not proof no industry-specific method exists. A norm requires a separate sampling authority and population/risk/error assumptions.
- **PERSONAL-CAE-CLICK — overturned as a GIAS mandate.** Approval responsibilities are real (13.3, 13.6, 14.6, 15.1), but p.92 and 12.3 allow appropriate qualified delegation while retaining accountability. The text does not specify a personal electronic button press, account model or signature mechanism. Do not erase approval obligations when correcting the click overclaim; local methodology and any jurisdiction-specific delegation limits still need definition.
- **HARD-SOFTWARE-GATES/ARCHITECTURE — unverified design claim, not a sourced standard.** GIAS specifies timing for work-program approval and report approval, supports documented supervision and record retention, and permits iterative work. It does not prescribe an app state machine, mandatory RCM screen, immutable ledger, database schema, retrieval architecture or universally blocking workflow. Such choices need separate design rationale and stakeholder validation.
- **RISK-ACCEPTANCE-EQUALS-AUDITOR-CLOSURE — overturned as equivalence.** A1 15.2 and 11.5 distinguish verifying implemented action from management's acceptance of exposure and CAE escalation. No universal closed-status taxonomy is prescribed. A software state representing accepted risk is a methodological design, not proof remediation occurred.
- **ADVISORY-EQUALS-FULL-ASSURANCE-EVIDENCE — overturned.** A1 pp.92,95,100,106 expressly qualifies advisory risk assessment, criteria and findings evidence by stakeholder agreement; 14.5/15.1 distinguish assurance conclusions/report content. Advisory is still subject to applicable Standards, not a blanket exemption.

https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr2602.htm

Do not present SR 11-7 as unchanged current guidance without this update. Confidence: high direct issuing-source attribution; shared interagency issuance is not independent research evidence.

### OCC-2026-13 — verified existence and limitations; contrary AI mandate overturned

Source: OCC Bulletin 2026-13, *Model Risk Management: Revised Guidance*, issued 2026-04-17:
https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-13.html

The bulletin rescinds the Model Risk Management handbook booklet and Bulletins 1997-24, 2011-12 and 2021-19. It expressly excludes generative and agentic AI from this guidance and says it sets no enforceable standards/prescriptive requirements. Applicability emphasizes over-$30-billion organizations, with possible relevance below that threshold for significant model exposure/nontraditional activities. Thus attribution of a binding, universal agentic-AI architecture requirement to this bulletin is overturned. Confidence: high textual attribution.

Additional primary source: joint Federal Reserve/FDIC/OCC attachment, *Supervisory Guidance on Model Risk Management*, 2026-04-17:
https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602a1.pdf
Read using web text extraction only. Printed p.2 footnote 1 preserves possible supervisory action for violations of law or unsafe/unsound practices. Printed p.3 footnote 3 excludes generative/agentic AI but points to institutions' governance for uncovered tools; traditional statistical/quantitative and non-generative/non-agentic AI models remain in scope. Printed p.10 describes internal audit, when part of the arrangements, as evaluating rigor/effectiveness and policy implementation rather than duplicating development/validation. ## Supplemental load-bearing checks and report-1 reconciliation

Requested after initial digest, checked against A1 in the same run. Metadata and direct-authority status convention above apply. The report-1 digest was read only to identify assertions, never as proof of their truth.

- **GIAS-9.5 / R1-13 — overturned: coordination is not optional.** Requirements p.69 require CAE coordination with internal/external assurance providers and consideration of reliance. If appropriate coordination cannot be achieved, raise concerns with senior management and if needed board. Actual reliance is conditional, but when used its basis must be documented and internal audit retains responsibility for its conclusions. Distinguish mandatory coordination/consideration from a duty to rely on every provider.
- **GIAS-13.1 / R1-17 — verified with scope**, Requirements p.93: effective communication throughout; communicate objectives, scope and timing to management and timely changes. At conclusion, discuss disagreement and seek mutual understanding. Auditors need not change results without valid reason; established methodology permits both positions/reasons to be expressed.
- **R1-21 — overturned citation, underlying requirement substantially supported.** Work-program content and prior approval are Standard **13.6 p.103**, not 13.1. 13.1 addresses engagement communication. Retain the substantive work-program requirement using the correct locator.
- **GIAS-11.2 / R1-38 — verified**, Requirements p.79: CAE establishes/implements methodologies supporting the specified communication qualities. Standard 15.1 separately applies those qualities to final engagement communication. These do not impose one universal report template or require every engagement report be distributed to the board. Under 15.1, CAE dissemination targets parties who can ensure results are considered; organizationwide board communication duties should not be conflated with a fixed distribution list.
- **GIAS-4.3 / R1-42 — verified**, Requirements p.33: professional skepticism applies during planning and performance; inquisitiveness, critical reliability assessment, candid questions about inconsistencies and seeking more evidence where information may be incomplete/inconsistent/false/misleading are required.
- **GIAS-5.1 — verified**, Requirements p.34: information use follows applicable policies/procedures/laws/regulations and cannot serve personal gain or harm legitimate ethical organizational objectives.
- **GIAS-5.2 — verified**, Requirements p.35: understand and comply with confidentiality/privacy/security duties; prevent unauthorized disclosure and manage inadvertent exposure; CAE ensures those assisting internal audit follow the same protections. Custody, retention/disposal, release and access/copies are relevant. No specific vendor, encryption implementation, data-residency geography or architecture is prescribed by this text alone.
- **GIAS-2.1 / R1-43 — verified bounded individual objectivity**, Requirements pp.20–21: impartial, unbiased judgments based on balanced relevant circumstances; recognize/manage potential biases. This is distinct from organizational independence.
- **GIAS-7.1 / R1-43 — verified bounded organizational independence**, Requirements p.46: annual board confirmation; report impairments/safeguards; charter records board-determined positioning/reporting; discuss impairing current/proposed roles and safeguards. Continuing non-audit CAE roles require charter documentation and alternative assurance for those responsibilities. Temporary non-audit roles require independent third-party assurance during assignment and next 12 months plus transition plan. Essential Conditions pp.46–47 separately specify board/senior-management support, including direct board relationship and noninterference. Do not collapse these conditions into personal objectivity or merely a software permission.
- **R1-15/44 — qualification**, 13.2 p.95, 13.4 p.100 and 14.2 p.106 contain specific advisory exceptions tied to stakeholder agreement. Do not apply the full assurance evidence pattern indiscriminately, or turn exceptions into blanket exemption. 13.3 p.98 requires CAE objectives/scope approval; p.92 supplies appropriate delegation context.
- **R1-26 — qualification**, 14.1 pp.104–105: information must be relevant, reliable and sufficient. Independent/direct acquisition, corroboration and effective source-system controls strengthen reliability; do not claim every evidence item must independently satisfy each listed strengthening route.
- **R1-31/33/36 — qualification/overturned where unconditional**, 14.3 pp.107–108: each finding is prioritized by significance using CAE methodology; this is required, not merely recommended. Root-cause identification retains feasibility qualification; generic assertions that missing root cause is automatically a failure overstate the text.
- **R1-32 — qualification**, 14.4 p.109: recommendation development, requesting management plans or collaborative agreement are alternative routes. No universal duty to author an auditor recommendation for every finding follows. Action ownership/dates are final-communication requirements under 15.1 p.113.
- **R1-07/08/10/11 — qualification**, 9.4 pp.66–67 requires plan/significant-change board approval and documented annual risk assessment; optional audit-universe format is not a mandatory output. 14.6 p.111 specifically governs engagement documentation, so applying its repetition test wholesale to portfolio planning is an extension, not the text of 9.4. No mandatory handwritten/digital signature or planning memo was established.
- **R1-39/40 — qualification**, 15.2 pp.114–115 plus 11.5 p.84 require risk-based implementation confirmation and excessive-risk escalation; “no new audit work” is misleading if read to exclude follow-up assessments or iterative work. Report issue and verified corrective-action completion are different events.
- **R1 common/frequent failure claims — unverified**, including R1-14/20/25/30/36/41: a standard proves the obligation, not observed frequency of nonconformance. No empirical QA dataset was checked here.

## Essential Conditions and prior-advisory objectivity clarification

- **GIAS-DOMAINIII-CONDITIONS — verified nuanced treatment**, A1 pp.37–38. Domain III sets CAE requirements while identifying board/senior-management activities as Essential Conditions foundational to effective internal audit governance. CAE must discuss the domain with them. If a condition is disputed, CAE must explain consequences of absence; discussion of equivalent alternatives is preferred. Agreement that a condition is unnecessary is possible, but CAE must document reasons and compensating alternative conditions. If CAE disagrees with refusal, CAE may conclude conformance impossible; documenting reasons, sharing positions and making records available to external assessor are described as preferred practices. Thus neither “optional GUIDE only” nor “every named board activity is an inflexible unconditional auditor action” faithfully captures the text. Identify the actor, condition, CAE obligation and documented alternative/conformance consequence separately. The board definition is contextual and can include an audit committee or another highest governing body.
- **GIAS-2.2-PRIOR-ROLE — verified; universal advisory cooling-off overturned**, A1 Standard 2.2 Requirements p.22. Avoid/mitigate actual, potential and perceived objectivity impairments, gifts/conflicts and undue influence. The 12-month presumption concerns assurance on activities for which the auditor had prior responsibility. Prior advisory service is addressed separately: CAE confirms its nature does not impair objectivity and assigns resources to manage individual objectivity. Advisory concerning an auditor's previous responsibilities requires disclosure of potential impairment to the requester before acceptance. Assurance over functions for which CAE is responsible requires independent external-to-function oversight. Therefore do not translate the prior-responsibility presumption into a blanket 12-month prohibition on assurance following advisory work.


## Verified compact working glossary

Source for all entries: A1, IIA *Global Internal Audit Standards*, issued 2024-01-09; readable text re-fetched 2026-11-05. **G** means a bounded paraphrase of an official Glossary entry, not a quotation. **C** means a working explanation synthesized from cited clauses, not an official glossary definition. These definitions establish vocabulary, not the prevalence of particular practices or a software schema.

1. **Assurance services [G]** — Objective assessments intended to increase confidence in governance, risk management and controls against criteria; assurance level depends on the procedures. Glossary p.10; Domain V p.92.
2. **Advisory services [G]** — Stakeholder-agreed advice, without providing assurance or assuming management duties. Glossary p.10. Specific evidence/planning exceptions depend on the engagement agreement: 13.2 p.95, 13.4 p.100, 14.2 p.106.
3. **Board [G]** — The highest governing body authorized to establish internal audit's authority and responsibilities; its name and composition vary. Glossary p.10; contextual definition p.38.
4. **Chief audit executive / CAE [G]** — Leadership accountable for managing the audit function and the quality of its services; title may vary. Glossary p.10. Appropriate delegation does not transfer ultimate accountability: p.92.
5. **Management / senior management [G/C]** — Senior management is the executive level accountable to the board for strategic execution (Glossary p.13). “Management of the activity” is used here operationally for those managing the reviewed activity, not as a separate official glossary definition; see 13.1 p.93 and 14.4 p.109.
6. **Engagement objectives [G]** — Statements of the assignment's purpose and intended goals. Glossary p.11; documented and approved under 13.3 p.98.
7. **Engagement scope [C]** — The assignment's focus and boundaries, including activities, locations, systems and time period, sufficient for its objectives. Standard 13.3 p.98. Not synonymous with objectives.
8. **Criteria and condition [G/C]** — Criteria describe the expected state (Glossary p.11); condition is the evidenced existing state (14.2 p.106). Their comparison supports evaluating a potential finding.
9. **Risk, appetite and tolerance [G]** — Risk is uncertainty's effect on objectives, potentially favorable or adverse. Appetite concerns accepted kinds/amounts of risk in pursuing objectives; tolerance concerns acceptable performance variation. Glossary p.13. These are related, not interchangeable thresholds.
10. **Control [G]** — Action by management, board or others to manage risk and improve achievement of goals; control processes are the organized policies, procedures and activities doing this. Glossary p.11.
11. **Engagement work program [G]** — Documented tasks, methods/tools and assigned auditors for meeting engagement objectives, developed from planning information. Glossary p.11; required content/approval in 13.6 p.103.
12. **Evidence [C]** — Information supporting analysis, findings and conclusions, assessed for relevance, reliability and sufficiency. Standard 14.1 pp.104–105. This is a working description, not a standalone official Glossary definition.
13. **Workpapers [G]** — Records of engagement planning and performance supporting findings and conclusions. Glossary p.14; repeatability, review and retention requirements in 14.6 p.111.
14. **Potential finding / exception [C]** — A detected criteria–condition difference requiring evaluation before deciding its significance/reporting. Standard 14.2 p.106. “Exception” here is a practical label, not a claim that GIAS defines a mandatory separate artifact or workflow state.
15. **Finding [G/C]** — An established gap between criteria and actual condition (Glossary p.11). Significance, reportability and priority are then evaluated under 14.3 pp.107–108. Not every raw deviation automatically warrants the same reporting treatment.
16. **Engagement conclusion [G]** — Professional judgment on findings collectively, expressed relative to engagement and management objectives; assurance conclusions address process effectiveness. Glossary p.11; 14.5 p.110.
17. **Recommendation [C]** — An auditor-proposed improvement or risk response, discussed with management. Standard 14.4 p.109. Recommendations are one available route, not mandatory auditor-authored text for every finding.
18. **Action plan [C]** — Management's intended response to findings, requested or agreed with audit; final communication identifies responsible people and planned dates. Standards 14.4 p.109 and 15.1 p.113. Distinct from the auditor's recommendation.
19. **Follow-up / implementation confirmation [C]** — Risk-based inquiry, assessment and status tracking to confirm management implemented recommendations/plans. Standard 15.2 pp.114–115. Report issuance alone does not establish implementation.
20. **Risk acceptance [C]** — Management retaining exposure, including potentially through delay/inaction; CAE escalates acceptance beyond appetite/tolerance when unresolved. Standards 11.5 p.84 and 15.2 p.115. It is not equivalent to auditor-confirmed remediation or a universal “closed” status.
21. **Methodologies [G]** — CAE-established policies, processes and procedures guiding the audit function. Glossary p.12; 9.3 pp.65–66. They implement Standards in context without making every local template universal.
22. **Objectivity [G]** — An unbiased individual mindset enabling balanced professional judgment. Glossary p.12; 2.1 pp.20–21. Distinguish from the function's organizational independence.
23. **Independence [G]** — Freedom from circumstances compromising the audit function's ability to perform impartially. Glossary p.12; organizational arrangements/safeguards in 7.1 pp.46–47.
24. **Professional skepticism [G]** — Questioning information and critically evaluating its reliability. Glossary p.13; 4.3 p.33. It entails further evidence where warranted, not automatic distrust of every source.
25. **Internal audit plan [G]** — The CAE's portfolio of anticipated engagements and other audit services for a period, responsive to risk and change. Glossary p.12; 9.4 pp.66–67. Distinct from an individual engagement's work program.

Interpretive safeguard: “potential finding → finding → action → confirmation” is a useful working distinction grounded in the cited clauses, not a compulsory linear state machine; Domain V p.92 explicitly allows overlapping and iterative work.
