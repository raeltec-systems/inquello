## Immediate interpretation risks / ledger candidates
1. Six stages and three architectural layers are useful organizing hypotheses, not demonstrated mandatory IIA taxonomies. R3 pp. 7–9 labels them 'confirmed' or 'confirmed explicitly'; cited requirements support individual obligations, not a compulsory software architecture. [INFERENCE; L; extractor inference from R3 pp. 7–9].
2. R3 p. 10 describes a stage-completion bundle as board-approved, including strategy and assurance map. R3 p. 14 instead says strategy is periodically reviewed with board/senior management and methodology is established by CAE. Preserve the specific approval objects, not a universal board gate. [INFERENCE; M; extractor inference].
3. Exact sample tables, automatic control sample-of-one, and 25→45/60 expansion examples are practice claims, not GIAS requirements. R3 pp. 26–31, 34. R3 p. 31 says Standards 'require' sampling documentation while tagging it [GUIDE]; quoted source uses 'should'. [INFERENCE; M; extractor inference].
4. R3 p. 5 says failed design short-circuits directly to a finding and no findings mean no actions/follow-up. The quoted Standards support professional judgment and positive assurance, not these absolute exclusivity claims. R3 pp. 26, 29–30 explicitly allow design evaluation during fieldwork. [INFERENCE; M; extractor inference].
5. R3 p. 32 labels direct board escalation for senior-management misconduct mandatory, but pp. 35, 38 correctly label it implementation guidance. Reporting violations under applicable laws/policies is distinct from the escalation-route example. [INFERENCE; M; extractor inference].
