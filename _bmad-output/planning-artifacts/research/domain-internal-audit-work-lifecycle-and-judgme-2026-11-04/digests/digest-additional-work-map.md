## Decision-relevant findings and cautions
1. **Six stages are a navigation model, not six mandated stages.** Import §2 H3 L103–114 maps Stage 1 to GIAS 9.4 outside Domain V, Stages 2–3 to Principle 13, Stages 4–5 to Principle 14, Stage 6 to Principle 15. Domain V's three Principles are not a three-cycle operating model. The import explicitly says “six stages” and “cross-cutting” are not Standards terms (L110). Any separate portfolio/function, engagement, and remediation/quality-cycle model is a further INFERENCE, not validated by calling these three Principles three cycles.
2. **Three layers are author synthesis.** §2 H4 L116–128 groups shared foundation, configurable methodology and engagement context. A four-layer variant that separates jurisdiction/sector overlays from engagement context can clarify authority, but this import does not establish a formal four-layer taxonomy. Local legal mandates cannot be demoted to optional methodology. A charter's *format* varies; its minimum content and approval are not optional.
3. **5Cs are not a mandatory five-field template.** L528 distinguishes 14.2 criteria/condition, 14.3 management collaboration on root cause *when possible*, effects and significance, and 14.4 the decision among recommendations/action plans/agreed actions. Template lists are in Considerations/evidence examples. Practitioner shorthand cannot replace the precise obligations.
4. **Sampling is not a universal 25/40/60 rule.** L366–367 and L980 distinguish guidance on documenting methodology/population/size/projectability from conflicting SOC/SOX/practitioner/vendor tables. “No mandatory sampling size” is an imported full-text-search conclusion requiring verification; no table should become a default compliance gate.
5. **RCM is a tool, not the required artifact.** L85, L99, stage tables and glossary distinguish optional matrix/narrative/chart from required risk assessment, adequate criteria, documented work program and evidence. RCM columns and acronym variants are methodology choices.
6. **Approval is responsibility plus authorized delegation, not an arbitrary role ladder.** Board approves plan/significant changes; CAE approves objectives/scope and changes, program before implementation and changes, documentation and final communication. §5.1/L667–670 and L984 reconcile supervisor practice with retained CAE accountability. Do not turn a vendor's five levels, review-note workflow or finalization lock into GIAS requirements.
7. **Iteration needs conditional approvals, communication and retained evidence.** §8 L852–881 maps thirty loops including plan revision, scope/program change, insufficient evidence, management challenge, corrections, post-completion impairment, overdue actions and risk escalation. Not all loops have the same class or trigger.
8. **Assurance/advisory is a scoped branch, not an exemption switch.** §6 L717–742 names specific carve-outs for formal risk assessment, criteria and evidence-based findings; work program, base final communication, documentation and supervision still apply according to the import. Follow-up of advisory actions is an explicit interpretive gap.
9. **Requirement status is not identical to section labelling.** The import sometimes labels descriptive introductions, glossary text and conditional “may” clauses REQ. Conversely L153 labels Domain III Essential Conditions GUIDE although L14/L35 classify Essential Conditions within Requirements. Check exact original layout and modal wording before converting tags into obligations.
10. **Avoid overclaims in the import.** L126 says formats must be established; 4.1 alone does not prove each named template is mandatory. L528's “mandatory core is four elements” must not erase root-cause feasibility or assurance/advisory conditions. L550/L556 must not imply every missing root cause automatically breaches GIAS. L805's claim that Zambia's audit committees *are* an adequate independence safeguard is an inference requiring a governance-specific assessment.
11. **LOCAL does not mean binding everywhere, or even binding locally.** Basel supervisory principles, the Chartered IIA code, institutional manuals and HHS-OIG's expressly voluntary GCPG have different authority. HIPAA obligations concern covered entities/security safeguards, not a universal internal-audit procedure. ISO/QMS auditing, external-audit PCAOB evidence rules, SOX/ICFR and second-line compliance monitoring must remain separate domains.
12. **Time-sensitive claims need current verification.** Import reports Cybersecurity effective 2026-02-05 and Third-Party 2026-09-15; Organizational Behavior 2026-12-15 and Resilience 2027-04-30 not yet effective on access day. It flags draft BIS IAC10, inconsistent future-topic status, and missing topic PDFs (L983,988,1002). Do not promote draft/issued/future-effective text to current binding status.
13. **Quality and evidence coverage are broad; prevalence evidence is weak.** Pre-2024 EQA findings illustrate failure modes, not present-day conformance rates under GIAS. Surveys differ by population, metric and year; vendor pages show possible workflows, not universal adoption or necessity. Source-age gates in the Domain pack fail many technology/adoption figures.
14. **Decision implications (INFERENCE only):** preserve authority and applicability separately from configurable templates; keep claim → criteria → procedures → evidence → evaluated finding → action → follow-up traceability; distinguish management attestation from auditor confirmation; preserve changed versions, accountable approver and timing; support disagreement/deviation/risk-acceptance records. These are research implications, not approved requirements or a design.
