# Practitioner-Accurate Domain Map of Internal Audit Work Under the 2024 Global Internal Audit Standards

## Executive Summary & Software Architecture Recommendations

This report provides a source-verified, practitioner-accurate map of internal audit methodology grounded in The Institute of Internal Auditors' (The IIA's) **2024 *Global Internal Audit Standards™*** (published January 9, 2024; mandatory effective January 9, 2025) [1], IIA **Topical Requirements** [2, 3, 4], IIA **Global Guidance** (Practice Guides, GTAGs, Three Lines Model, and the 2024 *Quality Assessment Manual*) [6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11], **external regulatory frameworks** across banking, public companies, and the public sector [16, 18, 20, 22, 24, 25, 28, 29], and **observed practice** across leading audit management platforms (Diligent One/HighBond, Wolters Kluwer TeamMate+, AuditBoard/Optro, Workiva) and Big 4/institutional methodologies [31, 32, 33, 34, 35, 36].

### Core Architectural Takeaways for Internal Audit Software Design

1. **Support Dual Engagement Execution Modes (Control-Reliance RCM Mode vs. Direct Substantive / Workplan Mode)**:
   - While Hypothesis H1's judgment chain (`business objective → risks → controls and criteria → testing → evidence → findings and conclusion → management action → follow-up`) is the backbone of internal control assurance [1], forcing every audit through a rigid Risk-and-Control Matrix (RCM) breaks down in **direct substantive audits**, **fraud/management-override investigations** [25], **design-only readiness assessments** [1], and **advisory engagements** [1]. Leading platforms such as Diligent HighBond explicitly provide two distinct project workflows: an *Internal Control / SOX Workflow* (RCM-driven, split into Test of Design [TOD] and Test of Operating Effectiveness [TOE]) and a *Workplan Workflow* (where procedures map directly to objectives without intermediate control entities) [31].
2. **Model Iteration as "Versioned Loops with Governance Approval Gates"**:
   - Hypothesis H2 is confirmed verbatim on page 92 of the 2024 Standards [1], which emphasizes that engagement steps are overlapping and iterative. However, the Standards simultaneously require **Chief Audit Executive (CAE) approval** whenever engagement objectives, scope, or work programs change mid-engagement (Standards 13.3, 13.6, 14.2) and **Board approval** for significant changes to the annual internal audit plan (Standard 9.4) [1]. Software must not implement naive status-unlocking; instead, it should implement **delta-approval workflows** (e.g., proposing a Scope/Program Amendment when a Stage 4 test exception uncovers a new risk, logging the CAE's digital sign-off while preserving prior baselines) [1, 32].
3. **Replace the 3-Layer Configurability Model (H4) with a 4-Layer Architecture**:
   - Between the universal **IIA Foundation Layer (`[REQ]`)** and the team-configurable **Methodology Layer (`[PRACTICE]`)** sits a binding **External Mandate & Topical Overlay Layer (`[LOCAL]` / `[TOPICAL]`)** [2, 3, 16, 18, 25, 28]. A bank's internal audit team cannot freely configure away MRA/MRIA issue validation testing [18, 20], a public manufacturer's SOX team cannot drop daily control samples below external-auditor PCAOB AS 2605 thresholds (25–40 items) [25, 34], and any team auditing cybersecurity must document conformance or formal exclusion rationale for the 17 mandatory items in the IIA Cybersecurity Topical Requirement [2, 3, 5].
4. **Prevent the #1 External Quality Assessment (EQA) Failure Modes by Design**:
   - Across IIA Quality Services and EQA benchmark studies [12] [13] [14], the most frequent non-conformances are: (a) **untimely supervisory sign-offs** (workpapers signed off by managers *after* the final audit report date) [14], (b) **missing linkage** between engagement risk assessments, evaluation criteria, and test procedures [13] [14], (c) **superficial root-cause analysis** that restates the condition rather than diagnosing underlying drivers [13] [15], and (d) **neglected QAIP ongoing monitoring** [12] [13]. Software can eliminate these structurally via hard pre-report gates (e.g., TeamMate+'s "No Open Coaching Notes" and timestamped pre-issuance review locks) [32], mandatory criteria fields on every RCM/work-program step [1], and embedded ongoing QAIP telemetry (Standard 12.1) [1] [11].

## Section 1: How the Standards Are Organised, and How Mandatory and Non-Mandatory Material Is Distinguished

### 1.1 Architecture of the International Professional Practices Framework (IPPF)
- `[REQ]` / `[GUIDE]` The **International Professional Practices Framework (IPPF)®** is the authoritative body of knowledge promulgated by The Institute of Internal Auditors (The IIA) and comprises **three components** — two mandatory and one supplemental (non-mandatory) (*Global Internal Audit Standards*, "About the International Professional Practices Framework", pp. 5–6) [1, 2]:
  1. **Global Internal Audit Standards™ (Mandatory)**: Published January 9, 2024, and effective January 9, 2025 (replacing the 2017 *International Standards for the Professional Practice of Internal Auditing*, its standalone Code of Ethics, and its separate Assurance `"A"` and Consulting `"C"` implementation standards) [1]. Organized into **5 Domains, 15 Guiding Principles, and 52 Standards** (*About the IPPF*, p. 5; *Fundamentals*, p. 8) [1].
  2. **Topical Requirements (Mandatory when applicable)**: Designed to enhance consistency and quality in specific risk subjects. The Standards state verbatim: `[REQ]` *"Internal auditors must conform with the relevant requirements when the scope of an engagement includes one of the identified topics."* (*About the IPPF*, p. 5) [1, 2]. Conformance is mandatory for assurance engagements touching the topic (whether planned, discovered mid-engagement, or requested ad hoc) and recommended for advisory services; any exclusion of a sub-requirement requires documented rationale in the workpapers [2, 3].
  3. **Global Guidance (Supplemental / Non-Mandatory)**: `[GUIDE]` *"Global Guidance supports the Standards by providing nonmandatory information, advice, and best practices for performing internal audit services. It is endorsed by The IIA through formal review and approval processes."* (*About the IPPF*, p. 6) [1]. Global Guidance comprises **Global Practice Guides** (detailed methodologies, templates, and walkthroughs) and **Global Technology Audit Guides (GTAG®)** (IT management, security, data analysis, and continuous auditing guidance) (*About the IPPF*, p. 6) [1, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10].

### 1.2 The Five Domains of the 2024 Global Internal Audit Standards
- `[GUIDE]` The 2024 Standards organize internal auditing into five domains, plus a dedicated section on *Applying the Global Internal Audit Standards in the Public Sector* (pp. 116–119) and an authoritative *Glossary* (pp. 10–14) (*Fundamentals*, pp. 7–8) [1]:
  - **Domain I: Purpose of Internal Auditing** (p. 15) — Statement of the value and purpose of internal auditing (assurance, advice, insight, and foresight).
  - **Domain II: Ethics and Professionalism** (pp. 16–36) — **Principles 1–5 (13 Standards)**: Replaces the former standalone IIA Code of Ethics; governs Integrity (Principle 1), Objectivity (Principle 2), Competency (Principle 3), Due Professional Care & Professional Skepticism (Principle 4), and Confidentiality (Principle 5).
  - **Domain III: Governing the Internal Audit Function** (pp. 37–59) — **Principles 6–8 (9 Standards)**: Outlines requirements for the Chief Audit Executive (CAE) and **Essential Conditions** for the Board and Senior Management across Mandate/Charter (Principle 6), Organizational Independence (Principle 7), and Board Oversight/Resources/Quality/EQA (Principle 8).
  - **Domain IV: Managing the Internal Audit Function** (pp. 60–91) — **Principles 9–12 (16 Standards)**: Governs CAE management of Strategic Planning, Audit Plan & Coordination/Reliance (Principle 9), Financial/Human/Tech Resources (Principle 10), Effective Communication, Conclusions, Errors & Risk Acceptance (Principle 11), and Internal Quality Assessment, Performance Measurement & Engagement Supervision (Principle 12).
  - **Domain V: Performing Internal Audit Services** (pp. 92–115) — **Principles 13–15 (14 Standards)**: Governs engagement-level execution across Planning Engagements Effectively (Principle 13: Standards 13.1–13.6), Conducting Engagement Work (Principle 14: Standards 14.1–14.6), and Communicating Engagement Results and Monitoring Action Plans (Principle 15: Standards 15.1–15.2).

### 1.3 How Mandatory and Non-Mandatory Material Interact Within Each Standard (`"must"`, `"should"`, `"may"`)
- `[REQ]` / `[GUIDE]` Within Domains II–V, every Principle is supported by Standards, and every Standard is divided into three structural subsections (*Fundamentals*, p. 8) [1]:
  1. **Requirements (`[REQ]`)**: Mandatory practices for internal auditing.
     - **Operative Word (`"must"`)**: `[REQ]` *"The Standards use the word 'must' in the Requirements sections and the words 'should' and 'may' to specify common and preferred practices in the Considerations for Implementation sections."* (*Fundamentals*, p. 8) [1]. The Glossary defines `"must"` verbatim as: `[REQ]` *"must – The Global Internal Audit Standards use the word 'must' to specify an unconditional requirement."* (*Glossary*, p. 12) [1].
  2. **Considerations for Implementation (`[GUIDE]`)**: Common and preferred practices to consider when implementing the Requirements; not mandatory.
     - **Operative Word (`"should"`)**: `[GUIDE]` *"should – As used in the Considerations for Implementation of the Global Internal Audit Standards, the word 'should' describes practices that are preferred but not required."* (*Glossary*, p. 13) [1].
     - **Operative Word (`"may"`)**: `[GUIDE]` *"may – As used in the Considerations for Implementation of the Global Internal Audit Standards, the word 'may' describes optional practices to implement the Requirements."* (*Glossary*, p. 12) [1].
  3. **Examples of Evidence of Conformance (`[GUIDE]`)**: Illustrative lists at the end of each Standard.
     - `[GUIDE]` *"Each standard ends with a list of examples of evidence. The examples are neither requirements nor the only ways to demonstrate conformance; rather, they are provided to help internal audit functions prepare for quality assessments, which rely on demonstrative evidence."* (*Fundamentals*, p. 8) [1].
- **Authoritative Status of the Glossary**:
  - `[REQ]` / `[GUIDE]` *"The Standards use certain terms as defined in the accompanying glossary. To understand and implement the Standards correctly, it is necessary to understand and adopt the specific meanings and usage of the terms as described in the glossary."* (*Fundamentals*, p. 8) [1].
- **Interaction Rule When a Requirement Cannot Be Met ("Conform with the Principle's Intent")**:
  - `[REQ]` While `"must"` denotes an unconditional requirement, the Standards define an explicit exception-and-disclosure mechanism when resource limitations or legal/sectoral rules prevent literal conformance with a specific requirement:
    - Under *Fundamentals* (p. 8): `[GUIDE]` *"While conformance with the requirements is expected, internal auditors occasionally may be unable to conform with a requirement yet still achieve the intent of the standard... In these exceptional circumstances, alternative actions should be implemented to meet the intent of the related standard. The chief audit executive is responsible for documenting and conveying the rationale for the deviation and the adopted alternative actions to the appropriate parties."* [1].
    - Under **Standard 4.1 (Conformance with the Global Internal Audit Standards, Domain II, Principle 4, p. 30)**:
      - `[REQ]` *"If laws or regulations prohibit internal auditors or the internal audit function from conforming with any part of the Standards, conformance with all other parts of the Standards is required and appropriate disclosures must be made."* [1].
      - `[REQ]` *"When internal auditors are unable to conform with a requirement, the chief audit executive must document and communicate a description of the circumstance, alternative actions taken, the impact of the actions, and the rationale."* [1].
    - Under **Domain III Introduction (Governing the Internal Audit Function, p. 38)** regarding Board/Senior Management **Essential Conditions**:
      - `[REQ]` *"The chief audit executive may reach agreement with the board and senior management that one or more of the essential conditions are not necessary to conform with the Standards. In such instances, the chief audit executive must document: The reasons for agreeing that a particular condition is unnecessary; Alternative conditions that compensate for the absent conditions, to support the judgments of the board and senior management."* [1].

### Detailed Evidence for Hypothesis Verdicts

#### H1 Detailed Analysis: The Judgment Chain & Its Documented Exceptions
- **Supporting Evidence (`[REQ]` & `[GUIDE]`)**:
  - For an assurance engagement, the 2024 Standards explicitly mandate the chain in H1:
    1. *Business Objective → Risks → Controls & Criteria*: `[REQ]` *"Internal auditors must develop an understanding of the activity under review to assess the relevant risks... Internal auditors must identify the criteria that management uses to measure whether the activity is achieving its objectives."* (Domain V, Principle 13, Standard 13.2, pp. 95–96) [1], and `[REQ]` *"Internal auditors must identify the most relevant criteria to be used to evaluate the aspects of the activity under review defined in the engagement objectives and scope."* (Standard 13.4, p. 100) [1].
    2. *Controls & Criteria → Testing (Work Program)*: `[REQ]` *"The engagement work program must identify: Criteria to be used to evaluate each objective; Tasks to achieve the engagement objectives; Methodologies, including the analytical procedures to be used, and tools to perform the tasks; Internal auditors assigned to perform each task."* (Standard 13.6, p. 103) [1].
    3. *Testing → Evidence → Defensible Judgment*: `[REQ]` *"Sufficient – when it enables internal auditors to perform analyses and complete evaluations and can enable a prudent, informed, and competent person to repeat the engagement work program and reach the same conclusions as the internal auditor."* (Domain V, Principle 14, Standard 14.1, p. 105) [1], reinforced by Standard 14.6 (p. 111): `[REQ]` *"The analyses, evaluations, and supporting information relevant to an engagement must be documented such that an informed, prudent internal auditor, or similarly informed and competent person, could repeat the work and derive the same engagement results."* [1].
    4. *Evidence → Findings & Conclusion → Management Action → Follow-up*: Standards 14.2 (condition vs. criteria), 14.3 (root causes, potential effects, significance), 14.4 (recommendations/action plans), 14.5 (engagement conclusions), 15.1 (final communication), and 15.2 (confirming implementation of action plans) (Domain V, Principles 14–15, pp. 106–115) [1].
- **Contradicting Evidence Requiring Refinement (`[REQ]`, `[GUIDE]`, `[LOCAL]`, `[PRACTICE]`)**:
  1. **Direct Substantive Testing & Fraud/Management-Override Investigations**: Under PCAOB AS 2401 (*Consideration of Fraud in a Financial Statement Audit*) and ACCA substantive audit methodology [25], executives can override otherwise well-designed internal controls; consequently, auditors bypass the "controls" node of H1 and test underlying transactions, journal entries, and account balances directly against assertions/criteria [25, 31]. Similarly, Diligent HighBond's *Workplan Workflow* is specifically architected for operational, compliance, or substantive audits where procedures link directly to objectives without creating a Risk and Control Matrix (RCM) [31].
  2. **Design-Only Stopping Point**: The Considerations for Implementation for **Standard 4.2 (Due Professional Care, Domain II, Principle 4, p. 32)** explicitly state: `[GUIDE]` *"To evaluate a control adequately, an internal auditor assesses the design of the control workflow before evaluating the effectiveness of its operation. There is little benefit in investing extensive cascade testing in an inadequately designed control."* [1]. In such cases, the chain jumps directly from *Control Design Assessment → Finding & Recommendation* without operating effectiveness testing.
  3. **Satisfactory / Zero-Finding Path**: Under **Standard 14.2 (Domain V, Principle 14, p. 106)**: `[REQ]` *"If internal auditors determine that no additional analyses are required and there is no difference between the criteria and the condition, the internal auditors must provide assurance in the engagement conclusion regarding the effectiveness of the activity’s governance, risk management, and control processes."* [1]. Here, the chain bypasses Findings, Management Action Plans, and Follow-Up.
  4. **Advisory Engagements**: Standards 13.2 (p. 95), 13.4 (p. 100), and 14.2 (p. 106) explicitly exempt advisory engagements from mandatory formal risk assessments, evaluation criteria, and evidence-based findings depending on stakeholder agreement [1].

In practice, the order in which steps are performed may vary by engagement and have overlapping and iterative aspects. For example, engagement planning includes gathering information and assessing risks, which may continue throughout the engagement. Each step may affect another or the engagement as a whole. Therefore, internal auditors should review and understand all standards in this domain before beginning an engagement."* (*Global Internal Audit Standards*, Domain V Introduction, p. 92) [1].

  2. **Standard 9.4 Internal Audit Plan (Domain IV, Principle 9, p. 66)**: `[REQ]` *"The internal audit plan must: ... Be dynamic and updated timely in response to changes in the organization’s business, risks, operations, programs, systems, controls, and organizational culture."* [1].
  3. **Standard 13.1 Engagement Communication (Domain V, Principle 13, p. 93)**: `[REQ]` *"Internal auditors must communicate the objectives, scope, and timing of the engagement with management. Subsequent changes must be communicated with management timely."* [1].

  9. **Standard 14.2 Analyses and Potential Engagement Findings (Domain V, Principle 14, p. 106)**: `[REQ]` *"If initial analyses do not provide sufficient evidence to support a potential engagement finding, internal auditors must exercise due professional care to determine whether additional analyses are required. The chief audit executive is responsible for determining whether senior management, by delay or inaction, has accepted a risk that exceeds the risk tolerance."* (Looping to Standard 11.5 Risk Acceptance escalation or back to Stage 1/Stage 4 follow-up re-testing) [1].

## Section 3: Stage-by-Stage Findings (Part A)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Terminology Note on "Sufficient, Reliable, Relevant, and Useful" (2017 IPPF vs. 2024 Standards)**:
> Under the superseded 2017 IPPF (Standard 2310), internal auditors were required to identify *"sufficient, reliable, relevant, and useful"* information. In the **2024 Global Internal Audit Standards (Domain V, Principle 14, Standard 14.1, pp. 104–105)**, the mandatory requirement is now framed as **"relevant, reliable, and sufficient"** (`[REQ]`), because the concept of *"useful"* ("helps the organization meet its goals") has been integrated directly into the 2024 definition of **Relevant** (*"consistent with engagement objectives, within the scope of the engagement, and contributes to the development of engagement results"*, p. 104) [1, 10]. Both formulations are addressed in subsection 7 (*Evidence and documentation*) of each stage below.

---

### Stage 1: Deciding What to Audit (The Risk-Based Internal Audit Plan, Before Any Engagement)

#### 1. Purpose and outcome
- `[REQ]` **Purpose**: Establish the internal audit function's mandate, charter, strategy, and a dynamic, risk-based internal audit plan aligned with the organization's strategies, objectives, and risks that allocates human, financial, and technological resources to the highest-value assurance and advisory engagements (Domain III, Principles 6–8, Standards 6.1–8.2, pp. 39–55; Domain IV, Principle 9, Standards 9.1–9.5, pp. 61–71; Principle 10, Standards 10.1–10.3, pp. 72–76) [1].
- `[REQ]` **How you know it is done**:
  1. The **Internal Audit Charter** documenting the mandate is approved by the Board (Standard 6.1, p. 40; Standard 6.2, p. 42) [1].
  2. An organization-wide **risk assessment** has been documented (performed at least annually) informed by Board and senior management input and the CAE's understanding of governance, risk management, and control processes (Standard 9.4, p. 66) [1].
  3. The **Internal Audit Plan** and **Internal Audit Budget** have been discussed with senior management and formally **approved by the Board** (Standard 9.4, p. 67; Standard 10.1, p. 72; Standard 6.3 Essential Conditions, p. 44) [1].
  4. Any resource shortfalls, excluded high-risk areas, scope limitations, and assurance-provider coordination/reliance bases are documented and communicated to the Board (Standard 8.2, p. 54; Standard 9.4, p. 67; Standard 9.5, p. 69) [1].

#### 2. Understand
- `[REQ]` **Organization's Governance, Risk Management, and Control Processes (Standard 9.1, Domain IV, Principle 9, p. 61)**:
  - `[REQ]` *"To develop an effective internal audit strategy and plan, the chief audit executive must understand the organization’s governance, risk management, and control processes."* [1].
  - `[REQ]` *"To understand governance processes, the chief audit executive must consider how the organization: Establishes strategic objectives and makes strategic and operational decisions; Oversees risk management and control; Promotes an ethical culture; Delivers effective performance management and accountability; Structures its management and operating functions; Communicates risk and control information throughout the organization; Coordinates activities and communications among the board, internal and external providers of assurance services, and management."* (Standard 9.1, p. 61) [1].
  - `[REQ]` *"To understand risk management and control processes, the chief audit executive must consider how the organization identifies and assesses significant risks and selects appropriate control processes. This includes understanding how the organization identifies and manages the following key risk areas: Reliability and integrity of financial and operational information; Effectiveness and efficiency of operations and programs; Safeguarding of assets; Compliance with laws and/or regulations."* (Standard 9.1, p. 61) [1].
- `[REQ]` **Stakeholder Expectations, Strategy, and Other Assurance Providers**:
  - Learned from the **Board (Audit Committee)**, **Senior Management (CEO, CFO, CRO, General Counsel, CISO, Business Unit Heads)**, **Enterprise Risk Management (ERM)** outputs, **Second-Line compliance/risk functions**, **External Auditors**, and **Regulators** (Standard 9.1, pp. 61–62; Standard 9.4, pp. 66–67; Standard 9.5, p. 69) [1, 6].

#### 3. Decide
- `[REQ]` **Key Judgments, Criteria, and Decision Authority**:
  - **Mandate, Charter, Plan, and Budget Approval**: **Authority = The Board** (with senior management input and CAE proposal). `[REQ]` *"The plan and significant changes to the plan must be approved by the board."* (Standard 9.4, p. 67) [1]; `[REQ]` *"The chief audit executive must seek budget approval from the board."* (Standard 10.1, p. 72) [1].
  - **Risk Prioritization & Selection of Proposed Engagements**: **Authority = CAE** (subject to Board approval). Criteria include risk likelihood and impact, strategic alignment, IT governance, fraud risk, ethics/compliance program effectiveness, time since last audit, and regulatory mandates (Standard 9.4, pp. 66–68) [1, 6].
  - **Reliance on Other Assurance Providers (2nd Line / External Audit)**: **Authority = CAE**. `[REQ]` *"When the internal audit function relies on the work of other assurance service providers, the chief audit executive must document the basis for that reliance and is still responsible for the conclusions reached by the internal audit function."* (Standard 9.5, p. 69) [1]. Criteria for reliance (p. 70): `[GUIDE]` competency, objectivity/independence, due professional care, alignment of scope/objectives, and testing/review of their workpapers [1, 6].
  - **Whether ERM Risk Information Can Be Relied Upon**: **Authority = CAE**. `[GUIDE]` *"The internal audit function should only rely on management’s information about risks if it has concluded that the organization’s risk management processes are effective."* (Standard 9.4 Considerations, p. 67) [1].

#### 4. Do (Concrete Activities in Realistic Order, Including Stage-Specific Depth)
1. **Review and Refresh Mandate & Charter (`[REQ]` Standards 6.1–6.2, pp. 39–43; Standard 7.1, p. 46)**:
   - Verify that statutory/regulatory requirements are in the charter, confirm organizational independence to the Board at least annually (`[REQ]` Standard 7.1, p. 46), and document safeguards for any non-audit responsibilities held by the CAE [1].
2. **Build and Refresh the Audit Universe (`[GUIDE]` Standard 9.4, p. 67; IIA Practice Guide *Developing a Risk-Based Internal Audit Plan* [6]; Software Workflows [31, 32])**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — How the Audit Universe Is Built and Refreshed**:
     - `[GUIDE]` Under Standard 9.4 Considerations (p. 67): *"One approach to preparing the internal audit plan is to organize potentially auditable units within the organization into an audit universe to facilitate the identification and assessment of risks."* [1].
     - `[GUIDE]` / `[PRACTICE]` In the IIA Practice Guide [6] and software platforms (Diligent HighBond, TeamMate+) [31, 32], the audit universe is structured as a multi-dimensional inventory of **auditable entities** (business units, legal entities, geographic locations, end-to-end business processes, IT systems/applications, key projects/programs, third-party relationships, and regulatory mandates) synthesized from organizational charts, general ledger structures, IT asset inventories, and strategic plans [6, 31, 32].
     - `[PRACTICE]` While the baseline risk assessment must occur **at least annually** (`[REQ]` Standard 9.4, p. 66) [1], high-performing and regulated teams refresh the universe **continuously or quarterly** as M&A, reorganizations, new product launches, or emerging risks occur [6, 18, 20].
3. **Conduct the Organization-Wide Risk Assessment & Integrate Strategy / ERM (`[REQ]` Standard 9.4, pp. 66–67)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Use of the Organization's Strategy and Enterprise Risk Management (ERM)**:
     - `[REQ]` *"The chief audit executive must base the internal audit plan on a documented assessment of the organization’s strategies, objectives, and risks. This assessment must be informed by input from the board and senior management as well as the chief audit executive’s understanding of the organization’s governance, risk management, and control processes. The assessment must be performed at least annually."* (Standard 9.4, p. 66) [1].
     - `[GUIDE]` Under Standard 9.4 Considerations (p. 67), internal audit links each auditable unit to strategic objectives and evaluates inherent and residual risks. Critically, internal audit **cannot blindly copy management's ERM register**: *"To strive to ensure that the audit universe and risk assessment cover the organization’s key risks, the internal audit function should independently review and validate the key risks that were identified within the organization’s risk management system. The internal audit function should only rely on management’s information about risks if it has concluded that the organization’s risk management processes are effective."* [1, 6].
     - `[PRACTICE]` In Diligent HighBond and TeamMate+, auditors score each auditable entity across weighted risk factors (e.g., financial materiality, operational complexity, regulatory exposure, IT/cyber sensitivity, fraud susceptibility, change velocity, and time since last audit) to generate composite risk scores (High/Medium/Low) and risk heat maps [6, 31, 32].
4. **Coordinate and Evaluate Reliance Across the Three Lines (`[REQ]` Standard 6.1, p. 39; Standard 9.5, pp. 69–71; IIA Three Lines Model [6])**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Coordination and Reliance on Other Assurance Providers (The Three Lines Model)**:
     - `[REQ]` *"The chief audit executive must coordinate with internal and external providers of assurance services and consider relying upon their work. If unable to achieve an appropriate level of coordination, the chief audit executive must raise any concerns with senior management and, if necessary, the board. When the internal audit function relies on the work of other assurance service providers, the chief audit executive must document the basis for that reliance and is still responsible for the conclusions reached by the internal audit function."* (Standard 9.5, p. 69) [1].
     - `[GUIDE]` Under The IIA's *Three Lines Model* (2020) [6] and Standard 9.5 Considerations (pp. 69–70) [1], providers include **Internal Providers** (1st-line management monitoring; 2nd-line risk management, compliance, quality assurance, health & safety, IT security) and **External Providers** (external financial statement auditors and third-party specialist assessors hired by management or the board).
     - `[GUIDE]` Teams build a **Combined Assurance Map** (Standard 9.5 Examples of Evidence, p. 71) mapping key organizational risks against 1st-, 2nd-, and 3rd-line coverage to eliminate duplicate testing and uncover assurance blind spots [1, 6].
5. **Draft the Internal Audit Plan, Resource Plan, and Budget (`[REQ]` Standard 9.4, pp. 66–67; Standards 10.1–10.3, pp. 72–76)**:
   - `[REQ]` Mandatory coverage considerations in the plan: *"The internal audit plan must: Consider the internal audit mandate and the full range of agreed-to internal audit services; Specify internal audit services that support the evaluation and improvement of the organization’s governance, risk management, and control processes; Consider coverage of information technology governance, fraud risk, the effectiveness of the organization’s compliance and ethics programs, and other high-risk areas; Identify the necessary human, financial, and technological resources necessary to complete the plan; Be dynamic and updated timely in response to changes in the organization’s business, risks, operations, programs, systems, controls, and organizational culture."* (Standard 9.4, p. 66) [1].
   - `[GUIDE]` The plan may be structured as an annual plan, a rolling 12-to-36-month plan, or a dynamic backlog of prioritized candidate engagements with contingency capacity reserved for ad-hoc advisory or investigative requests (Standard 9.4 Considerations, p. 68) [1, 30].
6. **Communicate Resource Shortfalls, Omitted High-Risk Areas, and Present to Board for Approval (`[REQ]` Standard 9.4, p. 67; Standard 8.2, p. 54)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Board Approval and Changes to the Plan**:
     - `[REQ]` *"The chief audit executive must review and revise the internal audit plan as necessary and communicate timely to the board and senior management: The impact of any resource limitations on internal audit coverage; The rationale for not including an assurance engagement in a high-risk area or activity in the plan; Conflicting demands for services between major stakeholders, such as high-priority requests based on emerging risks and requests to replace planned assurance engagements with advisory engagements; Limitations on scope or restrictions on access to information."* (Standard 9.4, p. 67) [1].
     - `[REQ]` *"The chief audit executive must discuss the internal audit plan, including significant interim changes, with the board and senior management. The plan and significant changes to the plan must be approved by the board."* (Standard 9.4, p. 67) [1].
   - **Loops Triggered Involving Stage 1**:
     - **Loop L1 (Stage 4/5/6 → Stage 1)**: Emerging risks, major control failures discovered during Stage 4 fieldwork, unaddressed overdue actions in Stage 6 (Standard 15.2), or management requests to swap an assurance audit for an advisory project trigger a mid-year review and revision of the Internal Audit Plan, requiring Board approval if the change is significant (`[REQ]` Standard 9.4, p. 67) [1].

#### 5. Communicate
- `[REQ]` **With the Board and Senior Management**:
  - **What**: Internal Audit Charter/Mandate (Standard 6.1–6.2), Annual Organizational Independence Confirmation (Standard 7.1), Internal Audit Strategy (Standard 9.2), Proposed Risk-Based Internal Audit Plan & Budget (Standards 9.4, 10.1), Resource Sufficiency & Shortfalls (Standards 8.2, 9.4), Rationale for Excluded High-Risk Areas (Standard 9.4), and Interim Plan Revisions (Standards 8.1, 9.4) [1].
  - **When & Form**: At least annually for independence and risk assessment; periodically (typically quarterly Audit Committee meetings) for plan updates and interim changes, documented via formal Board/Audit Committee packs and recorded in Board minutes (Standards 7.1, 8.1, 9.4) [1].
- `[REQ]` **With Other Assurance Providers (2nd Line & External Auditors)**:
  - **What**: Roles, responsibilities, planned coverage, shared risk assessments, and reliance agreements (Standard 6.1, p. 39; Standard 9.5, p. 69) [1].

- `[REQ]` **What Must Be Documented**:
  1. The Internal Audit Charter referencing the mandate (Standard 6.1, p. 40; Standard 6.2, p. 42) and any non-audit role safeguards (Standard 7.1, p. 46) [1].
  2. The annual organization-wide risk assessment supporting the plan (Standard 9.4, p. 66) [1].

#### 8. What Varies (`[REQ]` vs. Methodology vs. Context)
- **Foundation-Fixed (`[REQ]`)**: Having a Board-approved charter and risk-based plan; performing a documented risk assessment at least annually; considering IT governance, fraud, and compliance/ethics risks; communicating excluded high-risk areas and resource constraints to the Board; obtaining Board approval for the plan and significant changes; documenting the basis for reliance on other assurance providers [1].
- **Methodology Choices (`[PRACTICE]` / `[GUIDE]`)**:
  - *Planning Horizon*: Static 12-month calendar plan vs. rolling 6/12-month plan vs. Agile quarterly backlog [1, 30].
  - *Risk Scoring Formula*: 3x3 vs. 5x5 Likelihood-x-Impact matrix vs. multi-factor weighted scoring in Diligent HighBond or TeamMate+ (e.g., 25% Financial, 25% Operational, 20% Regulatory, 15% IT/Cyber, 15% Change/Velocity) [6, 31, 32].
  - *Universe Taxonomy*: Process-centric (Procure-to-Pay, Order-to-Cash) vs. Entity/Location-centric (Plants, Branches, Subsidiaries) vs. Risk-Pillar-centric (Credit, Liquidity, Market, Operational) [6, 18, 27, 31].
- **Engagement/Organizational Context**: Specific strategic goals, regulatory cycle mandates (e.g., 3-to-4-year mandatory full-universe cycle in banking under BCBS 223 / OCC rules [16, 18]), ERM maturity, and headcount/co-sourcing budget.

#### 9. Common Failure Modes (EQA / QAIP Deficiencies and Reasons)
1. **`[PRACTICE]` Outdated Internal Audit Charter Missing Mandatory 2024 GIAS Elements**:
   - *Deficiency*: Charter still references the 2017 IPPF or omits the Board's Essential Conditions, mandate scope, or non-audit role safeguards [12, 13].
   - *Reason*: Treating the charter as a one-time boilerplate document rather than reviewing it annually with the Board under Standards 6.1–6.2 [12, 13].
2. **`[PRACTICE]` Static, Rotational ("Last-Year-Plus-One") Audit Plans Disconnected from Strategy**:
   - *Deficiency*: EQA assessors find no documented link between the organization's strategic objectives / emerging risks and the engagements on the plan, or find that the team copied management's ERM register without evaluating ERM effectiveness [12, 13].
   - *Reason*: Over-reliance on historical rotational cycles or informal management requests without a documented, repeatable risk-scoring methodology [12, 13].
3. **`[PRACTICE]` Unmitigated Independence Impairments (CAE Owning 2nd-Line Functions Without Safeguards)**:
   - *Deficiency*: The CAE manages ERM, compliance, or SOX 1st-line testing, and internal audit audits those areas internally rather than using an independent external party reporting to the Board as required by Standard 7.1 (p. 46) [1, 12, 13].
   - *Reason*: Cost constraints in smaller organizations combined with failure to document compensating third-party oversight in the charter [12, 13].
4. **`[PRACTICE]` Failing to Communicate Unaudited High-Risk Areas and Resource Limitations to the Board**:
   - *Deficiency*: The CAE presents a plan fitted solely to existing headcount without disclosing which high-risk areas were left off the plan due to resource constraints (violating Standard 9.4, p. 67 and Standard 8.2, p. 54) [1, 12, 13].
   - *Reason*: Cultural reluctance by CAEs to report resource shortfalls or tell management/the Board what cannot be covered [12, 13].

### Stage 2: Understanding and Planning an Engagement

#### 1. Purpose and outcome
- `[REQ]` **Purpose**: Understand the specific activity under review (its objectives, processes, governance, risks, fraud exposures, and controls), evaluate the adequacy of evaluation criteria, establish the engagement's objectives and scope boundaries, and communicate the planned engagement to management (Domain V, Principle 13, Standards 13.1–13.4, pp. 93–101) [1].
- `[REQ]` **How you know it is done**:
  1. An **Engagement Risk Assessment** (and preliminary Risk and Control Matrix / process understanding) is documented (or prior assessment updated) (Standard 13.2, pp. 95–97) [1].
  2. Applicable **IIA Topical Requirements** have been assessed for applicability and documented [2, 3].
  3. **Evaluation Criteria** have been identified and assessed for adequacy (Standard 13.4, pp. 100–101) [1].
  4. **Engagement Objectives and Scope** (including any scope limitations) are documented and **approved by the CAE** (Standard 13.3, pp. 98–99) [1].
  5. Objectives, scope, and timing have been **communicated to management** (Standard 13.1, p. 93) [1].

#### 2. Understand
- `[REQ]` **Mandatory Information to Gather and Review (Standard 13.2, Domain V, Principle 13, pp. 95–96)**:
  - `[REQ]` *"Internal auditors must develop an understanding of the activity under review to assess the relevant risks. For advisory services, a formal, documented risk assessment may not be necessary, depending on the agreement with relevant stakeholders."* [1].
  - `[REQ]` *"To develop an adequate understanding, internal auditors must identify and gather reliable, relevant, and sufficient information regarding: The organization’s strategies, objectives, and risks relevant to the activity under review; The organization’s risk tolerance, if established; The risk assessment supporting the internal audit plan; The governance, risk management, and control processes of the activity under review; Applicable frameworks, guidance, and other criteria that can be used to evaluate the effectiveness of those processes."* (Standard 13.2, p. 95) [1].
  - `[REQ]` *"Internal auditors must review the gathered information to understand how processes are intended to operate."* (Standard 13.2, p. 96) [1].
- `[GUIDE]` **From Whom and Where (Standard 13.2 Considerations, p. 96; Practice Guide *Engagement Planning* [7])**:
  - **Sources**: Process owners, operational management, 2nd-line compliance/risk staff, Policies & Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), process flowcharts, system configuration documentation, prior audit reports and workpapers, open issue logs, regulatory examination reports, and preliminary data extracts [1, 7].

#### 3. Decide
- `[REQ]` **Key Judgments, Criteria, and Decision Authority**:
  - **Which Risks to Prioritize for Review (Engagement Risk Assessment)**: **Authority = Internal Auditors & Engagement Supervisor** (approved via scope/work program by CAE). `[REQ]` *"Internal auditors must identify the risks to review by: Identifying the potentially significant risks to the objectives of the activity under review; Considering specific risks related to fraud; Evaluating the significance of the risks and prioritizing them for review."* (Standard 13.2, p. 96) [1]. Significance is judged by **impact and likelihood** relative to established risk tolerance (Standard 13.2 Considerations, p. 97) [1].
  - **Adequacy of Evaluation Criteria**: **Authority = Internal Auditors / CAE** (in consultation with Board/Senior Management if inadequate). `[REQ]` *"Internal auditors must assess the extent to which the board and senior management have established adequate criteria to determine whether the activity under review has accomplished its objectives and goals. If such criteria are adequate, internal auditors must use them for the evaluation. If the criteria are inadequate, internal auditors must identify appropriate criteria through discussion with the board and/or senior management."* (Standard 13.4, p. 100) [1].
  - **Engagement Objectives and Scope (and Mid-Audit Changes)**: **Authority = CAE**. `[REQ]` *"The chief audit executive must approve the engagement objectives and scope and any changes that occur during the engagement."* (Standard 13.3, p. 98) [1].
  - **Resolution or Escalation of Scope Limitations**: **Authority = CAE → Board**. `[REQ]` *"Scope limitations must be discussed with management when identified, with a goal of achieving resolution... If a resolution cannot be achieved with management, the chief audit executive must elevate the scope limitation issue to the board according to an established methodology."* (Standard 13.3, p. 98) [1].

#### 4. Do (Concrete Activities in Realistic Order, Including Stage-Specific Depth)
1. **Confirm Team Objectivity & Check Topical Requirement Applicability (`[REQ]` Standard 2.2, p. 22; `[TOPICAL]` [2, 3])**:
   - Confirm no assigned auditor had operational responsibility for the activity within the previous 12 months (`[REQ]` Standard 2.2, p. 22) [1]. Screen whether the engagement touches a mandatory IIA Topical Requirement (e.g., Cybersecurity, Third-Party, Organizational Behavior) and document applicability [2, 3, 4].
2. **Issue Announcement / Kickoff Notice & Initial Document Request (`[REQ]` Standard 13.1, p. 93; `[PRACTICE]` [7, 31, 32])**:
   - Send an engagement announcement / notification memo to activity management and hold an opening/entrance meeting to discuss preliminary objectives, timing, and logistics (Standard 13.1 Considerations, p. 94) [1, 7].
3. **Understand the Activity: Process Walkthroughs, Interviews, and Preliminary Data (`[REQ]` Standard 13.2, pp. 95–97; Standard 4.2, p. 32)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Process Walkthroughs, Interviews, and Data**:
     - `[GUIDE]` Standard 13.2 Considerations (p. 96) explicitly prescribes: *"Methods for gathering information include deploying surveys, conducting interviews, observing activities, and reviewing prior documentation. Additionally, performing a walkthrough is a valuable method to gain an understanding of how processes operate and controls are designed."* [1].
     - `[GUIDE]` **What a Walkthrough Does**: During a walkthrough, the auditor traces a single transaction or event end-to-end ("cradle-to-grave") through the process from initiation to recording/reporting, interviewing the personnel who perform the steps, inspecting actual system screens and artifacts, and verifying two things: (a) whether the process operates as described in policies/narratives, and (b) **Control Design Adequacy (Test of Design / TOD)** — whether the controls as designed can prevent or detect material risks (`[GUIDE]` Standard 4.2 Considerations, p. 32; Standard 13.2 Considerations, p. 96) [1, 7, 31].
     - `[GUIDE]` / `[PRACTICE]` **Preliminary Data Analytics in Planning**: Auditors run high-level population profiling (transaction volumes, stratifications by location/product, trend anomalies) during Stage 2 to identify high-risk segments and bound the engagement scope [7, 8].
4. **Perform the Engagement-Level Risk and Control Assessment (`[REQ]` Standard 13.2, pp. 95–97)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — The Engagement-Level Risk and Control Assessment**:
     - `[REQ]` Auditors identify the activity's objectives, brainstorm and catalog potentially significant risks to those objectives, **explicitly consider fraud risks** (e.g., asset misappropriation, fraudulent reporting, corruption, management override), and evaluate risk significance (likelihood × impact) to prioritize which risks stay in scope (Standard 13.2, p. 96) [1].
     - `[REQ]` **Past Engagement Shortcut**: *"When internal auditors have identified the relevant risks for an activity under review in past engagements, only a review and update of the previous engagement risk assessment is required."* (Standard 13.2, p. 96) [1].
     - `[GUIDE]` / `[PRACTICE]` Auditors map each prioritized risk to key preventive and detective controls in a **Risk and Control Matrix (RCM)** or risk-and-control heat map (Standard 13.2 Examples of Evidence, p. 97; AuditBoard/HighBond/TeamMate+ RCM modules) [1, 7, 31, 32, 33].
5. **Identify and Assess Evaluation Criteria (`[REQ]` Standard 13.4, pp. 100–101)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — What "Criteria" Means, and What Happens When Criteria Are Absent**:
     - `[REQ]` **Definition of Criteria**: The 2024 Standards Glossary (p. 11) defines **criteria** verbatim as: *"In an engagement, specifications of the desired state of the activity under review (also called 'evaluation criteria')."* [1]. Standard 13.2 (p. 96) and Standard 13.4 (p. 100) require identifying both the criteria management uses to measure whether the activity achieves its objectives (e.g., KPIs, targets, tolerances) and the evaluation criteria internal audit will use to judge governance, risk management, and control effectiveness [1].
     - `[GUIDE]` **Examples of Adequate Criteria** (Standard 13.4 Considerations, p. 100): Internal policies, procedures, and KPI targets; contractual obligations; laws and regulations; and authoritative external frameworks (e.g., COSO *Internal Control – Integrated Framework*, COSO ERM, NIST CSF 2.0, COBIT, ISO 9001/27001, ITIL) [1, 7].
     - `[REQ]` / `[GUIDE]` **What Happens When Criteria Are Absent or Inadequate**:
       1. `[REQ]` Under **Standard 13.4 (p. 100)**: *"If the criteria are inadequate, internal auditors must identify appropriate criteria through discussion with the board and/or senior management."* [1].
       2. `[GUIDE]` Under **Standard 13.4 Considerations (p. 100)**: When management has not established criteria (or existing policies are outdated, vague, or below industry standards), internal auditors propose benchmark criteria from laws/regulations, recognized industry frameworks (COSO, NIST, ISO), or peer best practices and obtain management agreement on using those criteria for the audit.
       3. `[GUIDE]` **Critically, the absence of criteria is itself a potential finding**: Standard 13.4 Considerations (p. 100) states that the absence of established management criteria (such as missing policies, undefined risk tolerances, or lack of performance metrics) represents a **control design deficiency** in governance or risk management that should be evaluated as a finding under Standard 14.2/14.3 [1, 7].
6. **Establish and Approve Engagement Objectives, Scope, and Limitations (`[REQ]` Standard 13.3, pp. 98–99)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Agreeing Objectives, Scope, and Limitations**:
     - `[REQ]` *"Internal auditors must establish and document the objectives and scope for each engagement. The engagement objectives must articulate the purpose of the engagement and describe the specific goals to be achieved, including those mandated by laws and/or regulations. The scope must establish the engagement’s focus and boundaries by specifying the activities, locations, processes, systems, components, time period to be covered in the engagement, and other elements to be reviewed, and be sufficient to achieve the engagement objectives."* (Standard 13.3, p. 98) [1].
     - `[REQ]` *"Internal auditors must consider whether the engagement is intended to provide assurance or advisory services because stakeholder expectations and the requirements of the Standards differ depending on the type of engagement."* (Standard 13.3, p. 98) [1].
     - `[REQ]` **Handling Scope Limitations**: *"Scope limitations must be discussed with management when identified, with a goal of achieving resolution. Scope limitations are assurance engagement conditions, such as resource constraints or restrictions on access to personnel, facilities, data, and information, that prevent internal auditors from performing the work as expected in the audit work program. If a resolution cannot be achieved with management, the chief audit executive must elevate the scope limitation issue to the board according to an established methodology."* (Standard 13.3, p. 98) [1]. If unresolved, the scope limitation must also be disclosed in the final engagement communication (`[REQ]` Standard 15.1, p. 113) [1].
     - `[REQ]` **CAE Approval**: *"The chief audit executive must approve the engagement objectives and scope and any changes that occur during the engagement."* (Standard 13.3, p. 98) [1].
7. **Communicate Engagement Objectives, Scope, and Timing to Management (`[REQ]` Standard 13.1, pp. 93–94)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Engagement Communication with Management**:
     - `[REQ]` *"Internal auditors must communicate effectively throughout the engagement. Internal auditors must communicate the objectives, scope, and timing of the engagement with management. Subsequent changes must be communicated with management timely."* (Standard 13.1, p. 93) [1].
     - `[GUIDE]` Notice the precision of the Standards' language for **assurance** engagements: internal auditors **communicate and discuss** objectives and scope with management, and **the CAE approves** them (Standard 13.3, p. 98)—management does **not** hold veto power over assurance scope! By contrast, for **advisory** engagements, objectives and scope are mutually agreed upon with the client stakeholder (Standard 13.3 Considerations, p. 99) [1].
   - **Loops Triggered in Stage 2**:
     - **Loop L2 (Stage 2 Walkthrough → Stage 5 Immediate Design Finding / Stage 2 Scope Change)**: If walkthroughs reveal that controls are completely nonexistent or fundamentally broken in design, auditors may halt planned operating effectiveness testing (`[GUIDE]` Standard 4.2, p. 32), document a design finding immediately, or convert the engagement (with CAE approval and management discussion) into a readiness/advisory engagement [1].

#### 5. Communicate
- `[REQ]` **With Activity Management**:
  - **What**: Engagement objectives, scope (processes, locations, systems, audit period), timing, key contacts, and any subsequent changes to objectives/scope/timing (Standard 13.1, p. 93); discussions to agree on appropriate evaluation criteria when management's criteria are absent or inadequate (Standard 13.4, p. 100); discussions to resolve any scope limitations (Standard 13.3, p. 98) [1].
  - **When & Form**: At engagement initiation (Announcement Memo & Opening/Entrance Meeting), at the end of planning (Engagement Planning Memo / Terms of Reference), and promptly whenever scope or timing changes [1, 7].
- `[REQ]` **With the CAE (and Board if escalated)**:
  - **What**: Submission of engagement objectives and scope for CAE approval (Standard 13.3, p. 98); escalation of unresolved scope limitations from CAE to the Board (Standard 13.3, p. 98) [1].

| **Approved Audit Plan Entry & Prior Audit File** | Input | High-level audit rationale, preliminary scope, budgeted hours, prior engagement risk assessment, prior findings and open action plans. | Approved in Stage 1 by **Board / CAE**; reviewed by **Engagement Supervisor**. | `[REQ]` [1] (Standards 9.4, 13.2, pp. 66, 95–96) |
| **Process Flowcharts / Narratives & Walkthrough Workpapers** | Output | Step-by-step process description, systems used, handoffs, control points, trace of 1 transaction end-to-end, assessment of Control Design Adequacy (TOD). | Prepared by **Internal Auditor**; reviewed by **Engagement Supervisor (Audit Manager)**. | `[REQ]` (process understanding) / `[GUIDE]` (walkthrough/flowchart) [1] (Standard 13.2, pp. 96–97), [7], [31] |
| **Engagement-Level Risk Assessment & Preliminary RCM** | Output | Activity objectives, identified risks (including explicit fraud risks), inherent likelihood & impact scores, prioritized in-scope risks, mapped controls, and evaluation criteria. | Prepared by **Internal Auditor / Lead**; reviewed by **Engagement Supervisor**; approved via scope/program by **CAE**. | `[REQ]` [1] (Standards 13.2, 13.4, pp. 95–101), [7], [33] |
| **Topical Requirement Applicability Checklist** | Output | Assessment of whether any IIA Topical Requirement (e.g., Cybersecurity) applies to the engagement scope, and documented rationale for any excluded sub-requirements. | Prepared by **Engagement Lead**; reviewed/approved by **Engagement Supervisor / CAE**. | `[TOPICAL]` [2, 3, 5] |
| **Engagement Planning Memo (Terms of Reference / Scoping Memo)** | Output | Background, engagement objectives, scope boundaries (locations, systems, period covered), out-of-scope items, evaluation criteria, scope limitations (if any), key milestones, and team members. | Prepared by **Engagement Lead / Supervisor**; **Approved by CAE** (or delegated Director); communicated to **Management**. | `[REQ]` (objectives/scope doc & CAE approval) / `[PRACTICE]` (memo format) [1] (Standards 13.1, 13.3, pp. 93–99), [7] |

#### 7. Evidence and Documentation
- `[REQ]` **What Must Be Documented**:
  1. Understanding of the activity and the engagement risk assessment (or review/update of a past engagement risk assessment), including consideration of fraud risks (Standard 13.2, pp. 95–97) [1].
  2. Engagement objectives and scope, plus **evidence of CAE approval** of the objectives and scope and any subsequent changes (Standard 13.3, pp. 98–99) [1].
  3. Evaluation criteria identified and the assessment of their adequacy (Standard 13.4, pp. 100–101) [1].
  4. Communication of objectives, scope, and timing to management (Standard 13.1, pp. 93–95) [1].
- `[REQ]` **Information Standard & Review/Sign-Off**:
  - Standard 13.2 (p. 95) explicitly applies the three-prong evidence standard to planning: `[REQ]` *"To develop an adequate understanding, internal auditors must identify and gather reliable, relevant, and sufficient information..."* [1]. Under Standard 12.3 (p. 90) and Standard 14.6 (p. 111), the Engagement Supervisor reviews planning workpapers and the CAE (or designated delegate per methodology) approves the objectives and scope before Stage 3/4 execution proceeds [1].

#### 8. What Varies (`[REQ]` vs. Methodology vs. Context)
- **Foundation-Fixed (`[REQ]`)**: Gathering reliable, relevant, and sufficient information to understand the activity; assessing and prioritizing engagement risks including fraud risks; assessing adequacy of evaluation criteria; documenting objectives and scope; obtaining CAE approval of objectives and scope (and any changes); communicating objectives, scope, and timing to management; escalating unresolved scope limitations to the Board [1].
- **Methodology Choices (`[PRACTICE]` / `[GUIDE]`)**:
  - *Process Documentation Format*: Narrative memos vs. BPMN/Visio flowcharts vs. direct RCM table entries [1, 7, 31].
  - *Planning Artefact Structure*: Standalone Engagement Planning Memo / Terms of Reference vs. digital planning tabs in TeamMate+, AuditBoard, or Diligent HighBond [7, 31, 32, 33].
  - *Delegation of CAE Approval*: Whether the CAE personally signs off on every Engagement Planning Memo or formally delegates sign-off to Audit Directors/Senior Managers while retaining overall responsibility (`[GUIDE]` Standard 13.3 Considerations, p. 99) [1].
- **Engagement Context**: Whether a prior engagement risk assessment exists (allowing a review-and-update under Standard 13.2, p. 96), whether management has mature documented policies/KPIs or no criteria at all, and whether the engagement is assurance or advisory.

#### 9. Common Failure Modes (EQA / QAIP Deficiencies and Reasons)
1. **`[PRACTICE]` Jumping Directly to Fieldwork Testing Without a Documented Engagement Risk Assessment**:
   - *Deficiency*: Auditors reuse last year's test program verbatim or start testing immediately without documenting an engagement-level risk assessment or fraud risk consideration (violating Standard 13.2) [12, 13, 14].
   - *Reason*: Time pressure to "get into fieldwork" and treating audit programs as static checklists rather than risk-driven plans [13, 14].
2. **`[PRACTICE]` Missing or Unapproved Engagement Objectives, Scope, or Evaluation Criteria**:
   - *Deficiency*: Workpapers lack explicit evaluation criteria (Standard 13.4) or lack documented CAE/Director approval of the engagement objectives and scope prior to fieldwork (Standard 13.3), or fail to obtain re-approval when scope shrinks mid-audit [12, 13, 14].
   - *Reason*: Informal verbal scope changes during fieldwork that are never logged back into the planning workpapers [13, 14].
3. **`[PRACTICE]` Failing to Document Topical Requirement Applicability**:
   - *Deficiency*: Beginning in 2026, engagements that touch cybersecurity or third-party management fail to document whether the mandatory IIA Topical Requirement was assessed during Stage 2 planning or fail to record the rationale for excluding specific topical items [2, 3, 5].
   - *Reason*: Teams unfamiliar with the new 2024 IPPF Topical Requirement mandate [2, 3].

### Stage 3: Designing the Work

#### 1. Purpose and outcome
- `[REQ]` **Purpose**: Translate the prioritized engagement risks, objectives, and evaluation criteria into an approved **Engagement Work Program** (specifying criteria, tasks, testing methodologies/analytical procedures, tools, sample designs, and assigned auditors), confirm engagement resource and competency sufficiency, and issue structured information requests to management (Domain V, Principle 13, Standards 13.4–13.6, pp. 100–104) [1].
- `[REQ]` **How you know it is done**:
  1. Engagement resources (financial, human competencies, technology tools) have been assessed as appropriate and sufficient, or gaps escalated to the CAE (Standard 13.5, pp. 101–102) [1].
  2. The **Engagement Work Program** (mapping objectives → risks → controls/criteria → test steps, methods, sampling plans, and assigned staff) is documented and **reviewed and approved by the CAE before implementation** (Standard 13.6, pp. 103–104) [1].
  3. Initial fieldwork **Information Request Lists (PBC / Provided-by-Client lists)** and sampling frames are prepared and dispatched to management (`[PRACTICE]` [31, 32, 33]).

#### 2. Understand
- `[REQ]` **Resource & Skill Requirements (Standard 13.5, Domain V, Principle 13, p. 101)**:
  - `[REQ]` *"When planning an engagement, internal auditors must identify the types and quantity of resources necessary to achieve the engagement objectives. Internal auditors must consider: The nature and complexity of the engagement; The time frame within which the engagement is to be completed; Whether the available financial, human, and technological resources are appropriate and sufficient to achieve the engagement objectives."* [1].
- `[REQ]` / `[GUIDE]` **Population Characteristics, System Data Structures, and Control Attributes (Standard 13.6, p. 103; Practice Guide *Audit Sampling* & *GTAG Data Analysis Technologies* [8])**:
  - Understand the frequency, automation level (manual vs. IT-dependent manual vs. fully automated application control), population size, data schema, and completeness controls of the transactions/controls to be tested [1, 8, 34].

#### 3. Decide
- `[REQ]` **Key Judgments, Criteria, and Decision Authority**:
  - **Sufficiency of Engagement Resources & Specialist Needs**: **Authority = Internal Auditors → CAE**. `[REQ]` *"If the available resources are inappropriate or insufficient, internal auditors must discuss the concerns with the chief audit executive to obtain the resources."* (Standard 13.5, p. 101) [1].
  - **Selection of Testing Procedures, Analytical Methods, and Sample Sizes**: **Authority = Engagement Lead / Supervisor (approved by CAE via Work Program)**. Criteria include risk significance, control frequency, level of automation, reliability of evidence yielded by each procedure type, and cost relative to benefit (`[REQ]` Standard 4.2, p. 31; Standard 13.6, p. 103) [1, 8, 34].
  - **Approval of the Engagement Work Program (and Any Subsequent Changes)**: **Authority = CAE**. `[REQ]` *"The chief audit executive must review and approve the engagement work program before it is implemented and promptly when any subsequent changes are made."* (Standard 13.6, p. 103) [1].

#### 4. Do (Concrete Activities in Realistic Order, Including Stage-Specific Depth)
1. **Define What "Good" Looks Like for Each Objective and Control (`[REQ]` Standard 13.4, p. 100; Standard 13.6, p. 103)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Defining What "Good" Looks Like**:
     - `[REQ]` The work program must explicitly specify the *"Criteria to be used to evaluate each objective"* (Standard 13.6, p. 103) [1].
     - `[GUIDE]` / `[PRACTICE]` In practice, defining what "good" looks like means decomposing each control or objective into testable **control attributes** across two dimensions (`[GUIDE]` Standard 4.2 Considerations, p. 32; Diligent HighBond & AuditBoard workflows [31, 33]):
       1. **Design Adequacy (Test of Design / TOD)**: Does the control, as designed, have the authority, competence, timeliness, precision/threshold, and follow-up mechanism to prevent or detect the risk before material impact occurs?
       2. **Operating Effectiveness (Test of Operating Effectiveness / TOE)**: Did the control actually operate consistently as designed throughout the audit period (e.g.,Attribute A: Requisition submitted prior to PO creation; Attribute B: Approved in ERP by a manager within the Delegation of Authority matrix; Attribute C: Three-way match completed within 2% tolerance prior to payment release)? [31, 33, 34].
2. **Choose Between Test Types (`[REQ]` Standard 13.6, p. 103; `[GUIDE]` Standard 14.1 Considerations, p. 105)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Choosing Between Test Types: Inquiry, Observation, Inspection, Reperformance, and Analytics**:
     - `[GUIDE]` Under Standard 14.1 Considerations (p. 105) and IIA Practice Guides [7, 8], internal auditors select and combine five core audit procedures, ordered below from **lowest to highest standalone evidence reliability**:
       1. **Inquiry**: Interviewing or surveying personnel (`[GUIDE]` Standard 14.1 Considerations, p. 105 notes that testimonial evidence obtained via inquiry is generally the weakest standalone evidence and requires corroboration) [1].
       2. **Observation**: Watching a process or physical activity being performed in real time (e.g., observing a plant cycle count or physical security badge check); reliable for that point in time, but subject to the Hawthorne effect (subjects behave differently when watched) [1, 7, 27].
       3. **Inspection**: Examining physical assets or documentary/electronic records (e.g., signed contracts, system configurations, change tickets, bank reconciliations, pull-request approvals) [1, 7, 30].
       4. **Analytical Procedures / Data Analytics (CAATs)**: Studying plausible relationships among financial and non-financial data, trend/ratio analysis, regression, or running automated scripts across **100% of a transaction population** to detect anomalies, duplicates, segregation-of-duties (SoD) conflicts, or policy bypasses (`[REQ]` Standard 13.6, p. 103; *GTAG: Data Analysis Technologies* [8]).
       5. **Reperformance**: Independently executing the control procedure or calculation from scratch (e.g., re-running a reserve valuation model, re-calculating a standard cost variance, or attempting an unauthorized system action in a test environment) — provides the **highest level of evidence reliability** because it is obtained directly by the internal auditor (`[REQ]` Standard 14.1, p. 104) [1, 7].
3. **Determine Sample Size and Sampling Method (`[GUIDE]` Standard 14.1 Considerations, p. 105; IIA *Audit Sampling* Practice Guide [8]; Practitioner Tables [34])**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Sample Size and Sampling Method**:
     - `[GUIDE]` Standard 14.1 Considerations (p. 105) states that auditors choose between **100% population testing** (using data analytics/automated tools when complete digital data is available) and **audit sampling** (when testing less than 100% of a population) [1, 8].
     - `[GUIDE]` Per the IIA Practice Guide *Audit Sampling* [8], sampling methods divide into:
       - **Statistical (Probabilistic) Sampling**: Random attribute sampling (for control deviation rates) or Monetary Unit Sampling / classical variables sampling (for dollar misstatements), allowing mathematical calculation of confidence level (e.g., 90% or 95%) and tolerable error rate.
       - **Non-Statistical (Judgmental / Haphazard / Stratified) Sampling**: Selecting items based on risk criteria or control frequency tables without statistical extrapolation [8].
     - `[PRACTICE]` **Standard Practitioner Control-Frequency Attribute Sampling Table** (two-source verified across PwC *Audit Guide*, SOXLayer, and University of Kentucky Internal Audit methodologies, assuming **zero expected deviations**) [34]:
       - **Annual Control (1 occurrence/year)**: Sample size = **1**
       - **Quarterly Control (4 occurrences/year)**: Sample size = **2**
       - **Monthly Control (12 occurrences/year)**: Sample size = **2 to 5** (PwC: **2** Low risk, **3** Moderate risk, **4** High risk)
       - **Semi-Monthly Control (24 occurrences/year)**: Sample size = **3 to 7**
       - **Weekly Control (52 occurrences/year)**: Sample size = **5 to 15** (PwC: **5** Low risk, **7** Moderate risk, **9** High risk)
       - **Daily / Multiple Times per Day (250+ occurrences/year)**: Sample size = **25 to 60** (PwC: **25** Low risk, **40** Moderate risk, **60** High risk; standard SOX 404 baseline is **25** for lower/moderate risk and **40–60** for higher risk)
       - **Automated Application Control**: Sample size = **1 ("Test of One")** per programmed scenario, *provided* that underlying **IT General Controls (ITGCs)** over logical access, program change management, and IT operations are tested and concluded to be effective [34].
4. **Assess Staffing, Competence, and Tools (`[REQ]` Standard 3.1, p. 26; Standard 13.5, pp. 101–102; Standard 13.6, p. 103)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Staffing and Competence**:
     - `[REQ]` Under **Standard 3.1 (p. 26)**: *"Internal auditors must engage only in those services for which they have or can attain the necessary competencies."* [1].
     - `[REQ]` Under **Standard 13.5 (p. 101)**: Auditors evaluate engagement nature/complexity, timeframe, and whether financial, human (skills, specialized knowledge, language), and technological resources (analytics software, GRC scripts) are appropriate and sufficient. If insufficient, auditors must escalate to the CAE to obtain co-sourced/guest-auditor specialists or specialized tools [1].
     - `[REQ]` Under **Standard 13.6 (p. 103)**: The work program must explicitly identify the *"Internal auditors assigned to perform each task."* [1].
5. **Document and Approve the Engagement Work Program (`[REQ]` Standard 13.6, pp. 103–104)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — The Work Programme, and Its Approval**:
     - `[REQ]` *"Internal auditors must develop and document an engagement work program to achieve the engagement objectives. The engagement work program must be based on the information obtained during engagement planning, including, when applicable, the results of the engagement risk assessment."* (Standard 13.6, p. 103) [1].
     - `[REQ]` *"The engagement work program must identify: Criteria to be used to evaluate each objective; Tasks to achieve the engagement objectives; Methodologies, including the analytical procedures to be used, and tools to perform the tasks; Internal auditors assigned to perform each task."* (Standard 13.6, p. 103) [1].
     - `[REQ]` *"The chief audit executive must review and approve the engagement work program before it is implemented and promptly when any subsequent changes are made."* (Standard 13.6, p. 103) [1].
6. **Prepare and Dispatch Information Requests (PBC / IRL Lists) (`[PRACTICE]` [31, 32, 33])**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Information Requests**:
     - `[PRACTICE]` In audit software (Diligent HighBond PBC Requests, TeamMate+ Auditee Portal, AuditBoard Request Lists) [31, 32, 33], the team translates the approved work program into a two-wave **Information Request List (IRL) / Provided-by-Client (PBC) list**:
       - *Wave 1 (Population Extracts)*: Requesting complete system transaction listings or control logs for the audit period so the auditor—not management—selects the sample.
       - *Wave 2 (Sample Item Evidence)*: Once the auditor selects the specific sample items (e.g., 25 daily reconciliations or 40 purchase orders), issuing targeted requests for the supporting documentation for those specific items, with assigned client owners, due dates, and automated reminder/escalation tracking [31, 32, 33].
   - **Loops Triggered in Stage 3**:
     - **Loop L3 (Stage 3 Resource/Access Constraint → Stage 2 Scope Modification)**: If designing the work reveals that needed data does not exist, specialist skills cannot be obtained in time, or a system is inaccessible, the team loops back to Stage 2 to revise engagement scope or declare a scope limitation (`[REQ]` Standards 13.3, 13.5, pp. 98, 101) [1].

#### 5. Communicate
- `[REQ]` **With the CAE / Engagement Supervisor**:
  - **What**: Discussion of any resource, skill, or technology shortfalls (Standard 13.5, p. 101); submission of the Engagement Work Program for mandatory pre-fieldwork review and approval (Standard 13.6, p. 103) [1].
- `[PRACTICE]` **With Activity Management / Process Owners**:
  - **What**: Issuance of the Information Request List (IRL) / PBC requests via the audit portal or email, specifying required populations, data formats, due dates, and walkthrough/testing schedules [31, 32, 33].

#### 7. Evidence and Documentation
- `[REQ]` **What Must Be Documented**:
  1. The **Engagement Work Program** containing all four mandatory elements (criteria for each objective, tasks, methodologies/analytical procedures/tools, and assigned internal auditors) (Standard 13.6, p. 103) [1].
  2. **Pre-implementation CAE review and approval** of the work program, plus prompt approval of any subsequent changes (Standard 13.6, p. 103) [1].
- `[GUIDE]` / `[PRACTICE]` **Sampling Documentation Standard**:
  - To satisfy Standard 14.1 (p. 105) and Standard 14.6 (p. 111) repeatability (*"enable a prudent, informed, and competent person to repeat the engagement work program and reach the same conclusions"*), the sampling workpaper must document how the population was extracted, how population completeness was verified, the sampling rationale/table used, and the unique identifiers of the selected items [1, 8, 34].

#### 8. What Varies (`[REQ]` vs. Methodology vs. Context)
- **Foundation-Fixed (`[REQ]`)**: Assessing engagement resource/competency sufficiency (Standard 13.5); documenting a work program that specifies criteria, tasks, methodologies/tools, and assigned auditors (Standard 13.6); obtaining CAE review and approval of the work program *before* implementation and promptly when changes occur (Standard 13.6) [1].
- **Methodology Choices (`[PRACTICE]` / `[GUIDE]`)**:
  - *Work Program Structure*: RCM-linked control testing steps (split into Test of Design [TOD] and Test of Operating Effectiveness [TOE] as in Diligent HighBond's *Internal Control Workflow* and AuditBoard) vs. objective-linked procedure steps (Diligent's *Workplan Workflow* and TeamMate+ *Audit Program*) [31, 32, 33].
  - *Sample Size Tables*: Whether a daily control requires 25, 30, 40, or 60 items, or whether statistical sampling is required vs. judgmental attribute tables [8, 34].
- **Engagement Context**: Population size and digitization (enabling 100% CAATs script vs. manual sample inspection), whether external auditors plan to rely on the work under PCAOB AS 2605 (which locks in minimum sample sizes) [25, 34], and whether ITGCs are effective (which determines if a "Test of One" is valid for automated controls) [34].

#### 9. Common Failure Modes (EQA / QAIP Deficiencies and Reasons)
1. **`[PRACTICE]` Implementing the Work Program Before CAE/Supervisor Approval (or Unapproved Mid-Fieldwork Edits)**:
   - *Deficiency*: EQA reviewers inspect system timestamps and find fieldwork test workpapers completed days or weeks *before* the Work Program was approved, or find test steps dropped/modified during fieldwork without re-approval (violating Standard 13.6, p. 103) [1, 13, 14].
   - *Reason*: Treating work-program approval as an administrative formality rather than a pre-fieldwork gate [13, 14].
2. **`[PRACTICE]` Unjustified Sample Sizes & Unverified Population Completeness (IPE / IUC Failure)**:
   - *Deficiency*: Auditors select "5 items" or let management hand-pick sample items without documenting the population size, how population completeness was verified against the general ledger/source system, or the sampling methodology/table used [13, 14, 34].
   - *Reason*: Lack of training on the IIA *Audit Sampling* Practice Guide or accepting client-filtered spreadsheets without verifying query parameters [8, 14].
3. **`[PRACTICE]` Mismatch Between Risks/Criteria and Test Steps ("Boilerplate Checklists")**:
   - *Deficiency*: The Stage 2 risk assessment identifies a specific high-priority risk (e.g., vendor bank-account fraud), but the Stage 3 work program uses a generic off-the-shelf procurement checklist that never tests vendor master-file bank changes [12, 13, 14].
   - *Reason*: Copying prior-year or template work programs without mapping every prioritized Stage 2 risk to specific Stage 3 test procedures [13, 14].

### Stage 4: Performing the Work

#### 1. Purpose and outcome
- `[REQ]` **Purpose**: Execute the approved Engagement Work Program by gathering relevant, reliable, and sufficient information, performing analyses and evaluations to compare the existing state (*condition*) against the established *criteria*, investigating exceptions, documenting workpapers so a prudent informed person could repeat the work and reach the same results, and providing ongoing engagement supervision (Domain V, Principle 14, Standards 14.1, 14.2, 14.6, pp. 104–107, 111–112; Domain IV, Principle 12, Standard 12.3, p. 90) [1].
- `[REQ]` **How you know it is done**:
  1. All work-program tasks (including any CAE-approved adjustments) are executed and verified as complete by the CAE or engagement supervisor (Standard 12.3, p. 90; Standard 14.2, p. 106) [1].
  2. Evidence gathered for each procedure has been evaluated and confirmed to be **relevant, reliable, and sufficient** (Standard 14.1, pp. 104–105) [1].
  3. Every test step concludes whether a difference exists between the *criteria* and the *condition* (identifying potential engagement findings where differences exist, or supporting positive assurance where no differences exist) (Standard 14.2, p. 106) [1].
  4. All workpapers have been reviewed for accuracy, relevance, and completeness by the engagement supervisor, all review/coaching notes are resolved, and the engagement documentation is approved by the CAE (Standard 12.3, p. 90; Standard 14.6, p. 111) [1, 32].

#### 2. Understand
- `[REQ]` **Actual Operation of Controls and Transactions ("The Condition")**:
  - `[REQ]` *"Internal auditors must analyze information to determine whether there is a difference between the evaluation criteria and the existing state of the activity under review, known as the 'condition.' Internal auditors must determine the condition by using information and evidence gathered during the engagement."* (Domain V, Principle 14, Standard 14.2, p. 106) [1].
- `[REQ]` **Provenance and Reliability of Information Sources (Standard 14.1, pp. 104–105; Standard 4.3, p. 33)**:
  - Understand whether evidence is original vs. copied, direct vs. client-prepared, corroborated by multiple sources, and generated from a system with effective governance, risk management, and control processes (Standard 14.1, p. 104) [1].

#### 3. Decide
- `[REQ]` **Key Judgments, Criteria, and Decision Authority**:
  - **Whether Evidence Is Relevant, Reliable, and Sufficient**: **Authority = Internal Auditor & Engagement Supervisor**. `[REQ]` *"Internal auditors must evaluate whether the information is relevant and reliable and whether it is sufficient such that analyses provide a reasonable basis upon which to formulate potential engagement findings and conclusions."* (Standard 14.1, p. 105) [1].
  - **Whether to Gather Additional Information or Declare an Inability-to-Obtain-Evidence Finding**: **Authority = Internal Auditor / Supervisor**. `[REQ]` *"Internal auditors must determine whether to gather additional information for analyses and evaluation when evidence is not relevant, reliable, or sufficient to support engagement findings. If relevant evidence cannot be obtained, internal auditors must determine whether to identify that as a finding."* (Standard 14.1, p. 105) [1].
  - **Whether Additional Analyses Are Needed & Adjusting the Work Program**: **Authority = Internal Auditor (judgment) + CAE (mandatory approval of work-program adjustment)**. `[REQ]` *"If initial analyses do not provide sufficient evidence to support a potential engagement finding, internal auditors must exercise due professional care to determine whether additional analyses are required. If additional analyses are required, the work program must be adjusted accordingly and approved by the chief audit executive."* (Standard 14.2, p. 106) [1].
  - **Escalation of Suspected Fraud or Illegal Acts**: **Authority = Internal Auditor → CAE → Authorized Internal/External Authorities**. `[REQ]` *"If internal auditors identify legal or regulatory violations, they must report such incidents to individuals or entities that have the authority to take appropriate action, as specified in laws, regulations, and applicable policies and procedures."* (Domain II, Principle 1, Standard 1.3, p. 19) [1].

#### 4. Do (Concrete Activities in Realistic Order, Including Stage-Specific Depth)
1. **Obtain and Evaluate Evidence, Including Judging Its Reliability (`[REQ]` Standard 14.1, pp. 104–105; Standard 4.3, p. 33)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Obtaining and Evaluating Evidence, Including Judging Its Reliability**:
     - `[REQ]` Standard 14.1 (pp. 104–105) defines the mandatory three-prong quality standard and explicit reliability hierarchy verbatim:
       > *"To perform analyses and evaluations, internal auditors must gather information that is:*
       > - *Relevant – consistent with engagement objectives, within the scope of the engagement, and contributes to the development of engagement results.*
       > - *Reliable – factual and current. Internal auditors use professional skepticism to evaluate whether information is reliable. Reliability is strengthened when the information is:*
       >   - *Obtained directly by an internal auditor or from an independent source.*
       >   - *Corroborated.*
       >   - *Gathered from a system with effective governance, risk management, and control processes.*
       > - *Sufficient – when it enables internal auditors to perform analyses and complete evaluations and can enable a prudent, informed, and competent person to repeat the engagement work program and reach the same conclusions as the internal auditor."* (Standard 14.1, pp. 104–105) [1].
     - `[REQ]` Simultaneously, **Standard 4.3 (Professional Skepticism, Domain II, Principle 4, p. 33)** requires: *"To exercise professional skepticism, internal auditors must: Maintain an attitude that includes inquisitiveness; Critically assess the reliability of information; Be straightforward and honest when raising concerns and asking questions about inconsistent information; Seek additional evidence to make a judgment about information and statements that might be incomplete, inconsistent, false, or misleading."* [1].
     - `[GUIDE]` / `[PRACTICE]` In practice, when management provides spreadsheets or system reports ("Information Produced by the Entity" [IPE]), auditors verify **completeness and accuracy** (inspecting the SQL query/report parameters, tying control totals back to the general ledger or source database, and testing a sample of source records) before relying on the extract [8, 34].
2. **Execute Data Analytics and Full-Population Testing (`[REQ]` Standard 13.6, p. 103; `[GUIDE]` Standard 14.1, p. 105; *GTAG: Data Analysis Technologies* [8]; Benchmarks [36])**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Use of Data Analytics**:
     - `[GUIDE]` Under Standard 14.1 Considerations (p. 105) and *GTAG 16: Data Analysis Technologies* [8], Computer-Assisted Audit Techniques (CAATs), SQL/Python scripts, ACL/Diligent Robots, or BI tools enable internal auditors to evaluate **100% of a transaction population** rather than a sample, eliminating sampling risk for automated or routine data patterns (e.g., duplicate vendor payments, split purchase orders just below approval thresholds, weekend manual journal entries, or terminated employees with active system logins) [1, 8, 31].
     - `[PRACTICE]` **Empirical Reality Check (Two-Source Verified Statistic)**: According to The IIA's *2025 Pulse of Internal Audit*, Weaver's 2025 industry benchmarks, and Deloitte's *Internal Audit Digital & Analytics Survey* [36], while **~92%** of CAEs view data analytics as vital and **~41%** of internal audit teams report using Generative AI (primarily for scoping research, summarizing documents, and drafting report text), only **~28%** of internal audit functions report having "high or advanced" data analytics capabilities embedded in routine fieldwork—primarily due to fragmented ERP data access, poor data quality, and specialist skill gaps [36].
3. **Compare Condition Against Criteria & Investigate Exceptions (`[REQ]` Standard 14.2, p. 106)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Investigating Exceptions**:
     - `[REQ]` *"Internal auditors must analyze information to determine whether there is a difference between the evaluation criteria and the existing state of the activity under review, known as the 'condition.' ... A difference between the criteria and the condition indicates a potential engagement finding that must be noted and further evaluated."* (Standard 14.2, p. 106) [1].
     - `[GUIDE]` / `[PRACTICE]` When a sample item or analytics output shows an exception (a deviation from a control attribute or policy criterion), the auditor does not immediately write a final report finding. First, the auditor **validates the factual accuracy** of the exception with the process operator to confirm whether it is a **true exception** or a **false positive** (e.g., an approved policy waiver stored in a separate repository or an analytics script join error) [8, 31, 34].
4. **Determine When to Expand Testing or Change Approach (`[REQ]` Standards 14.1–14.2, pp. 105–106; `[GUIDE]` Practice Guide *Audit Sampling* [8])**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — When to Expand Testing or Change Approach**:
     - `[REQ]` Under **Standard 14.2 (p. 106)**: *"If initial analyses do not provide sufficient evidence to support a potential engagement finding, internal auditors must exercise due professional care to determine whether additional analyses are required. If additional analyses are required, the work program must be adjusted accordingly and approved by the chief audit executive."* [1].
     - `[GUIDE]` / `[PRACTICE]` **Decision Rules in Practice**:
       1. *Isolated vs. Systemic Determination*: If a zero-expected-deviation attribute sample (e.g., 25 daily controls) yields 1 exception, methodologies (such as PwC's *Audit Guide* and IIA *Audit Sampling* [8, 34]) distinguish between expanding the sample (e.g., testing an additional 15–25 items to test whether the deviation rate is within tolerable bounds) vs. declaring the control **ineffective** immediately if 2+ exceptions occur or if the single exception reveals a systemic design flaw [8, 34].
       2. *Quantifying Impact & Root Cause*: Once a control is known to have failed, expanding the sample purely to prove failure is redundant; instead, auditors **change approach**—shifting from attribute sampling to a targeted data analytics query across the full population to quantify the actual financial/operational exposure (the *effect*) and isolate the *root cause* [8, 34].
       3. *Switching from Control-Reliance to Substantive Testing*: When an IT general control (ITGC) or automated control fails, auditors cannot rely on a "Test of One" and must adjust the work program (with CAE approval under Standard 13.6/14.2) to perform compensating manual or substantive testing [1, 34].
5. **Handle Suspected Fraud or Illegal Acts (`[REQ]` Standards 1.2, 1.3, 4.2, 4.3, pp. 18–19, 31, 33)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Handling Suspected Fraud**:
     - `[REQ]` Internal auditors must assess the *"Probability of significant errors, fraud, noncompliance, and other risks"* (Standard 4.2, p. 31) and seek additional evidence when statements appear *"incomplete, inconsistent, false, or misleading"* (Standard 4.3, p. 33) [1].
     - `[REQ]` Under **Standard 1.3 (Legal and Ethical Behavior, Domain II, Principle 1, p. 19)**: *"If internal auditors identify legal or regulatory violations, they must report such incidents to individuals or entities that have the authority to take appropriate action, as specified in laws, regulations, and applicable policies and procedures."* [1].
     - `[GUIDE]` / `[PRACTICE]` **Critical Operational Rule when Fraud Is Suspected**: When an auditor discovers red flags of potential fraud during Stage 4 fieldwork (e.g., altered invoices, fictitious vendors, unauthorized override), the auditor **must NOT confront the suspected perpetrator** or alert implicated local management (which risks destruction of evidence or tipping off). Instead, the auditor preserves the evidence, escalates immediately to the **Engagement Supervisor and CAE**, and the CAE coordinates with General Counsel, Corporate Security / Forensic Investigators, Senior Management, or the Audit Committee (if senior management is implicated) to launch a formal forensic investigation (`[GUIDE]` Standard 1.3 Considerations, p. 19; Standard 8.1, p. 52) [1].
6. **Document Workpapers and Conduct Ongoing Supervision During Fieldwork (`[REQ]` Standard 12.3, p. 90; Standard 14.6, pp. 111–112)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Supervision During Fieldwork**:
     - `[REQ]` **Standard 12.3 (Oversee and Improve Engagement Performance, Domain IV, Principle 12, p. 90)** states verbatim:
       > *"The chief audit executive must establish and implement methodologies for engagement supervision, quality assurance, and the development of competencies. The chief audit executive or an engagement supervisor must provide internal auditors with guidance throughout the engagement, verify work programs are complete, and confirm engagement workpapers adequately support findings, conclusions, and recommendations... The chief audit executive must ensure that evidence of supervision is documented and retained, according to the internal audit function’s established methodologies."* [1].
     - `[REQ]` **Standard 14.6 (Engagement Documentation, Domain V, Principle 14, p. 111)** states verbatim:
       > *"Internal auditors and the engagement supervisor must review the engagement documentation for accuracy, relevance, and completeness. The chief audit executive must review and approve the engagement documentation."* [1].
     - `[PRACTICE]` **How Software Implements Fieldwork Supervision (TeamMate+, Diligent HighBond, AuditBoard, Workiva)**:
       - Auditors attach documentary evidence, spreadsheets, and analytics outputs to each test step (in TeamMate+, the **Record of Work Done [RWD]**; in Diligent/AuditBoard, **Test Workpapers** with TOD/TOE tabs; in Workiva, **Document Markups and Tickmarks**) and mark the workpaper `Prepared / Ready for Review` [31, 32, 33].
       - The Engagement Supervisor reviews workpapers *in-flight* during fieldwork and raises **Coaching Notes / Review Notes** anchored to specific procedures or cells [32, 33].
       - The preparer addresses each note and marks it `Resolved / Awaiting Verification`; the supervisor verifies the fix and transitions the note to `Closed / Accepted`. In TeamMate+, a permanent timestamped audit trail of coaching notes is retained, and a hard **"No Open Notes"** validation gate blocks engagement finalization if any coaching note remains open [32].
   - **Loops Triggered in Stage 4**:
     - **Loop L4 (Stage 4 Insufficient Evidence → Stage 4 Re-Collection / Stage 5 Finding)**: If evidence is not relevant, reliable, or sufficient, gather additional evidence; if relevant evidence cannot be obtained at all, evaluate whether to report the inability to obtain evidence as a finding (`[REQ]` Standard 14.1, p. 105) or escalate a scope limitation (`[REQ]` Standard 13.3, p. 98) [1].
     - **Loop L5 (Stage 4 Exception / New Risk Discovery → Stage 3 Work Program Revision / Stage 2 Scope Change)**: When initial analyses require additional procedures or uncover a new risk area, adjust the work program and obtain **CAE approval** (`[REQ]` Standard 14.2, p. 106; Standard 13.6, p. 103) and, if scope or objectives change, obtain **CAE approval** (`[REQ]` Standard 13.3, p. 98) and notify management timely (`[REQ]` Standard 13.1, p. 93) [1].

#### 5. Communicate
- `[REQ]` **With Process Personnel and Activity Management**:
  - **What**: Follow-up on PBC/information requests, straightforward inquiries about inconsistent or incomplete information (`[REQ]` Standard 4.3, p. 33), and interim status updates / validation of preliminary factual exceptions (`[REQ]` Standard 13.1, p. 93) [1].
- `[REQ]` **With Engagement Supervisor and CAE**:
  - **What**: Ongoing supervisory check-ins, coaching/review note resolution (`[REQ]` Standard 12.3, p. 90), requests for CAE approval of work-program adjustments (`[REQ]` Standard 14.2, p. 106), and immediate confidential escalation of suspected fraud or illegal acts (`[REQ]` Standard 1.3, p. 19) [1].

#### 7. Evidence and Documentation
- `[REQ]` **What Must Be Documented**:
  1. Information and evidence supporting engagement results, documented to the **Repeatability Standard**: `[REQ]` *"The analyses, evaluations, and supporting information relevant to an engagement must be documented such that an informed, prudent internal auditor, or similarly informed and competent person, could repeat the work and derive the same engagement results."* (Standard 14.6, p. 111; see also Standard 14.1 definition of *Sufficient*, p. 105) [1].
  2. Any potential engagement finding where a difference exists between criteria and condition (Standard 14.2, p. 106) [1].
  3. Evidence of engagement supervision (Standard 12.3, p. 90) and two-tier review/approval of engagement documentation: review by internal auditors and the engagement supervisor for accuracy, relevance, and completeness, and review/approval by the CAE (Standard 14.6, p. 111) [1].
  4. Retention of engagement documentation in accordance with laws, regulations, and policies (Standard 14.6, p. 111) [1].

#### 8. What Varies (`[REQ]` vs. Methodology vs. Context)
- **Foundation-Fixed (`[REQ]`)**: Gathering relevant, reliable, and sufficient information (Standard 14.1); exercising professional skepticism (Standard 4.3); comparing condition against criteria and noting differences as potential findings (Standard 14.2); obtaining CAE approval when adjusting the work program for additional analyses (Standard 14.2); documenting work to the prudent-person repeatability standard (Standard 14.6); documenting supervisory review and CAE approval of documentation (Standards 12.3, 14.6) [1].
- **Methodology Choices (`[PRACTICE]` / `[GUIDE]`)**:
  - *Tickmark Conventions & Workpaper Templates*: Standardized Excel/Workiva tickmark legends vs. structured form fields in TeamMate+ RWD or AuditBoard test tabs [31, 32, 33].
  - *Review Note Retention Policy*: Permanently archiving resolved coaching notes in the database (as in WIPO's TeamMate+ workflow [32]) vs. deleting routine coaching notes upon sign-off while retaining only the reviewer's sign-off timestamp (`[GUIDE]` Standard 12.3 Examples of Evidence, p. 91) [1, 32].
  - *Sensitive Evidence Storage*: Storing full raw PII/payroll files in the workpaper vs. documenting only unique transaction IDs and test results to comply with GDPR/privacy policies (`[REQ]` Standard 5.2 Protection of Information, p. 35) [1].
- **Engagement Context**: Availability of structured digital logs for 100% analytics vs. physical shop-floor/paper records, complexity of models/systems, and whether fraud indicators arise.

#### 9. Common Failure Modes (EQA / QAIP Deficiencies and Reasons)
1. **`[PRACTICE]` Untimely Supervisory Review (Workpapers Signed Off *After* the Final Audit Report Date)**:
   - *Deficiency*: EQA assessors compare workpaper review timestamps against the final report issue date and discover that the Audit Manager or CAE batch-signed off on fieldwork workpapers days or weeks *after* the final report was already distributed to the Audit Committee (violating Standards 12.3 and 14.6) [1, 12, 14].
   - *Reason*: Supervisors reviewing only the draft Word/PowerPoint report under deadline pressure while leaving the underlying audit-software workpaper sign-offs as "post-report administrative cleanup" [14].
2. **`[PRACTICE]` Failure to Meet the "Repeatability Standard" (Conclusory Workpapers Without Source Identifiers)**:
   - *Deficiency*: Workpapers state *"Tested 25 items, no exceptions noted"* without listing the 25 unique transaction IDs, the attributes tested, or how the population was verified—making it impossible for an informed, prudent auditor to repeat the work (`[REQ]` Standards 14.1, 14.6) [1, 13, 14].
   - *Reason*: Confusing a summary assertion with documented audit evidence [13, 14].
3. **`[PRACTICE]` Relying on Uncorroborated Client Inquiry ("Management Stated...")**:
   - *Deficiency*: Concluding that a control is effective based solely on an interview statement from the control owner without inspecting documentary evidence, system configurations, or reperforming the control (violating Standards 4.3 and 14.1) [1, 13, 14].
   - *Reason*: Lack of professional skepticism or reluctance to challenge senior personnel when documentary evidence is slow to arrive [14].

### Stage 5: Forming and Discussing Findings

#### 1. Purpose and outcome
- `[REQ]` **Purpose**: Evaluate each potential engagement finding to determine its root cause, potential effects, and significance (likelihood × impact), prioritize the findings, validate factual accuracy with management, develop recommendations and/or collaborate with management to agree on corrective action plans (with responsible owners and target completion dates), and resolve or escalate any disagreements or unacceptable risk acceptances (Domain V, Principle 14, Standards 14.2–14.4, pp. 106–110; Principle 13, Standard 13.1, p. 93; Principle 15, Standard 15.1, p. 113; Domain IV, Principle 11, Standard 11.5, p. 84) [1].
- `[REQ]` **How you know it is done**:
  1. Every potential finding has been evaluated for **significance** (likelihood and impact) and prioritized using the CAE's established methodology; all significant risks are documented as findings to be communicated (Standard 14.3, pp. 107–108) [1].
  2. Each finding documents the **criteria**, **condition**, **root causes (when possible)**, **potential effects**, **significance/prioritization**, and **recommendations and/or management action plans** specifying the **responsible individual(s)** and **planned completion date** (Standards 14.2–14.4, 15.1, pp. 106–110, 113) [1].
  3. Findings and recommendations have been discussed with activity management (typically culminated in an Exit Conference), and any unresolved disagreements on facts or action plans are documented for dual-position disclosure or escalated for unacceptable risk acceptance (Standards 13.1, 14.4, 11.5, pp. 93, 109, 84) [1].

#### 2. Understand
- `[REQ]` **Root Causes and Organizational Context (Standard 14.3, Domain V, Principle 14, p. 107)**:
  - `[REQ]` *"When evaluating potential engagement findings, internal auditors must collaborate with management to identify the root causes when possible, determine the potential effects, and evaluate the significance of the issue."* [1].
- `[REQ]` **Compensating Controls, Risk Appetite/Tolerance, and Feasibility of Remediation (Standards 14.3–14.4, pp. 107–110; Standard 11.5, p. 84)**:
  - Understand whether mitigating or compensating controls reduce actual exposure, what the organization's risk appetite/tolerance is, and what operational constraints affect management's proposed remediation plan and timeline [1, 15].

#### 3. Decide
- `[REQ]` **Key Judgments, Criteria, and Decision Authority**:
  - **Significance & Prioritization of Each Finding (and Whether Non-Significant Risks Are Reported)**: **Authority = Internal Auditors / Engagement Supervisor / CAE (per CAE's methodology)**. `[REQ]` *"To determine the significance of the risk, internal auditors must consider the likelihood of the risk occurring and the impact the risk may have on the organization’s governance, risk management, or control processes. If internal auditors determine that the organization is exposed to a significant risk, it must be documented and communicated as a finding. Internal auditors must determine whether to report other risks as findings, based on the circumstances and established methodologies. Internal auditors must prioritize each engagement finding based on its significance, using methodologies established by the chief audit executive."* (Standard 14.3, pp. 107–108) [1].
  - **Whether to Issue Recommendations, Request Action Plans, or Collaborate on Agreed Actions**: **Authority = Internal Auditors / CAE** (while **Management** owns the decision and responsibility for selecting and implementing the actual remediation). `[REQ]` *"Internal auditors must determine whether to develop recommendations, request action plans from management, or collaborate with management to agree on actions..."* (Standard 14.4, p. 109) [1]. Note: Under Standard 14.4 Considerations (p. 109), internal auditors must not assume management responsibility by dictating or implementing the fix [1].
  - **Handling Disagreement on Engagement Results or Action Plans**: **Authority = Internal Auditors / CAE (never obligated to alter valid evidence-based results)**. `[REQ]` *"If a mutual understanding cannot be reached, internal auditors must not be obligated to change any portion of the engagement results unless there is a valid reason to do so."* (Standard 13.1, p. 93) [1].
  - **Judging Whether Management Has Accepted Unacceptable Risk**: **Authority = CAE → Senior Management → Board**. `[REQ]` *"When the chief audit executive concludes that management has accepted a level of risk that exceeds the organization’s risk appetite or risk tolerance, the matter must be discussed with senior management. If the chief audit executive determines that the matter has not been resolved by senior management, the matter must be escalated to the board. It is not the responsibility of the chief audit executive to resolve the risk."* (Standard 11.5, p. 84) [1].

#### 4. Do (Concrete Activities in Realistic Order, Including Stage-Specific Depth)
1. **Construct Finding Attributes: What the Standards Actually Require vs. Common Practice ("The 5 Cs") (`[REQ]` Standards 14.2–14.4, 15.1, pp. 106–110, 113; `[GUIDE]` / `[PRACTICE]` [15, 31, 32])**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Finding Attributes: The "5 Cs" vs. Exact 2024 Standards Requirements**:
     - `[GUIDE]` / `[PRACTICE]` Across practitioner literature (The IIA, Chartered IIA UK [15]) and audit software (Diligent HighBond, TeamMate+, AuditBoard [31, 32, 33]), findings are structured around the classic **"5 Cs" mnemonic**: **Criteria, Condition, Cause, Consequence (Effect), and Corrective Action (Recommendation)**.
     - `[REQ]` **What the 2024 Global Internal Audit Standards Actually Require Verbatim**: The 2024 Standards do **not** use the phrase "5 Cs", but they mandate **all five underlying attributes plus two additional mandatory elements** across Standards 14.2, 14.3, 14.4, and 15.1:
       1. **Criteria (`[REQ]` Standards 13.4 & 14.2, pp. 100, 106)**: Specifications of the desired state (*"what should exist"*).
       2. **Condition (`[REQ]` Standard 14.2, p. 106)**: *"the existing state of the activity under review, known as the 'condition'"* (*"what actually exists"*, determined from engagement evidence).
       3. **Root Cause (`[REQ]` Standard 14.3, p. 107 — qualified by *"when possible"*)**: *"When evaluating potential engagement findings, internal auditors must collaborate with management to identify the root causes when possible..."* (Glossary, p. 13: *"Core issue or underlying reason for a difference between the criteria and condition of an activity under review."*).
       4. **Potential Effects (`[REQ]` Standard 14.3, p. 107)**: *"...determine the potential effects..."* (the risk or actual/potential exposure resulting from the condition).
       5. **Significance and Prioritization (`[REQ]` Standard 14.3, pp. 107–108 & Standard 15.1, p. 113 — BEYOND the classic 5 Cs)**: Evaluating likelihood × impact and prioritizing each finding (e.g., High / Medium / Low rating).
       6. **Recommendations and/or Action Plans (`[REQ]` Standard 14.4, p. 109 & Standard 15.1, p. 113)**: Actions to resolve the gap between criteria and condition, mitigate risk, and address the root cause.
       7. **Action Owner and Planned Completion Date (`[REQ]` Standard 15.1, p. 113 — BEYOND the classic 5 Cs)**: *"The final communication must specify the individuals responsible for addressing the findings and the planned date by which the actions should be completed."* (Plus acknowledging any actions already initiated or completed before final communication, Standard 15.1, p. 113) [1].
2. **Collaborate with Management on Root-Cause Analysis (RCA) (`[REQ]` Standard 14.3, p. 107; `[GUIDE]` / `[PRACTICE]` [15, 26])**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Root-Cause Analysis**:
     - `[REQ]` Standard 14.3 (p. 107) requires internal auditors to *"collaborate with management to identify the root causes when possible"*, and Standard 14.4 (p. 109) requires recommendations/action plans to *"Address the root cause of the finding"* [1].
     - `[GUIDE]` Standard 14.3 Considerations (p. 108) explains that if practical constraints (such as complex cross-departmental failures or tight timelines) prevent identifying the ultimate root cause during the engagement, auditors should recommend that management conduct a formal root-cause investigation as part of the action plan [1].
     - `[GUIDE]` / `[PRACTICE]` Per the Chartered IIA Guide on *Root Cause Analysis and the 5 Cs* [15] and ISO/manufacturing audit practice [26], auditors apply structured RCA methods—specifically the **5 Whys**, **Ishikawa (Fishbone) diagrams**, **Fault Tree Analysis**, or **8D problem solving**—to push past proximate symptoms (*"the reconciliation was not signed"*) to underlying systemic causes (e.g., broken ERP workflow routing, unrealistic workload after downsizing, ambiguous SOPs, or misaligned incentive structures) [15, 26].
3. **Evaluate Significance and Rate / Prioritize Findings (`[REQ]` Standard 14.3, pp. 107–108; `[PRACTICE]` [35])**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Rating or Prioritising Findings**:
     - `[REQ]` Standard 14.3 (pp. 107–108) mandates evaluating significance based on **likelihood** and **impact** and prioritizing every finding according to the CAE's established methodology [1].
     - `[PRACTICE]` **Two-Source Verified Rating Scales in Practice** (MIT Audit Division, UNOPS Internal Audit, and Richard Chambers & Associates) [35]:
       - **3-Tier Finding Scale**: **High (Critical / Major)** — material financial, regulatory, safety, or strategic exposure requiring immediate executive/Audit Committee attention; **Medium (Moderate / Significant)** — meaningful control deficiency with moderate exposure requiring timely management remediation; **Low (Minor / Advisory)** — isolated deviation or efficiency improvement opportunity (often communicated in a separate Management Letter or verbally rather than the formal board report) [1, 35].
       - **4-Tier Finding Scale** (common in banking and SOX): Adds **Critical / Repeat / MRIA-equivalent** above High, or distinguishes **Significant Deficiency** vs. **Material Weakness** (under PCAOB AS 2201 for ICFR) [18, 25, 35].
4. **Validate Facts with Management (`[REQ]` Standards 13.1, 14.3, 14.4, pp. 93, 107, 109)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Validating Facts with Management**:
     - `[REQ]` Internal auditors discuss potential findings with activity management during fieldwork and at an **Exit Meeting / Closing Conference** to confirm that the *condition* and *criteria* are factually accurate and that no mitigating evidence was overlooked (`[REQ]` Standard 1.1 Honesty and Professional Courage, p. 17; Standard 13.1, p. 93; Standard 14.3, p. 107) [1].
     - `[PRACTICE]` In TeamMate+, AuditBoard, and Diligent HighBond, auditors release draft Finding/Observation records to the auditee portal so process owners can confirm factual accuracy before formal action-plan negotiation [31, 32, 33].
5. **Agree Action Plans, Owners, and Target Completion Dates (`[REQ]` Standard 14.4, pp. 109–110; Standard 15.1, p. 113)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Agreeing Action Plans, Including Owners and Dates**:
     - `[REQ]` Under **Standard 14.4 (p. 109)**, internal auditors develop recommendations, request management action plans, or collaborate on agreed actions that resolve the condition-criteria gap, mitigate risk to an acceptable level, and address the root cause. Under **Standard 15.1 (p. 113)**, the final communication **must** specify the **individuals responsible** for addressing the findings and the **planned completion date** [1].
     - `[GUIDE]` Standard 14.4 Considerations (p. 109) notes that while auditors evaluate the adequacy of management's proposed action plan (cost-benefit, feasibility, and whether it actually fixes the root cause), **management—not internal audit—owns the action plan**; if internal audit designed and implemented the remediation, it would impair objectivity for future assurance reviews under Standard 2.2 (p. 22) [1].
6. **Handle Disagreement and Unacceptable Risk Acceptance (`[REQ]` Standards 1.1, 8.1, 11.5, 13.1, 14.4, pp. 17, 52, 84, 93, 109)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Handling Disagreement and Risk Acceptance**:
     - **Case A: Factual or Analytical Disagreement on Findings (`[REQ]` Standard 13.1, p. 93 & Standard 1.1, p. 17)**:
       - `[REQ]` *"At the end of an engagement, if internal auditors and management do not agree on the engagement results, internal auditors must discuss and try to reach a mutual understanding of the issue with the management of the activity under review. If a mutual understanding cannot be reached, internal auditors must not be obligated to change any portion of the engagement results unless there is a valid reason to do so. Internal auditors must follow an established methodology to allow both parties to express their positions regarding the content of the final engagement communication and the reasons for any differences of opinion regarding the engagement results."* (Standard 13.1, p. 93) [1].
       - `[REQ]` Furthermore, Standard 1.1 (p. 17) strictly prohibits concealing or omitting findings: *"Internal auditors must not make false, misleading, or deceptive statements, nor conceal or omit findings or other pertinent information from communications."* And Standard 8.1 (p. 52) requires the CAE to bring unresolved disagreements with senior management on scope or findings to the **Board** [1].
     - **Case B: Disagreement on Recommendations or Action Plans (`[REQ]` Standard 14.4, p. 109)**:
       - `[REQ]` *"If internal auditors and management disagree about the engagement recommendations and/or action plans, internal auditors must follow an established methodology to allow both parties to express their positions and rationale and to determine a resolution."* (Standard 14.4, p. 109) [1].
     - **Case C: Management Risk Acceptance Exceeding Risk Appetite/Tolerance (`[REQ]` Standard 11.5, p. 84)**:
       - `[REQ]` *"The chief audit executive must communicate unacceptable levels of risk. When the chief audit executive concludes that management has accepted a level of risk that exceeds the organization’s risk appetite or risk tolerance, the matter must be discussed with senior management. If the chief audit executive determines that the matter has not been resolved by senior management, the matter must be escalated to the board. It is not the responsibility of the chief audit executive to resolve the risk."* (Standard 11.5, p. 84) [1].
   - **Loops Triggered in Stage 5**:
     - **Loop L6 (Stage 5 Management Factual Challenge → Stage 4 Re-Testing / Additional Evidence)**: When management presents new evidence or disputes the factual condition during Stage 5 validation, the auditor loops back to Stage 4 to inspect/corroborate the newly supplied evidence and either revises the finding (if valid) or retains the finding with documented dual positions (`[REQ]` Standards 13.1, 14.2, pp. 93, 106) [1].

#### 5. Communicate
- `[REQ]` **With Activity Management**:
  - **What**: Draft findings (criteria, condition, root cause, potential effect, rating), recommendations, requests for management action plans (owner + due date), and Exit Meeting discussion (Standards 13.1, 14.3, 14.4) [1].
- `[REQ]` **With Senior Management and the Board (When Escalated)**:
  - **What**: Escalation of disagreements on findings/action plans (Standards 8.1, 13.1, 14.4) and escalation of unacceptable risk acceptance (Standard 11.5, p. 84) [1].

#### 7. Evidence and Documentation
- `[REQ]` **What Must Be Documented**:
  1. Each finding where the organization is exposed to a significant risk, including its criteria, condition, root cause (when possible), potential effects, significance evaluation (likelihood × impact), and prioritization (Standards 14.2, 14.3, pp. 106–108) [1].
  2. Recommendations and/or agreed management action plans, responsible individuals, and planned completion dates (Standards 14.4, 15.1, pp. 109, 113) [1].
  3. Both parties' positions and rationale whenever internal audit and management disagree on engagement results or recommendations/action plans (Standards 13.1, 14.4, pp. 93, 109) [1].
  4. Discussions with senior management and escalation to the Board whenever management accepts risk exceeding risk appetite/tolerance (Standard 11.5, p. 84) [1].

#### 8. What Varies (`[REQ]` vs. Methodology vs. Context)
- **Foundation-Fixed (`[REQ]`)**: Determining condition vs. criteria (14.2); collaborating with management on root causes when possible, determining potential effects, and evaluating significance via likelihood × impact (14.3); documenting and communicating all significant-risk exposures as findings and prioritizing them (14.3); discussing recommendations/action plans with management (14.4); specifying action owners and target completion dates (15.1); refusing to alter valid findings without valid reason and documenting both positions in disagreements (13.1, 14.4); escalating unacceptable risk acceptance to senior management and the Board (11.5) [1].
- **Methodology Choices (`[PRACTICE]` / `[GUIDE]`)**:
  - *Terminology*: Calling the record a **"Finding"** (2024 Standards term), **"Observation"**, **"Issue"**, **"Deficiency"**, **"Audit Point"**, or **"Non-Conformance Report (NCR)"** [1, 15, 26, 31, 32].
  - *Threshold for Reporting Minor Issues*: Whether Low-risk findings are included in the main audit report, moved to a separate **Management Letter**, or communicated verbally (`[REQ]` Standard 14.3 leaves non-significant risks to the CAE's methodology, p. 107) [1, 35].
  - *Recommendations vs. Direct Action Plans*: Whether internal audit writes both a formal Recommendation *and* collects a Management Action Plan, or collaborates directly on an Agreed Action Plan (`[REQ]` Standard 14.4 explicitly permits all three approaches, p. 109) [1].
- **Engagement Context**: Complexity of root causes, management receptiveness vs. pushback, and organizational risk appetite thresholds.

#### 9. Common Failure Modes (EQA / QAIP Deficiencies and Reasons)
1. **`[PRACTICE]` Superficial or Missing Root-Cause Analysis (Restating the Condition as the Cause)**:
   - *Deficiency*: The finding states: *Condition: "3 of 25 reconciliations were not reviewed." Cause: "Reconciliations were not reviewed by the supervisor."* This restates the symptom rather than identifying *why* the review failed (violating Standard 14.3, p. 107) [1, 12, 13, 15].
   - *Reason*: Auditors treating the "Cause" field in software templates as a text-box to fill out at their desk rather than conducting a structured 5-Whys inquiry with the process owner [13, 15].
2. **`[PRACTICE]` Vague Action Plans ("Management Will Remind Staff") or Missing Owners/Dates**:
   - *Deficiency*: Accepting soft action plans that do not address the root cause, assigning action ownership to a committee or multiple departments instead of a single accountable individual, or leaving completion dates as "TBD / Ongoing" (violating Standards 14.4 and 15.1) [1, 13, 14].
   - *Reason*: Rushing to close the exit meeting and issue the report without challenging unworkable management responses [13, 14].
3. **`[PRACTICE]` Softening or Dropping Valid Findings Under Management Pressure Without New Evidence**:
   - *Deficiency*: Downgrading a High finding to Medium or deleting it from the draft report solely because an executive objected during the exit conference, without new corroborating evidence (violating Standards 1.1, 2.2, and 13.1) [1, 12, 14].
   - *Reason*: Lack of professional courage (Standard 1.1) or weak CAE backing when confronting senior management [1, 14].

### Stage 6: Reporting and Follow-Up

#### 1. Purpose and outcome
- `[REQ]` **Purpose**: Formulate an overall engagement conclusion (and, where requested, macro-level thematic or organizational conclusions), issue an accurate, objective, clear, concise, constructive, complete, and timely CAE-approved final engagement communication to appropriate stakeholders, correct any significant post-issuance errors or omissions, and monitor and independently confirm the implementation of management's corrective action plans—escalating overdue actions that represent unacceptable risk acceptance to senior management and the Board (Domain V, Principle 14, Standard 14.5, p. 110; Principle 15, Standards 15.1–15.2, pp. 112–115; Domain IV, Principle 11, Standards 11.2–11.5, pp. 79–85; Domain III, Principle 8, Standard 8.1, p. 52) [1].
- `[REQ]` **How you know it is done**:
  1. An **Engagement Conclusion** summarizing results relative to objectives and the overall significance of aggregated findings is documented (Standard 14.5, p. 110) [1].
  2. The **Final Engagement Communication (Audit Report)** containing all mandatory elements is **reviewed and approved by the CAE** and disseminated to stakeholders who can ensure results receive due consideration (Standard 11.3, p. 81; Standard 15.1, pp. 113–114) [1].
  3. All engagement documentation is reviewed and approved by the CAE and locked for retention (Standard 14.6, p. 111) [1, 32].
  4. Findings and action plans are logged in a **Tracking System**, follow-up assessments confirm remediation effectiveness (closing the actions) or overdue actions are escalated for risk acceptance to senior management and the Board (Standard 15.2, pp. 114–115; Standard 11.5, p. 84) [1, 31, 32].

#### 2. Understand
- `[REQ]` **Aggregated Impact of Findings Across the Activity and Organization (Standards 14.5, 11.3, pp. 110, 80–81)**:
  - Understand how individual findings interact: do multiple Medium findings in the same process compound into an overall ineffective control environment (`[REQ]` Standard 14.5, p. 110), and do findings across multiple engagements reveal systemic **themes** or support a **business-unit/organization-level conclusion** (`[REQ]` Standard 11.3, p. 81)? [1, 10].
- `[REQ]` **Remediation Progress and Evidence of Action Plan Implementation (Standard 15.2, pp. 114–115)**:
  - Understand management's progress on open action plans, the reasons for any missed target dates, and the evidence demonstrating that new controls actually operate effectively [1, 31, 32].

#### 3. Decide
- `[REQ]` **Key Judgments, Criteria, and Decision Authority**:
  - **Overall Engagement Conclusion / Opinion Rating**: **Authority = Internal Auditors / Engagement Supervisor, Approved by CAE**. `[REQ]` *"The engagement conclusion must summarize the internal auditors’ professional judgment about the overall significance of the aggregated engagement findings. Assurance engagement conclusions must include the internal auditors’ judgment regarding the effectiveness of the governance, risk management, and/or control processes of the activity under review, including an acknowledgment of when processes are effective."* (Standard 14.5, p. 110) [1].
  - **Approval and Dissemination of Final Engagement Communication (and External Release)**: **Authority = CAE** (retaining overall responsibility even if delegated). `[REQ]` *"The chief audit executive must review and approve final engagement communications, which include engagement conclusions, and decide to whom and how they will be disseminated before they are issued. If these duties are delegated to other internal auditors, the chief audit executive retains overall responsibility. The chief audit executive must seek the advice of legal counsel and/or senior management as required before releasing final communications to parties outside the organization, unless otherwise required or restricted by laws and/or regulations."* (Standard 11.3, p. 81; see also Standard 15.1, p. 113) [1].
  - **Whether a Post-Issuance Error or Omission Is "Significant"**: **Authority = CAE (using criteria agreed with the Board)**. `[REQ]` *"Significance is determined according to criteria agreed upon with the board."* (Standard 11.4, p. 83) [1].
  - **Extent of Follow-Up Verification Procedures & Whether Delay Constitutes Unacceptable Risk Acceptance**: **Authority = Internal Auditors (extent based on finding significance) & CAE (risk acceptance by delay/inaction)**. `[REQ]` *"The extent of these procedures must consider the significance of the finding... The chief audit executive is responsible for determining whether senior management, by delay or inaction, has accepted a risk that exceeds the risk tolerance."* (Standard 15.2, pp. 114–115) [1].

#### 4. Do (Concrete Activities in Realistic Order, Including Stage-Specific Depth)
1. **Formulate the Engagement Conclusion (Micro Opinion) (`[REQ]` Standard 14.5, p. 110; Practice Guide *Formulating and Expressing Internal Audit Opinions* [10]; Rating Scales [35])**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Engagement Conclusions or Opinions**:
     - `[REQ]` **Standard 14.5 (Engagement Conclusions, Domain V, Principle 14, p. 110)** states verbatim:
       > *"Internal auditors must develop an engagement conclusion that summarizes the engagement results relative to the engagement objectives and management’s objectives. The engagement conclusion must summarize the internal auditors’ professional judgment about the overall significance of the aggregated engagement findings. Assurance engagement conclusions must include the internal auditors’ judgment regarding the effectiveness of the governance, risk management, and/or control processes of the activity under review, including an acknowledgment of when processes are effective."* [1].
     - `[PRACTICE]` In institutional methodologies (MIT, UNOPS, Richard Chambers [35]), overall engagement conclusions use a 3-tier or 4-tier rating scale—such as **Satisfactory (Effective)**, **Partially Satisfactory / Needs Improvement (Some Improvement Needed)**, and **Unsatisfactory (Ineffective / Major Improvement Needed)**—governed by aggregation rules (e.g., an engagement cannot be rated *Satisfactory* if one or more High/Critical findings exist) [10, 35].
2. **Draft, Review, and Approve the Final Engagement Communication (`[REQ]` Standard 15.1, pp. 113–114; Standard 11.2, p. 79; Standard 11.3, p. 81)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Report Content Requirements**:
     - `[REQ]` **Standard 15.1 (Final Engagement Communication, Domain V, Principle 15, pp. 113–114)** mandates the exact required contents of every final engagement communication:
       1. **All Engagements (Assurance & Advisory)**:
          - `[REQ]` *"For each engagement, internal auditors must develop a final communication that includes the engagement’s objectives, scope, recommendations and/or action plans if applicable, and conclusions."* (p. 113) [1].
          - `[REQ]` *"The final communication must specify the individuals responsible for addressing the findings and the planned date by which the actions should be completed."* (p. 113) [1].
          - `[REQ]` *"When internal auditors become aware that management has initiated or completed actions to address a finding before the final communication, the actions must be acknowledged in the communication."* (p. 113) [1].
       2. **Additional Mandatory Content for Assurance Engagements**:
          - `[REQ]` *"The final communication for assurance engagements also must include: The findings and their significance and prioritization; An explanation of scope limitations, if any; A conclusion regarding the effectiveness of the governance, risk management, and control processes of the activity reviewed."* (p. 113) [1].
       3. **Additional Conditional Disclosures When Applicable**:
          - `[REQ]` **Nonconformance Disclosure (Standard 15.1, p. 114)**: *"If the engagement is not conducted in conformance with the Standards, the final engagement communication must disclose the following details about the nonconformance: Standard(s) with which conformance was not achieved; Reason(s) for nonconformance; Impact of nonconformance on the engagement findings and conclusions."* [1].
          - `[REQ]` **Joint Standards Citation (Standard 4.1, p. 29)**: *"If the Standards are used in conjunction with requirements issued by other authoritative bodies, internal audit communications must also cite the use of the other requirements, as appropriate."* (e.g., GAO Yellow Book, PCAOB, BCBS) [1].
          - `[REQ]` **Quality Attributes (Standard 15.1, p. 113 & Standard 11.2, p. 79)**: *"The final communication must be accurate, objective, clear, concise, constructive, complete, and timely, as described in Standard 11.2 Effective Communication."* [1].
          - `[REQ]` **CAE Pre-Issuance Review and Approval (Standard 15.1, p. 113 & Standard 11.3, p. 81)**: *"Internal auditors must ensure the final communication is reviewed and approved by the chief audit executive before it is issued."* [1].
3. **Disseminate / Distribute the Final Communication (`[REQ]` Standard 15.1, p. 113; Standard 11.3, p. 81; Standard 8.1, p. 52)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Distribution**:
     - `[REQ]` *"The chief audit executive must disseminate the final communication to parties who can ensure that the results are given due consideration."* (Standard 15.1, p. 113) [1].
     - `[GUIDE]` Under Standard 15.1 Considerations (p. 114) and Standard 8.1 (p. 52), primary recipients are **activity management** (who implement action plans), **senior management** (who oversee the business unit), and the **Board / Audit Committee** (who receive either full high-risk reports or periodic executive summaries of engagement results, conclusions, and themes) [1].
     - `[REQ]` Before distributing any final communication **outside the organization** (e.g., to external auditors, regulators, joint-venture partners, or the public), the CAE *"must seek the advice of legal counsel and/or senior management as required before releasing final communications to parties outside the organization, unless otherwise required or restricted by laws and/or regulations."* (Standard 11.3, p. 81) [1].
4. **Correct Significant Errors and Omissions in Final Communications (`[REQ]` Standard 11.4, p. 83)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Errors and Omissions in Final Communications**:
     - `[REQ]` **Standard 11.4 (Errors and Omissions, Domain IV, Principle 11, p. 83)** states verbatim:
       > *"If a final engagement communication contains a significant error or omission, the chief audit executive must communicate corrected information promptly to all parties who received the original communication. Significance is determined according to criteria agreed upon with the board."* [1].
     - `[GUIDE]` Under Standard 11.4 Considerations (p. 83), a significant error or omission is one that would change the reader's understanding of the engagement conclusion, the severity of a finding, or the appropriateness of an action plan [1].
5. **Monitor and Validate Management Actions (Follow-Up) (`[REQ]` Standard 15.2, pp. 114–115; `[LOCAL]` [18, 20]; `[PRACTICE]` [31, 32])**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Monitoring and Validating Management Actions**:
     - `[REQ]` **Standard 15.2 (Confirming the Implementation of Recommendations or Action Plans, Domain V, Principle 15, pp. 114–115)** states verbatim:
       > *"Internal auditors must confirm that management has implemented internal auditors’ recommendations or management’s action plans following an established methodology, which includes:*
       > - *Inquiring about progress on the implementation.*
       > - *Performing follow-up assessments using a risk-based approach.*
       > - *Updating the status of management’s actions in a tracking system.*
       > *The extent of these procedures must consider the significance of the finding."* [1].
     - `[GUIDE]` / `[PRACTICE]` **Risk-Based Follow-Up Verification Tiers**:
       - *Low-Significance Findings*: May be closed based on management's attestation and uploaded documentary proof in the issue portal (TeamMate+ Auditee Portal, Diligent HighBond, AuditBoard) [1, 31, 32, 33].
       - *High/Critical Findings & Regulatory MRAs/MRIAs*: Require an independent **Follow-Up Assessment / Issue Validation** where internal auditors independently re-test the control's design and operating effectiveness over a sustained post-remediation operating window (e.g., 30–90 days of transactions) before changing the issue status to `Closed / Verified` [1, 18, 20, 31, 32].
6. **Escalate Overdue Actions and Unresolved Risk Acceptance to the Board (`[REQ]` Standard 15.2, p. 115; Standard 11.5, p. 84; Standard 8.1, p. 52)**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Escalating Unresolved Risk Acceptance to the Board**:
     - `[REQ]` **Standard 15.2 (p. 115)** links overdue remediation directly to **Standard 11.5 (Communicating the Acceptance of Risks, p. 84)**:
       > *"If management has not progressed in implementing the actions according to the established completion dates, internal auditors must obtain and document an explanation from management and discuss the issue with the chief audit executive. The chief audit executive is responsible for determining whether senior management, by delay or inaction, has accepted a risk that exceeds the risk tolerance."* (Standard 15.2, p. 115) [1].
     - `[REQ]` Once the CAE determines that explicit refusal *or* chronic delay/inaction results in risk exceeding the organization's risk appetite or tolerance, the CAE **must** discuss the matter with senior management and, if unresolved, **must escalate the matter to the Board** (`[REQ]` Standard 11.5, p. 84) [1].
7. **Formulate Overall, Business-Unit, or Thematic Opinions (`[REQ]` Standard 11.3, p. 81; Standard 8.1, p. 52; `[GUIDE]` [10]; `[LOCAL]` [20, 29])**:
   - **Stage-Specific Depth — Overall or Thematic Opinions**:
     - `[REQ]` **Standard 11.3 (Communicating Results, Domain IV, Principle 11, p. 81)** distinguishes three levels of results communicated to the Board and senior management:
       1. *Engagement Conclusions* (single engagement — governed by Standard 14.5).
       2. *Themes*: `[REQ]` *"When the chief audit executive identifies themes related to the organization’s governance, risk management, and control processes, the themes must be communicated timely, along with insights, advice, and/or conclusions, to the board and senior management."* (Standard 11.3, p. 81) [1].
       3. *Conclusions at the Level of the Business Unit or Organization (Macro Opinions)*: `[REQ]` *"The chief audit executive may be required to make a conclusion at the level of the business unit or organization about the effectiveness of governance, risk management, and/or control processes, based on multiple engagements. Such a conclusion reflects the professional judgment of the chief audit executive based on multiple engagements and must be supported by relevant, reliable, and sufficient information. When communicating such a conclusion to the board or senior management, the chief audit executive must include: A summary of the request; The criteria used as a basis for the conclusion, for example a governance framework or risk and control framework; The scope, including limitations and the period to which the conclusion pertains; A summary of the information that supports the conclusion; A disclosure of reliance on the work of other assurance providers, if any."* (Standard 11.3, p. 81) [1, 10].
     - `[LOCAL]` Note that while macro opinions are optional under the baseline GIAS ("may be required"), they are **mandatory (`[LOCAL]`)** in US banking under Federal Reserve SR 13-1 (*Internal Audit Risk Opinion*) [20, 21] and in the UK Public Sector under the IASAB Application Note & CIPFA Code (*annual overall Head of Internal Audit opinion* feeding the Annual Governance Statement) [29].
   - **Loops Triggered in Stage 6**:
     - **Loop L7 (Stage 6 Post-Issuance Error Discovery → Stage 6 Amended Report Re-Issuance)**: Discovery of a significant error or omission in a final report triggers prompt issuance of corrected communications to all original recipients (`[REQ]` Standard 11.4, p. 83) [1].
     - **Loop L8 (Stage 6 Failed Follow-Up Testing / Overdue Action → Stage 4 Re-Testing / Stage 1 Audit Plan / Board Risk-Acceptance Escalation)**: If follow-up testing shows the remediated control still fails, the issue stays open with revised target dates and re-enters follow-up testing, triggers a follow-up engagement in Stage 1, or escalates to the Board as unacceptable risk acceptance (`[REQ]` Standards 15.2, 11.5, pp. 114–115, 84) [1].

#### 5. Communicate
- `[REQ]` **With Activity Management, Senior Management, and the Board**:
  - **What**: Final Engagement Communication (Audit Report) (Standard 15.1, p. 113); Corrected Communications for significant errors/omissions (Standard 11.4, p. 83); Periodic Board reporting on engagement conclusions, systemic themes, macro conclusions, follow-up monitoring status, overdue actions, and escalated risk acceptances (Standards 8.1, 11.3, 11.5, 15.2) [1].
- `[REQ]` **With Legal Counsel / Senior Management (Prior to External Distribution)**:
  - **What**: Legal/management consultation before releasing any final audit communication to external parties (Standard 11.3, p. 81) [1].

- `[REQ]` **What Must Be Documented**:
  1. Final Engagement Communication containing all mandatory Standard 15.1 elements and evidence of **pre-issuance CAE review and approval** (Standard 11.3, p. 81; Standard 15.1, p. 113) [1].
  2. Complete engagement documentation reviewed by the engagement supervisor and approved by the CAE, archived per retention policy (Standard 14.6, p. 111) [1].
  3. An active **tracking system** recording the status of management's actions, documented explanations from management for any missed completion dates, and risk-based follow-up verification workpapers (Standard 15.2, pp. 114–115) [1].
  4. Documentation of all 5 required elements whenever a macro conclusion (business-unit or organization level) is issued (Standard 11.3, p. 81) [1].

#### 8. What Varies (`[REQ]` vs. Methodology vs. Context)
- **Foundation-Fixed (`[REQ]`)**: Developing an engagement conclusion (including effectiveness judgment for assurance) (14.5); issuing a final communication with objectives, scope, findings/significance/prioritization, scope limitations, conclusions, recommendations/action plans, responsible individuals, planned completion dates, pre-report remediation acknowledgment, and nonconformance disclosures (15.1); CAE pre-issuance review and approval of the final communication and engagement documentation (11.3, 14.6, 15.1); prompt correction of significant errors/omissions to all original recipients (11.4); tracking actions in a system, performing risk-based follow-up, documenting reasons for delays, and escalating unacceptable risk acceptance by delay/inaction to senior management and the Board (11.5, 15.2) [1].
- **Methodology Choices (`[PRACTICE]` / `[GUIDE]`)**:
  - *Report Format*: 30-page narrative Word document vs. 3-page Executive Summary + tabular Finding Matrix vs. PowerPoint/dashboard report in Workiva, AuditBoard, or Diligent HighBond [1, 31, 32, 33].
  - *Overall Engagement Rating Scale*: 3-tier (*Satisfactory / Needs Improvement / Unsatisfactory*), 4-tier, or qualitative narrative conclusion without a single-word grade [10, 35].
  - *Follow-Up Retest Threshold*: Re-testing 100% of High and Medium issues vs. re-testing only High issues and accepting management self-attestation with documentary proof for Medium/Low issues [1, 31, 32].
- **Engagement Context**: Whether a macro opinion is requested or legally required, whether external distribution is needed, and whether management completes remediation on time or requests extensions.

#### 9. Common Failure Modes (EQA / QAIP Deficiencies and Reasons)
1. **`[PRACTICE]` Excessive Cycle Time Between Fieldwork Completion and Final Report Issuance**:
   - *Deficiency*: Final reports are issued 60 to 120+ days after fieldwork ends, violating the **timely** communication requirement of Standards 11.2 and 15.1 [1, 12, 13].
   - *Reason*: Bottlenecks in multi-layer report wordsmithing and prolonged back-and-forth waiting for management action plans without enforced SLA escalation [12, 13].
2. **`[PRACTICE]` Omitting Mandatory Standard 15.1 Elements (Action Owners, Target Dates, or Overall Effectiveness Conclusion)**:
   - *Deficiency*: EQA reviewers inspect final assurance reports and find that they list findings without an overall conclusion on the effectiveness of governance, risk management, and control processes (Standard 14.5/15.1), or list recommendations without named responsible individuals and target completion dates (Standard 15.1) [1, 13, 14].
   - *Reason*: Using legacy report templates that were not updated for the explicit checklist in 2024 Standard 15.1 [13, 14].
3. **`[PRACTICE]` "Check-the-Box" Follow-Up Closure Without Risk-Based Verification**:
   - *Deficiency*: Closing High-risk findings in the tracking spreadsheet based solely on an email from management saying *"Done"* without inspecting evidence or performing a risk-based follow-up assessment (violating Standard 15.2, p. 114) [1, 12, 13].
   - *Reason*: Lack of budgeted hours in the annual audit plan for Stage 6 follow-up verification testing [13].
4. **`[PRACTICE]` Allowing Repeated Due-Date Extensions Without Assessing Implicit Risk Acceptance**:
   - *Deficiency*: High-risk findings have their target dates extended 3 or 4 times over multiple years without the CAE evaluating whether management's inaction constitutes unacceptable risk acceptance requiring escalation to the Board under Standards 15.2 (p. 115) and 11.5 (p. 84) [1, 12, 13].
   - *Reason*: Treating due-date extensions as routine administrative updates rather than implicit risk acceptance [1, 13].

## Section 5: Cross-Cutting Themes (Part B)

### 5.1 Supervision
- **How It Shows Up at Every Stage**:
  - *Stage 1*: CAE oversees the risk assessment and methodology training (`[REQ]` Standard 9.3, p. 65; Standard 9.4, p. 66) [1].
  - *Stage 2*: Engagement Supervisor guides process understanding and risk scoring; CAE approves engagement objectives and scope (`[REQ]` Standard 12.3, p. 90; Standard 13.3, p. 98) [1].
  - *Stage 3*: Engagement Supervisor reviews test design and sampling plans; CAE reviews and approves the Engagement Work Program before implementation (`[REQ]` Standard 13.6, p. 103) [1].
  - *Stage 4*: Supervisor provides ongoing fieldwork guidance, issues and clears coaching/review notes, verifies work programs are complete, and reviews workpapers for accuracy, relevance, and completeness; CAE approves engagement documentation (`[REQ]` Standards 12.3, 14.6, pp. 90, 111) [1, 32].
  - *Stage 5*: Supervisor and CAE review finding significance ratings, root-cause logic, and action plan adequacy; CAE handles escalations (`[REQ]` Standards 11.5, 12.3, 14.3, pp. 84, 90, 107) [1].
  - *Stage 6*: CAE reviews and approves the final engagement communication prior to issuance and oversees follow-up tracking and post-engagement staff performance feedback (`[REQ]` Standards 11.3, 12.3, 15.1, 15.2, pp. 81, 90, 113–115) [1].
- **What Is Mandatory (`[REQ]`)**:
  - **Standard 12.3 (Domain IV, Principle 12, p. 90)**: *"The chief audit executive must establish and implement methodologies for engagement supervision, quality assurance, and the development of competencies. The chief audit executive or an engagement supervisor must provide internal auditors with guidance throughout the engagement, verify work programs are complete, and confirm engagement workpapers adequately support findings, conclusions, and recommendations. To assure quality, the chief audit executive must verify whether engagements are performed in conformance with the Standards and the internal audit function’s methodologies. To develop competencies, the chief audit executive must provide internal auditors with feedback about their performance and opportunities for improvement. The chief audit executive must ensure that evidence of supervision is documented and retained, according to the internal audit function’s established methodologies."* [1].
  - Plus mandatory CAE approvals in **Standard 13.3** (objectives/scope, p. 98), **Standard 13.6** (work program, p. 103), **Standard 14.2** (work-program adjustments, p. 106), **Standard 14.6** (documentation, p. 111), and **Standards 11.3 / 15.1** (final communication, pp. 81, 113) [1].
- **What Typically Varies (`[PRACTICE]` / `[GUIDE]`)**:
  - Formal delegation matrices (whether the CAE delegates Stage 2/3/4/6 approvals to Audit Directors or Senior Managers based on engagement risk tier while retaining overall responsibility under Standards 11.3, 12.3, 13.3, 13.6) [1]; whether coaching notes are permanently retained or purged after resolution [1, 32]; and whether post-engagement staff feedback is done per-audit or quarterly [1].
- **What Evidence a Quality Reviewer (EQA) Expects to See**:
  - Documented supervision methodology; **chronological system timestamps** proving that work-program approval occurred *before* fieldwork and that supervisory workpaper review sign-offs occurred *before* the final report issue date [11, 12, 14, 32]; resolved coaching note logs or supervisory review checklists (Standard 12.3 Examples of Evidence, p. 91) [1]; and post-engagement performance feedback records [1, 11].

### 5.2 Professional Judgment and Skepticism
- **How It Shows Up at Every Stage**:
  - *Stage 1*: Independently validating management's ERM risk register rather than accepting it at face value (`[GUIDE]` Standard 9.4, p. 67) [1].
  - *Stage 2*: Brainstorming fraud risks (`[REQ]` Standard 13.2, p. 96), challenging whether management's existing KPIs/policies are adequate evaluation criteria (`[REQ]` Standard 13.4, p. 100), and tracing walkthroughs end-to-end rather than relying on verbal descriptions [1].
  - *Stage 3*: Weighing cost vs. benefit, complexity, and fraud probability when selecting test procedures and sample sizes (`[REQ]` Standard 4.2, p. 31) [1].
  - *Stage 4*: Critically assessing the reliability of client-supplied evidence, verifying IPE population completeness, asking straightforward questions about inconsistencies, and seeking corroborating evidence (`[REQ]` Standards 4.3, 14.1, pp. 33, 104–105) [1].
  - *Stage 5*: Probing beyond superficial symptoms to root causes, judging likelihood × impact significance, and refusing to drop valid findings under management pushback (`[REQ]` Standards 1.1, 4.3, 13.1, 14.3) [1].
  - *Stage 6*: Synthesizing aggregated findings into an overall engagement or macro conclusion (`[REQ]` Standards 11.3, 14.5) and critically testing whether management's follow-up actions actually fixed the issue (`[REQ]` Standard 15.2) [1].
- **What Is Mandatory (`[REQ]`)**:
  - **Standard 4.2 Due Professional Care (Domain II, Principle 4, p. 31)**: *"Internal auditors must exercise due professional care by assessing the nature, circumstances, and requirements of the services to be provided, including: The organization’s strategy and objectives; The interests of those for whom internal audit services are provided and the interests of other stakeholders; Adequacy and effectiveness of governance, risk management, and control processes; Cost relative to potential benefits of the internal audit services to be performed; Extent and timeliness of work needed to achieve the engagement’s objectives; Relative complexity, materiality, or significance of risks to the activity under review; Probability of significant errors, fraud, noncompliance, and other risks that might affect objectives, operations, or resources; Use of appropriate techniques, tools, and technology."* [1].
  - **Standard 4.3 Professional Skepticism (Domain II, Principle 4, p. 33)**: *"Internal auditors must exercise professional skepticism when planning and performing internal audit services. To exercise professional skepticism, internal auditors must: Maintain an attitude that includes inquisitiveness; Critically assess the reliability of information; Be straightforward and honest when raising concerns and asking questions about inconsistent information; Seek additional evidence to make a judgment about information and statements that might be incomplete, inconsistent, false, or misleading."* [1].
- **What Typically Varies (`[PRACTICE]`)**:
  - Structured decision aids (e.g., mandatory IPE completeness checklists, fraud risk brainstorming templates, and multi-source corroboration rules for High-risk controls) [7, 8, 34].
- **What Evidence a Quality Reviewer (EQA) Expects to See**:
  - Workpapers that corroborate management inquiry with inspection, reperformance, or analytics (`[GUIDE]` Standard 4.3 Examples of Evidence, p. 33) [1, 14]; documented IPE/population completeness checks [8, 14]; fraud risk assessment entries in Stage 2 planning workpapers [1, 13]; and supervisor coaching notes challenging weak or conclusory evidence [11, 32].

### 5.3 Documentation
- **How It Shows Up at Every Stage**:
  - *Stage 1*: Documented Charter, Strategy, Methodologies, Annual Risk Assessment, Audit Plan, and Reliance Basis (`[REQ]` Standards 6.1, 6.2, 9.2, 9.3, 9.4, 9.5) [1].
  - *Stage 2*: Documented process understanding, engagement risk assessment, evaluation criteria, and CAE-approved objectives and scope (`[REQ]` Standards 13.2, 13.3, 13.4) [1].
  - *Stage 3*: Documented, CAE-approved Engagement Work Program and sampling plans (`[REQ]` Standard 13.6) [1].
  - *Stage 4*: Fieldwork workpapers (RWD), attached/referenced evidence, analytics logs, and supervisory review sign-offs (`[REQ]` Standards 12.3, 14.1, 14.2, 14.6) [1].
  - *Stage 5*: Documented findings (criteria, condition, root cause, effect, significance/priority), recommendations/action plans, and any disagreement positions (`[REQ]` Standards 13.1, 14.2–14.4) [1].
  - *Stage 6*: Final Engagement Communication, CAE approval, follow-up tracking system records, delay explanations, and archival retention (`[REQ]` Standards 14.6, 15.1, 15.2) [1].
- **What Is Mandatory (`[REQ]`)**:
  - **Standard 14.6 Engagement Documentation (Domain V, Principle 14, p. 111)**: *"Internal auditors must document information and evidence to support the engagement results. The analyses, evaluations, and supporting information relevant to an engagement must be documented such that an informed, prudent internal auditor, or similarly informed and competent person, could repeat the work and derive the same engagement results. Internal auditors and the engagement supervisor must review the engagement documentation for accuracy, relevance, and completeness. The chief audit executive must review and approve the engagement documentation. Internal auditors must retain engagement documentation according to relevant laws and/or regulations as well as policies and procedures of the internal audit function and the organization."* [1].
  - **Standard 5.2 Protection of Information (Domain II, Principle 5, p. 35)**: Mandatory protection of confidential/private information in workpapers and access controls over audit files [1].
  - **Standard 4.1 (p. 30) & Domain III Intro (p. 38)**: Mandatory documentation of rationale and alternative actions whenever a requirement or essential condition cannot be met [1].
- **What Typically Varies (`[PRACTICE]` / `[GUIDE]`)**:
  - Software platform (TeamMate+, Diligent HighBond, AuditBoard, Workiva) vs. SharePoint/OneDrive folders [31, 32, 33]; cross-referencing/indexing numbering schemes; retention duration (e.g., 5, 7, or 10 years per local law) [1]; and redaction/minimisation rules for PII/GDPR data [1].
- **What Evidence a Quality Reviewer (EQA) Expects to See**:
  - Complete engagement files where every report finding and conclusion traces back to reviewed workpapers (`[REQ]` Standard 12.3, p. 90; Standard 14.6, p. 111), unique sample item identifiers enabling reperformance, documented two-level sign-offs (Supervisor + CAE), and enforced retention/access-control policies (Standard 14.6 Examples of Evidence, p. 112) [1, 11, 14].

### 5.4 Communication
- **How It Shows Up at Every Stage**:
  - *Stage 1*: Charter, strategy, risk-based plan, budget, resource constraints, omitted high-risk areas, and independence confirmations communicated to the Board and senior management (`[REQ]` Standards 6.1–6.3, 7.1, 8.1, 8.2, 9.4, 10.1) [1].
  - *Stage 2*: Engagement announcement, objectives, scope, and timing communicated to management (`[REQ]` Standard 13.1, p. 93); criteria discussions (`[REQ]` Standard 13.4, p. 100); scope limitation escalations (`[REQ]` Standard 13.3, p. 98) [1].
  - *Stage 3*: Resource shortfall discussions with CAE (`[REQ]` Standard 13.5, p. 101) and PBC/information requests to management (`[PRACTICE]` [31, 32]).
  - *Stage 4*: Ongoing status updates, timely communication of scope/timing changes (`[REQ]` Standard 13.1, p. 93), straightforward inquiries (`[REQ]` Standard 4.3, p. 33), and confidential escalation of legal/regulatory violations (`[REQ]` Standard 1.3, p. 19) [1].
  - *Stage 5*: Collaborative root-cause and recommendation/action-plan discussions, exit conference, disagreement handling, and escalation of unacceptable risk acceptance (`[REQ]` Standards 11.5, 13.1, 14.3, 14.4) [1].
  - *Stage 6*: Final engagement communication, errata/corrections for significant errors, thematic and macro conclusions, and follow-up monitoring reports to the Board and senior management (`[REQ]` Standards 8.1, 11.3, 11.4, 15.1, 15.2) [1].
- **What Is Mandatory (`[REQ]`)**:
  - **Standard 1.1 Honesty and Professional Courage (p. 17)**: *"Internal auditors must be truthful, accurate, clear, open, and respectful in all professional relationships and communications... Internal auditors must not make false, misleading, or deceptive statements, nor conceal or omit findings or other pertinent information from communications. Internal auditors must disclose all material facts known to them that, if not disclosed, could affect the organization’s ability to make well-informed decisions."* [1].
  - **Standard 11.2 Effective Communication (p. 79)**: *"The chief audit executive must establish and implement methodologies to promote accurate, objective, clear, concise, constructive, complete, and timely internal audit communications."* [1].
  - Plus **Standards 8.1, 11.1, 11.3, 11.4, 11.5, 13.1, and 15.1** [1].
- **What Typically Varies (`[PRACTICE]`)**:
  - Frequency and format of interim status updates (weekly email vs. Agile daily/weekly standups [30]); Board reporting cadence (quarterly vs. monthly Audit Committee packs); and report styling [31, 33].
- **What Evidence a Quality Reviewer (EQA) Expects to See**:
  - Board/Audit Committee agendas and meeting minutes confirming required CAE disclosures (Standard 8.1, p. 53); engagement entrance and exit meeting records (Standard 13.1, p. 95); final reports meeting all Standard 15.1 content requirements; and report-cycle-time KPIs demonstrating *timely* issuance [1, 11, 13].

### 5.5 Independence and Objectivity
- **How It Shows Up at Every Stage**:
  - *Stage 1*: Organizational placement of the CAE reporting functionally to the Board; annual confirmation of organizational independence to the Board; charter documentation and 3rd-party assurance safeguards for any non-audit roles held by the CAE (`[REQ]` Standards 6.2, 7.1, 7.2, pp. 42, 46–51) [1].
  - *Stage 2*: Checking individual auditor objectivity before staffing the engagement; enforcing the **12-month cooling-off rule** for assurance over prior operational responsibilities; managing objectivity when auditing an area where IA previously provided advisory services; disclosing potential impairments before accepting advisory work (`[REQ]` Standard 2.2, pp. 22–23) [1].
  - *Stage 3*: Designing the work program free from management interference or undue influence over scope or sample selection (`[REQ]` Standards 2.2, 13.3, pp. 22, 98) [1].
  - *Stage 4*: Applying an impartial, unbiased mindset when evaluating evidence (managing self-review, familiarity, and unconscious bias) and promptly disclosing any mid-audit objectivity impairment to the CAE/supervisor (`[REQ]` Standards 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, pp. 20–24) [1].
  - *Stage 5*: Refusing to subordinate professional judgment under senior management pressure and avoiding assuming management responsibility when recommending corrective actions (`[REQ]` Standards 1.1, 2.2, 13.1, 14.4, pp. 17, 22, 93, 109) [1].
  - *Stage 6*: Disclosing and resolving any post-completion discovery of an impairment that affects the reliability or perceived reliability of engagement findings/conclusions (`[REQ]` Standard 2.3, p. 24) [1].
- **What Is Mandatory (`[REQ]`)**:
  - **Standard 2.1 Individual Objectivity (p. 20)**: *"Internal auditors must maintain professional objectivity when performing all aspects of internal audit services. Professional objectivity requires internal auditors to apply an impartial and unbiased mindset and make judgments based on balanced assessments of all relevant circumstances. Internal auditors must be aware of and manage potential biases."* [1].
  - **Standard 2.2 Safeguarding Objectivity (p. 22)**: *"Internal auditors must recognize and avoid or mitigate actual, potential, and perceived impairments to objectivity. Internal auditors must not accept any tangible or intangible item, such as a gift, reward, or favor, that may impair or be presumed to impair objectivity. Internal auditors must avoid conflicts of interest and must not be unduly influenced by their own interests or the interests of others, including senior management or others in a position of authority, or by the political environment or other aspects of their surroundings. Internal auditors must refrain from assessing specific activities for which they were previously responsible. Objectivity is presumed to be impaired if an internal auditor provides assurance services for an activity for which the internal auditor had responsibility within the previous 12 months. If the internal audit function is to provide assurance services where it had previously performed advisory services, the chief audit executive must confirm that the nature of the advisory services does not impair objectivity and must assign resources such that individual objectivity is managed. Assurance engagements for functions over which the chief audit executive has responsibility must be overseen by an independent party outside the internal audit function. If internal auditors are to provide advisory services relating to activities for which they had previous responsibilities, they must disclose potential impairments to the party requesting the services before accepting the engagement. The chief audit executive must establish methodologies to address impairments to objectivity. Internal auditors must discuss impairments and take appropriate actions according to relevant methodologies."* [1].
  - **Standard 2.3 Disclosing Impairments to Objectivity (p. 24)** & **Standard 7.1 Organizational Independence (p. 46)** (quoted in full in Section 1 / Stage 1) [1].
- **What Typically Varies (`[PRACTICE]` / `[LOCAL]`)**:
  - Extended cooling-off periods (e.g., 24 months instead of the 12-month `[REQ]` floor); whether sign-off of conflict-of-interest declarations occurs annually only or per-engagement in the software; and strict regulatory bans on CAE dual-hatting in large banks (`[LOCAL]` OCC 12 CFR Part 30 App D, BCBS 223, Fed SR 13-1 forbid the CAE from holding any 1st- or 2nd-line risk/compliance role) [16, 18, 20].
- **What Evidence a Quality Reviewer (EQA) Expects to See**:
  - Board minutes showing annual independence confirmation and functional reporting actions (CAE hiring/firing/compensation approval by the Board under Standard 7.1/7.2, pp. 46–51); signed annual and/or engagement-level Conflict of Interest & Objectivity declarations (Standard 2.2 Examples of Evidence, p. 23); staffing rotation records proving the 12-month cooling-off rule; and third-party oversight reports for any non-audit function managed by the CAE [1, 11, 12].

## Section 6: Advisory Versus Assurance (Part C)

### 6.1 Foundational Distinction in the 2024 Global Internal Audit Standards
- `[REQ]` / `[GUIDE]` Unlike the 2017 IPPF—which separated Assurance (`"A"`) and Consulting (`"C"`) implementation standards—the 2024 Standards unify both under **internal audit services** while renaming "consulting" to **advisory services** (*Glossary*, pp. 10–11) [1]:
  - **Assurance Services (*Glossary*, p. 10)**: *"Services through which internal auditors perform objective assessments to provide assurance. Examples of assurance services include compliance, financial, operational/performance, and tecnología/system engagements. Internal auditors may provide assurance on a single point in time or over a period of time."* [1].
  - **Advisory Services (*Glossary*, p. 10)**: *"Services through which internal auditors provide advice to an organization’s stakeholders without providing assurance or taking on management responsibilities. The nature and scope of advisory services are subject to agreement with relevant stakeholders. Examples include advising on the design and implementation of new policies, processes, systems, and products; providing forensic services; providing training; and facilitating discussions about risks and controls."* [1].
  - **Universal Applicability Rule (*Domain V Introduction*, p. 92)**: `[GUIDE]` *"Internal audit services involve providing assurance, advice, or both. Internal auditors are expected to apply and conform with the Standards when performing engagements, whether they are providing assurance or advice, except when otherwise specified in individual standards."* (Note: Blended engagements containing both assurance and advisory components are explicitly recognized) [1].

### 6.2 Stage-by-Stage Comparison: Assurance vs. Advisory Engagements

### 7.1 Test Case 1: Bank or Regulated Financial Institution

#### A. What Stays the Same (Foundation Layer `[REQ]`)
- The bank's internal audit function (the "Third Line") must conform to all 5 Domains, 15 Principles, and 52 Standards of the 2024 *Global Internal Audit Standards* [1], including the six-stage lifecycle (H3): Board-approved charter and risk-based plan (Stage 1), engagement risk assessment and scoping (Stage 2), CAE-approved work program (Stage 3), repeatable workpaper evidence and supervisory review (Stage 4), root-cause findings and action plans (Stage 5), and CAE-approved final reports with tracked follow-up (Stage 6) [1].

#### B. What Changes in Stages, Artefacts, Terminology, Ratings, Approvals, or Reporting (`[LOCAL]` & `[PRACTICE]`)
- **Stage 1 (Universe & Plan)**:
  - *Full-Universe Multi-Year Cycle Mandate*: Unlike unregulated corporates that may leave low-risk audit universe entities untouched indefinitely, banks are required by supervisors to cover **every activity, legal entity, and risk pillar** (credit, market, liquidity, operational, compliance/AML, ICT, model, and conduct risk) over a defined multi-year risk-based cycle (typically 3 to 4 years) [16, 17, 18, 19].
  - *Continuous Risk Monitoring*: Quarterly or continuous risk-assessment updates feeding Audit Committee plan revisions [18, 20].
- **Stage 2 & Stage 3 (Scoping & Work Design)**:
  - *Regulatory Examination & MRA/MRIA Integration*: Engagement scoping must incorporate supervisory examination letters, **Matters Requiring Attention (MRAs)**, and **Matters Requiring Immediate Attention (MRIAs)** issued by the OCC, Federal Reserve, PRA/FCA, or ECB [18, 20, 21].
  - *Model Risk Management (MRM) Audits*: When auditing quantitative models (credit scoring, stress testing/CCAR, VaR, AML transaction monitoring), internal audit evaluates the 2nd-line Model Validation team's "effective challenge" across conceptual soundness, ongoing monitoring, and outcomes analysis/back-testing [24].
- **Stage 5 & Stage 6 (Ratings, Approvals, Reporting, and Issue Validation)**:
  - *Terminology & Ratings*: Findings are mapped to regulatory severity tiers (*Critical / MRIA-equivalent*, *High / MRA-equivalent*, *Medium*, *Low*), and reports grade both **Control Environment Effectiveness** and **Management Risk Awareness / Governance** [18, 20, 21].
  - *Mandatory Formal "Issue Validation" (Closure Testing)*: In banking, Stage 6 follow-up is elevated into a formal, heavily audited sub-stage called **Issue Validation**. Before any High-risk internal audit issue or regulatory MRA/MRIA can be closed, internal audit must execute an independent validation work program—testing both **design sustainability** and **operating effectiveness over a sustained post-remediation run-rate period** (typically 60–90 days of live transactions)—and issue a formal **Issue Validation Closure Memo** to the Audit Committee and bank examiners [18, 19, 20, 21].
  - *Mandatory Macro Risk Opinion*: Internal audit must deliver an overall **Internal Audit Risk Opinion** on the adequacy of the bank's risk management framework and internal controls to the Audit Committee and supervisors [20, 21, 22].

#### C. `[LOCAL]` Requirements That Add to or Modify the Standards (Two-Source Verified, Currently in Force as of `2026-11-04`)
1. **`[LOCAL]` International (Basel Committee on Banking Supervision — BCBS 223)**:
   - **Rule & Date**: *The internal audit function in banks* (BCBS 223, published June 2012; verified currently in force `2026-11-04`), establishing 20 principles across Supervisory Expectations, Relationship with the Supervisory Authority, and Supervisory Assessment [16, 17].
   - **Modifications to GIAS**:
     - Mandates a **permanent, independent internal audit function** (Principle 1) covering **every activity and every entity of the banking group** (Principle 7: *"The scope of the internal audit function’s activities should ensure adequate coverage of matters of regulatory interest within the audit plan"*) [16, 17].
     - **Strict Outsourcing Prohibition**: Prohibits outsourcing internal audit activities to the bank's external audit firm, requires large/internationally active banks to perform core internal audit work in-house, and mandates that the CAE/Board retain ultimate responsibility for any co-sourced specialist work (Principle 14) [16, 17].
     - Mandates regular **two-way direct dialogue between Internal Audit and bank supervisors** without management presence when requested (Principles 16–18) [16, 17].
2. **`[LOCAL]` United States — Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC 12 CFR Part 30, Appendix D & *Comptroller's Handbook*)**:
   - **Rule & Date**: *OCC Guidelines Establishing Heightened Standards for Certain Large Insured National Banks, Insured Federal Savings Associations, and Insured Federal Branches* (**12 CFR Part 30, Appendix D**, published September 2014, effective November 10, 2014; asset threshold tailoring amendments proposed 2025/2026; verified currently in force `2026-11-04`) and the OCC *Comptroller's Handbook: Internal and External Audits* (2019/2023; currently in force `2026-11-04`) [18] [19].
   - **Modifications to GIAS**:
     - Legally codifies the Three Lines of Defense: **Front Line Units** (1st line), **Independent Risk Management** (2nd line), and **Internal Audit** (3rd line), prohibiting the CAE from holding any 1st- or 2nd-line risk management responsibility [18] [19].
     - Requires Internal Audit to independently audit the design and ongoing effectiveness of the bank's written **Risk Governance Framework** and verify the accuracy and reliability of risk data used to monitor adherence to **Board-approved Risk Appetite Statements** [18] [19].
     - Requires independent validation of remediated audit issues and regulatory MRAs/MRIAs prior to closure [18] [19].
3. **`[LOCAL]` United States — Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System (SR 13-1 / CA 13-1 & SR 11-7 / SR 26-2)**:
   - **Rule & Date**: Federal Reserve Supervisory Letter **SR 13-1 / CA 13-1** (*Supplemental Policy Statement on the Internal Audit Function and Its Outsourcing*, issued January 23, 2013, supplementing SR 03-5; updated 2025/2026; verified currently in force `2026-11-04`) [20] [21], and **SR 11-7 / OCC Bulletin 2011-12** (*Supervisory Guidance on Model Risk Management*, April 2011) as updated in **April 2026 by SR 26-2 / OCC Bulletin 2026-13** (verified currently in force `2026-11-04`) [24].
   - **Modifications to GIAS**:
     - SR 13-1 mandates continuous risk assessment, prohibits outsourcing internal audit to the external auditor, requires an independent **Internal Audit Risk Opinion** on the adequacy of the risk management framework across risk pillars, and requires rigorous tracking and independent validation of MRAs/MRIAs [20] [21].
     - SR 11-7 and its April 2026 update SR 26-2 require independent model validation across three pillars (*conceptual soundness, ongoing monitoring, and outcomes analysis*) and mandate 3rd-line internal audit evaluation of the overall Model Risk Management framework [24].
4. **`[LOCAL]` United Kingdom & European Union — Chartered IIA *Internal Audit Code of Practice* (2025) & EBA *Guidelines on Internal Governance* (EBA/GL/2021/05)**:
   - **Rule & Date**: The Chartered IIA UK **Internal Audit Code of Practice** (published September 2024, **effective January 2025**, unifying the prior *Financial Services Code* and private-sector code into 37 principles aligned with the 2024 GIAS; verified currently in force `2026-11-04`) and the European Banking Authority **Guidelines on Internal Governance under Directive 2013/36/EU (EBA/GL/2021/05)** (issued July 2, 2021, effective December 31, 2021; verified currently in force `2026-11-04`) [22] [23].
   - **Modifications to GIAS**:
     - Requires explicit internal audit coverage and conclusions on **capital and liquidity adequacy (ICAAP/ILAAP)**, **risk culture and conduct risk / fair treatment of customers**, **ICT and operational resilience (DORA alignment)**, and **AML/CFT controls**, with direct unrestricted access to non-executive directors and prudential/conduct supervisors (PRA, FCA, ECB) [22] [23].

### 7.2 Test Case 2: Manufacturer

#### A. What Stays the Same (Foundation Layer `[REQ]`)
- Corporate internal audit in a manufacturing group follows the same 2024 *Global Internal Audit Standards* [1] and the six-stage engagement lifecycle (H3), maintaining functional independence to the Audit Committee, executing risk-based engagement planning, documenting repeatable workpapers, and tracking management action plans to closure [1].

#### B. What Changes in Stages, Artefacts, Terminology, Ratings, Approvals, or Reporting (`[LOCAL]` & `[PRACTICE]`)
- **Dual Audit Ecosystem inside Manufacturers (Corporate IA vs. Plant Quality/EHS Auditing)**:
  - Manufacturing organizations operate **two parallel audit regimes** that must coordinate under **Standard 9.5 (Coordination and Reliance, p. 69)** [1, 26, 27]:
    1. **Corporate Internal Audit (3rd Line)**: Focuses on financial controls (SOX 404 / ICFR), ERP systems (SAP/Oracle), plant operational/inventory audits, capital expenditure (CapEx), procurement, and anti-bribery/supply-chain compliance [25, 27].
    2. **Quality Management System (QMS), Supplier & EHS Auditing (2nd Line)**: Conducted by Certified Quality Auditors and EHS specialists at the plant and supplier level under **ISO 19011:2018**, **ISO 9001:2015**, **IATF 16949** (automotive), and **ISO 14001 / 45001** [26].
- **Stage 2 & Stage 4 (Shop-Floor Operational & Inventory Walkthroughs)**:
  - *Physical-to-ERP Walkthroughs*: In Stage 2 and Stage 4, manufacturing internal auditors perform **"cradle-to-grave" vertical walkthroughs** on the plant floor—tracing a raw material batch from receiving dock inspection → **Bill of Materials (BOM)** configuration (verifying Engineering BOM vs. Manufacturing BOM alignment and authorized **Engineering Change Orders [ECOs]**) → Work-in-Process (WIP) routing and backflushing → scrap/rework recording → finished-goods warehouse **physical-to-book cycle counts** → **standard cost variance and material usage variance** accounting [27].
- **Stage 3 & Stage 4 (SOX 404 External Auditor Reliance Rules)**:
  - For public manufacturers, Stage 3 sample sizes and Stage 4 workpaper templates (separating Test of Design [TOD] and Test of Operating Effectiveness [TOE] across interim and roll-forward periods in AuditBoard/SOXHub or Workiva) are tightly synchronized with the external auditor's methodology so the external auditor can rely on internal audit's testing under **PCAOB AS 2605** [25, 33, 34].
- **Stage 5 & Stage 6 (Terminology, Root Cause, and CAPA Workflows)**:
  - *ICFR Terminology (Corporate IA)*: Deficiencies in financial reporting controls are classified strictly as **Control Deficiency**, **Significant Deficiency**, or **Material Weakness** (which must be disclosed publicly in the company's Form 10-K if un-remediated at fiscal year-end) [25].
  - *ISO / Plant Terminology (QMS/EHS)*: Findings are termed **Major Non-Conformance Reports (Major NCRs)** (systemic breakdown or shipment of nonconforming product), **Minor NCRs** (isolated lapse), and **Opportunities for Improvement (OFIs)** [26].
  - *Structured RCA & CAPA*: Manufacturing audit workflows mandate formal **Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA)** records using engineering root-cause methodologies—**8D (Eight Disciplines) Problem Solving**, **5-Whys**, **Ishikawa (Fishbone) diagrams**, and **Poka-Yoke (mistake-proofing)**—followed by a mandatory **CAPA Effectiveness Verification Check** after production runs [26, 27].

#### C. `[LOCAL]` & Industry-Standard Requirements (Two-Source Verified, Currently in Force as of `2026-11-04`)
1. **`[LOCAL]` United States (Public Companies) — Sarbanes-Oxley Act Section 404 & PCAOB Auditing Standards AS 2201, AS 2605, and AS 2401**:
   - **Rule & Date**: **PCAOB AS 2201** (*An Audit of Internal Control Over Financial Reporting*), **PCAOB AS 2605** (*Consideration of the Internal Audit Function*, codified Dec 2016; verified currently in force `2026-11-04`), and **PCAOB AS 2401** (*Consideration of Fraud in a Financial Statement Audit*) [25].
   - **Modifications to GIAS**:
     - Under **AS 2605**, external auditors evaluate corporate internal audit's competence and objectivity (organizational reporting to the Audit Committee) and **reperform a sample of internal audit's ICFR walkthroughs and control tests** as a condition of relying on internal audit's work or using internal auditors for direct assistance on lower-risk controls [25].
     - Forces internal audit's Stage 3 sample sizes (e.g., 25–40 for daily controls), IPE completeness documentation, and Stage 5 deficiency aggregation (Material Weakness vs. Significant Deficiency) to conform to PCAOB standards [25, 34].
2. **`[GUIDE]` / `[PRACTICE]` International — ISO 19011:2018, ISO 9001:2015 (Clause 9.2 & Clause 10.2), and IATF 16949**:
   - **Rule & Date**: **ISO 19011:2018** (*Guidelines for auditing management systems*), **ISO 9001:2015** (*Quality management systems — Requirements*, Clause 9.2 Internal Audit & Clause 10.2 Nonconformity and corrective action), and **IATF 16949** (automotive quality management standard; verified currently in force `2026-11-04`) [26].
   - **Modifications to GIAS**:
     - Requires plant and supplier audits to verify conformity against process turtle diagrams, customer-specific requirements, FMEAs (Failure Mode and Effects Analysis), and Control Plans, and mandates closed-loop CAPA root-cause elimination and effectiveness verification before a nonconformity can be closed [26].

### 7.3 Test Case 3: Public-Sector Body

#### A. What Stays the Same (Foundation Layer `[REQ]`)
- Public-sector internal auditors must conform to the 2024 *Global Internal Audit Standards*, which include a dedicated section (**"Applying the Global Internal Audit Standards in the Public Sector"**, pp. 116–119) embedded directly in the Standards [1].

#### B. What Changes in Stages, Artefacts, Terminology, Ratings, Approvals, or Reporting (`[REQ]`, `[LOCAL]` & `[PRACTICE]`)
- **Governance & Mandate (Stage 1 — GIAS Public Sector Section, pp. 116–119)**:
  - `[GUIDE]` The internal audit mandate, charter, and organizational placement are frequently established by **statute, legislation, or ministerial/constitutional decree** rather than a private corporate charter, and the "Board" may be an elected legislative body, a statutory oversight committee, a ministry, or an appointed Audit and Risk Assurance Committee (pp. 116–117) [1, 28, 29].
  - `[REQ]` Under **Standard 4.1 (p. 29)** and **Standard 6.1 (p. 39)**: Where laws or regulations prescribe the mandate or require joint compliance with government auditing standards (such as the US GAO *Yellow Book* or INTOSAI *ISSAI*), the charter must include the legal requirements and audit reports must cite both the IIA Standards and the government auditing standards [1, 28, 29].
- **Stage 2 & Stage 3 (Value for Money / "The 3 Es" & Appropriations Compliance)**:
  - Public-sector performance/operational engagements evaluate **Value for Money (VfM)** across the **"3 Es"**: **Economy** (minimizing cost of inputs), **Efficiency** (maximizing output per unit of input), and **Effectiveness** (achieving intended public policy outcomes and equity), alongside strict compliance with legislative appropriations, grant conditions, and public procurement laws [28, 29].
- **Stage 6 (Public Transparency / Freedom of Information vs. Confidentiality & Mandatory Annual Macro Opinion)**:
  - *Public Disclosure Override*: Under **Standard 5.2 (p. 35)**, **Standard 11.3 (p. 81)**, and *Applying the GIAS in the Public Sector* (p. 119), unlike private companies where audit reports are strictly confidential, public-sector audit reports are often **public records** subject to Freedom of Information (FOI) or sunshine laws and must be published to legislative bodies or citizens unless redacted for national security or personal privacy [1, 28, 29].
  - *Mandatory Annual Overall Opinion (UK Public Sector)*: In the UK public sector, the Chief Audit Executive ("Head of Internal Audit") must issue a formal **annual overall opinion** on the organization's overall adequacy and effectiveness of governance, risk management, and control to support the statutory Annual Governance Statement [29].

#### C. `[LOCAL]` Requirements That Add to or Modify the Standards (Two-Source Verified, Currently in Force as of `2026-11-04`)
1. **`[LOCAL]` United States (Federal, State, and Local Government & Grant Recipients) — US GAO *Government Auditing Standards* (2024 Revision "Yellow Book", GAO-24-106786) & OMB Circular A-123 / GAO Green Book**:
   - **Rule & Date**: U.S. Government Accountability Office, *Government Auditing Standards: 2024 Revision* (**GAO-24-106786**, published February 2024, **effective for audits beginning on or after December 15, 2025**; verified currently in force `2026-11-04`), alongside **OMB Circular No. A-123** (*Management's Responsibility for Enterprise Risk Management and Internal Control*) and the **GAO Green Book** (*Standards for Internal Control in the Federal Government*, 17 principles; currently in force `2026-11-04`) [28].
   - **Modifications to GIAS**:
     - The 2024 Yellow Book replaces traditional quality control with a proactive, risk-based **System of Quality Management (SOQM)** and requires a triennial **External Peer Review** (**every 3 years**, stricter than the IIA's 5-year EQA requirement in Standard 8.4) [1, 28].
     - Prescribes specific Yellow Book GAGAS compliance statements in every audit report, strict independence rules for non-audit services, 80 hours of CPE every 2 years (including 24 hours directly related to government auditing), and mandatory reporting of fraud, noncompliance with provisions of laws/regulations/contracts/grant agreements, and **abuse/waste** of public funds [28].
     - Under OMB Circular A-123, federal management must assess internal controls against the 17 principles of the GAO Green Book [28].
2. **`[LOCAL]` United Kingdom (Central & Local Government, NHS) — IASAB *Application Note: Global Internal Audit Standards in the UK Public Sector* & CIPFA *Code of Practice* (Effective April 1, 2025)**:
   - **Rule & Date**: UK Internal Audit Standards Advisory Board (IASAB) / HM Treasury, **Application Note: Global Internal Audit Standards in the UK Public Sector** (published December 2024, **effective 1 April 2025**, replacing the 2013 PSIAS; verified currently in force `2026-11-04`) and **CIPFA's Code of Practice for the Governance of Internal Audit in UK Local Government** (**effective 1 April 2025**; verified currently in force `2026-11-04`) [29].
   - **Modifications to GIAS**:
     - Adapts GIAS Domain III for UK public-sector governance structures (Accounting Officers, Section 151 Officers, and Audit Committees) [29].
     - Mandates that the CAE hold a professional qualification (CMIIA, CCAB, or equivalent) [29].
     - Elevates **Standard 11.3's** optional organization-level conclusion into a **mandatory annual overall Head of Internal Audit opinion** on the framework of governance, risk management, and control, which must be published as part of the body's statutory Annual Governance Statement [29].
     - Adds explicit public-interest duties covering Value for Money (Economy, Efficiency, Effectiveness) and Seven Principles of Public Life (Nolan Principles) ethics [29].

### 7.4 Test Case 4: Technology / SaaS Company

#### A. What Stays the Same (Foundation Layer `[REQ]`)
- A cloud-native Technology / SaaS company's internal audit function must still satisfy all 5 Domains, 15 Principles, and 52 Standards of the 2024 *Global Internal Audit Standards* [1], including CAE/Board governance, objectivity safeguards, documented objectives/scope/criteria, CAE-approved work programs, repeatable evidence, and tracked remediation [1].

#### B. What Changes in Stages, Artefacts, Terminology, Ratings, Approvals, or Reporting (`[TOPICAL]` & `[PRACTICE]`)
- **Stage 1 (Dynamic Agile Backlogs Replacing Static Annual Waterfall Plans)**:
  - Because SaaS engineering teams deploy code continuously and product architectures shift quarterly, SaaS internal audit functions replace rigid 12-month waterfall schedules with an **Agile Internal Audit Backlog** prioritized quarterly or monthly with the Audit Committee and CISO/CTO [30].
- **Stage 2 & Stage 3 ("Test Once, Satisfy Many" Unified Control Mapping & Sprint Scoping)**:
  - Engagements are scoped into **1-to-4-week time-boxed Agile Sprints** with a formal **"Definition of Ready"** (data access and system owners confirmed before the sprint starts) and **"Definition of Done"** (workpapers reviewed and sprint findings validated before sprint close) [30].
  - Evaluation criteria and RCMs are structured around a **Unified Common Control Framework ("Test Once, Satisfy Many")** in platforms like Vanta, Drata, Hyperproof, or AuditBoard—mapping a single technical control simultaneously to **AICPA SOC 2 (Trust Services Criteria: Security, Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, Privacy)**, **ISO/IEC 27001:2022**, **NIST CSF 2.0**, **IIA Cybersecurity Topical Requirement**, and **SOX ITGCs** [3, 5, 30, 33].
- **Stage 4 (API-Driven Continuous Evidence vs. Point-in-Time Screenshots)**:
  - Traditional manual PBC sample requests for screenshots are largely replaced by **automated API integrations and CI/CD pipeline evidence**: pulling 100% population JSON/log evidence directly from AWS/Azure/GCP cloud configuration monitors, GitHub/GitLab pull-request merge approvals (verifying branch protection and peer code review before production deployment), Okta/SSO identity access logs, Jira change tickets, and endpoint encryption telemetry [30].
- **Stage 5 & Stage 6 (Engineering Ticket Integration & Continuous Sprint Reporting)**:
  - Rather than tracking findings in isolated spreadsheets, audit issues and action plans are bi-directionally synced with engineering issue trackers (**Jira / Linear / ServiceNow** tickets with severity SLAs, e.g., Critical = 7 days, High = 30 days), and sprint summaries are delivered at the end of each 2-week sprint rather than waiting months for a monolithic waterfall report [30, 33].

## Section 10: Contradictions, Open Questions, and Staleness Map

### 10.1 Unresolved Contradictions & Tensions in Evidence (Reported Plainly Without Silent Resolution)

1. **Contradiction 1 — "Useful" Information (2017 IPPF vs. 2024 Standards vs. Practitioner Literature)**:
   - *The Tension*: The user's prompt (and current IIA Practice Guides published between 2020 and 2023 [7, 8, 10]) refers to the four-prong evidence standard **"sufficient, reliable, relevant, and useful"** (from 2017 IPPF Standard 2310). However, in the **2024 *Global Internal Audit Standards* (Standard 14.1, pp. 104–105)**, the mandatory `[REQ]` text states only three prongs: **"relevant, reliable, and sufficient"** [1].
   - *Why It Occurred*: The IIA folded the meaning of "useful" into the 2024 definition of **Relevant** (*"consistent with engagement objectives, within the scope of the engagement, and contributes to the development of engagement results"*, p. 104) [1]. Software designers should label evidence evaluation around the 2024 three-prong `[REQ]` standard (*Relevant, Reliable, Sufficient*) while noting in tooltips that *Relevant* encompasses usefulness.
2. **Contradiction 2 — Retaining vs. Purging Supervisory Coaching/Review Notes (Standard 12.3 vs. Firm/Legal Practice)**:
   - *The Tension*: Under **Standard 12.3 (p. 90)**, `[REQ]` *"The chief audit executive must ensure that evidence of supervision is documented and retained, according to the internal audit function’s established methodologies."* In WIPO's documented TeamMate+ workflow [32], all coaching notes, preparer responses, and closure timestamps are **permanently retained** in the audit database as proof of supervision. Conversely, in many corporate and Big 4 SOX methodologies (and recognized in Standard 12.3 Examples of Evidence, p. 91 [1]), routine coaching notes are **cleared/deleted** once the workpaper is revised and signed off by the supervisor, so that unrefined preliminary questions cannot be subpoenaed out of context in litigation—leaving the supervisor's sign-off timestamp and review checklist as the retained evidence of supervision [1, 32].
   - *Software Implication*: Audit software must make **Coaching Note Retention Policy** a configurable tenant-level setting (`Retain full thread history after closure` vs. `Purge note text upon supervisor sign-off while preserving sign-off audit log`).
3. **Contradiction 3 — Linear RCM Control Testing (H1) vs. Direct Substantive & Agile Workflows (H3)**:
   - *The Tension*: Audit software built exclusively around a rigid `Objective → Risk → Control → Test` hierarchy (such as SOX-only modules) forces auditors to invent artificial "pseudo-controls" when performing direct substantive data analytics, fraud/management-override investigations (PCAOB AS 2401 [25]), or advisory reviews [31]. Conversely, Diligent HighBond resolves this by offering both an *Internal Control Workflow* and a non-RCM *Workplan Workflow* [31].
4. **Contradiction 4 — "Three Lines Model" (IIA 2020/2024) vs. "Three Lines of Defense" (Banking Supervisors)**:
   - *The Tension*: In July 2020, The IIA officially retired the phrase *"Three Lines of Defense"* in favor of the **"Three Lines Model"** to emphasize value creation and collaboration rather than purely defensive silos [6]. However, binding banking regulations—including **BCBS 223** [16], **US OCC 12 CFR Part 30 Appendix D** [18], and **EBA/GL/2021/05** [22]—still legally codify the **"Three Lines of Defense"** with strict structural separation between the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd lines.
