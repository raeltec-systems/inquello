# Research Brief: Internal Audit Methodology and Practice

Role and purpose: Senior internal-audit methodologist and researcher. Designing software that helps internal auditors carry out sound internal audit work. Need a sourced, practitioner-accurate map of how internal audit work is actually done: what any internal auditor must understand, decide, do and communicate at each stage, and which parts legitimately vary between organisations.

Primary source: 2024 Global Internal Audit Standards (The IIA, published January 9, 2024).

Hypotheses to test:
- H1: Judgment chain (business objective -> risks -> controls and criteria -> testing -> evidence -> findings and conclusion -> management action -> follow-up)
- H2: Iteration (work is iterative, not linear; p. 92 and other passages)
- H3: Six stages (1. deciding what to audit, 2. understanding and planning an engagement, 3. designing the work, 4. performing the work, 5. forming and discussing findings, 6. reporting and follow-up) + cross-cutting themes (supervision, professional judgment, documentation, communication, independence/objectivity)
- H4: Three layers (shared professional foundation [REQ], configurable methodology, engagement context)

Required sections:
1. How the Standards are organised, and how mandatory and non-mandatory material is distinguished, with citations.
2. Verdicts on H1–H4.
3. Stage-by-stage findings (Part A) across 9 headings per stage.
4. Layer table for each stage (Element | Layer | Classification | Varies how? | Source).
5. Cross-cutting themes (Part B).
6. Advisory versus assurance (Part C).
7. Industry test cases (Part D: Bank, Manufacturer, Public-sector body, Technology/SaaS).
8. Iteration map.
9. Glossary (Part E: 40–60 terms).
10. Contradictions and open questions.
11. Source list.
