# Contradictions and corrections

This log compares assertions, not the reputations of report-producing tools. The primary-authority digest adjudicates the exact statements below. Unchecked practice or jurisdiction claims remain unverified. Source IDs such as R1-21 resolve in the corresponding per-import digest; page/section references are to the preserved import.

| Issue | Conflicting or overbroad import statements | Adjudication and source | Consequence for later specification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Six stages versus three cycles | R1-05 and report 3 describe six stages; R2-02 describes coverage, engagement and management-action cycles. | Compatible levels of analytical grouping, not competing mandatory taxonomies. GIAS Domain V p. 92 permits overlap and iteration. See GIAS-ITERATION. | Preserve the complete work and feedback relationships; do not impose a rigid six-step state machine merely from the report headings. |
| Three layers versus four | R2-08 uses foundation/methodology/context; report 4 executive summary adds external regulatory/topical overlay as a fourth architectural layer. | Different analytical models. External applicability must be represented correctly, but GIAS does not prescribe software layers. See GIAS-9.3, HARD-SOFTWARE-GATES/ARCHITECTURE. | Distinguish applicable obligations from configurable methods without presenting one storage/configuration architecture as mandated. |
| Personal CAE approval gates | Report 4 executive summary demands digital CAE gates; its Stage 2 and section 5.1 acknowledge delegation. R2-12/25/46 preserve delegation. | Approval obligations/timing are real; qualified delegation and retained accountability are explicit in GIAS 12.3 p. 90 and Domain V p. 92. Personal digital clicks are not mandated. | Model accountable authority and permitted delegation; choose enforcement/UI through design, not invented standards text. |
| Universal RCM | Some imported work chains imply every engagement requires an RCM; R2-29 and additional report qualify it. | RCM is an optional technique in 13.2 Considerations pp. 96–97; work-program content 13.6 is required. See UNIVERSAL-RCM. | Support required reasoning/criteria/tasks without forcing one artefact on all audit/advisory work. |
| Fixed sample numbers | R1-24 asserts often 5–20%; report 4 executive summary/industry discussion alternates 25–40 practitioner norms and PCAOB mandate. | No fixed number or percentage established by GIAS 13.6/14.6. Underlying practice/sector assertions remain unverified without exact authority and applicable assumptions. | Do not hard-code those figures or use them as acceptance criteria. Sampling method and inference limits need context. |
| Mandatory five Cs/root cause | R1-33 makes all elements and root cause mandatory; R2-38/41 distinguishes feasibility and recommendation/action alternatives. | 14.3 p. 107 says root causes identified when possible; p. 108 template examples are optional. 14.4 p. 109 allows recommendation/action routes. | Preserve substantive findings analysis, uncertainty and action responsibility; do not require a known cause or identical five-field template in every case. |
| Work-program locator | R1-21 cites 13.1; R2-28 cites 13.6. | Correct source is 13.6 p. 103. 13.1 governs communication. See R1-21 correction. | Keep the real content/prior-approval obligation with the correct citation. |
| Coordination optionality | R1-13 calls coordination with other assurance optional; R2-13 says coordination/reliance needs evaluation. | 9.5 p. 69 requires coordination and consideration of reliance; actual reliance is conditional and documented when used. | Do not omit coordination obligations or force reliance on every provider. |
| Prior advisory versus prior responsibility | Report 3 p. 61 applies 12-month cooling-off to prior advice; p. 63 treats prior advisory under objectivity assessment. | 2.2 p. 22's 12-month presumption concerns assurance over prior responsibility. Previous advisory is assessed separately by CAE for objectivity and staffing. | Preserve role/context distinctions rather than blanket bans following advisory engagements. |
| Essential Conditions | Additional report L 153 labels conditions GUIDE while L 14/L 35 places them with mandatory material. | GIAS pp. 37–38 distinguishes CAE requirements and board/management Essential Conditions, including discussion, consequences and documented alternative arrangements. See GIAS-DOMAINIII-CONDITIONS. | Identify actor and obligation; neither mark all optional nor translate every board condition into an unconditional auditor action. |
| Board strategy/assurance-map approval | Report 3 p. 10 suggests approval of strategy/assurance map while p. 14 distinguishes strategy review. | Board approval of audit plan/significant changes is verified under 9.4. Any broader strategy/assurance-map approval assertion remains unverified here and must not inherit that status. | Keep plan approval distinct from other governance activities; verify the exact 9.2/charter obligation before reliance. |
| Model-risk guidance as agentic-AI mandate | Report 4 regulatory assertions cite 2026 model-risk guidance while proposing software controls. | SR 26-2/OCC 2026-13 exist, issued 2026-04-17. Joint guidance excludes generative/agentic AI and is nonprescriptive/nonenforceable as guidance; other law/governance still applies. See FED-SR 26-2 and OCC-2026-13. | Do not use this guidance as direct binding authority for a universal agentic-AI architecture or claim exemption from all governance. |
| Topical effective/applicability claims | Reports mix issued/effective/current/future topics and general mandatory wording. | IIA index checked 2026-11-05: Cybersecurity effective 2026-02-05; Third-Party 2026-09-15; Organizational Behavior 2026-12-15; Resilience 2027-04-30. Risk-assessed assurance applicability; advisory recommended. | Do not apply every topic to every engagement or future-effective requirements as current. Detailed topic mapping remains open. |
| “Common” failures and QA percentages | R1-14/20/25/30/36/41 infer common failures; report 4/additional report use older EQA datasets. | A standard establishes an obligation, not prevalence. No current representative empirical estimate was independently verified. | Keep risks and historical examples separate from measured incidence, product metrics or acceptance thresholds. |
| Report issue, action completion and risk acceptance | R2-49–53 distinguishes these; other summaries collapse completion/closure. | 15.2 pp. 114–115 requires risk-based implementation confirmation; 11.5 p. 84 separates management risk acceptance and CAE escalation. No universal software closure taxonomy follows. | Retain separate meaning and evidence for milestones; an overdue action is not automatically excessive accepted risk. |
