# Internal audit work: standards-grounded synthesis

## Executive summary
Use a connected, iterative work map: organisational risk-based coverage planning; engagements from understanding and work design through evidence, findings, conclusions and reporting; and management-action follow-up that informs later planning. The reports' six stages and three cycles describe different levels of this map. They are useful analytical groupings, not IIA-mandated software steps. [1][7][8][9][10][11]

The strongest verified foundation is the distinction between required professional outcomes and optional ways of documenting them. The Global Internal Audit Standards (GIAS) require relevant risk assessment, suitable criteria and documented work programmes, sufficient evidence, appropriate supervision/approval, supported communication and risk-based follow-up. They do not mandate universal risk and control matrices (RCMs), a five-Cs template, fixed sample counts, personal chief audit executive (CAE) button presses or a particular software architecture. [1]

All five reports have been extracted and reconciled. This evidence synthesis is not a product specification or implementation approval. Primary checks verify the bounded claims below; they do not validate every imported assertion, current practice prevalence or any organisation's legal compliance. The user confirmed the fifth report was produced by Claude; its production date remains unknown. Its original remains in Space, referenced rather than downloaded. [7][8][9][10][11]

## 1. Authority, terminology and applicability
- **[REQ] Professional obligations:** GIAS Requirements use mandatory language; implementation considerations describe common/preferred approaches, and evidence examples are not mandatory or exhaustive templates. The 2024 Standards were issued 9 January 2024 and became effective 9 January 2025. [1, pp. 7–8,12–13][2]
- **[REQ with actor/context qualifications] Governance:** Domain III identifies CAE requirements and board/senior-management Essential Conditions. Discussion, consequences of absence and documented alternative arrangements matter; treating all conditions as optional guidance or every board activity as an unconditional auditor action is inaccurate. [1, pp. 37–38]
- **[INFERENCE] Separate obligation from method:** Distinguish the professional foundation, applicable external/topical obligations, the function's methodology and engagement context. This preserves meaningful distinctions without asserting that a three- or four-layer software architecture is required. [1, pp. 65–66][8][10][11]
- **[REQ with exceptions] Assurance and advice:** Advisory work remains subject to applicable standards, but individual clauses permit stakeholder-agreed differences in formal risk assessment, evaluation criteria and evidence for findings. Do not apply the entire assurance pattern indiscriminately or treat advice as exempt from professional duties. [1, pp. 92,95,100,106,110,113]

## 2. The work map and decision points
### Coverage planning
**[REQ]** The audit plan supports organisational objectives and draws on a documented assessment of strategies, objectives and risks, informed by board/senior-management input. That assessment occurs at least annually; the plan is dynamic. Resources, omitted high-risk coverage, competing demands and access/scope restrictions must be addressed and communicated. Board approval applies to the plan and significant changes. A named audit-universe or heat-map artefact is an optional implementation approach, not a universal output mandate. [1, Standard 9.4, pp. 66–69]

**[REQ]** Coordination with other assurance providers and consideration of reliance are required. Actual reliance is conditional; when used its basis must be documented and responsibility for conclusions remains with internal audit. [1, Standard 9.5, pp. 69–71]

### Engagement understanding and work design
**[REQ]** Understand the activity, assess relevant risks including fraud, document objectives/scope, identify suitable criteria and resources, and create a documented work programme. The programme identifies criteria, tasks, methods/tools and assigned auditors. Its approval precedes implementation; changes require prompt approval. Objectives/scope can change as understanding changes, with the applicable approval and communication. [1, Standards 13.1–13.6, pp. 93–104]

**[GUIDE/INFERENCE]** Walkthroughs, planning memos, RCMs, data requests and sampling plans are ways to organise this work, not interchangeable proof of its adequacy. Sampling requires a defensible method and inference limits; neither a universal percentage nor a fixed 25/40 count is established by the checked GIAS text. [1, Standards 13.2,13.6,14.6][7][8][10]

### Work, evidence and findings
**[REQ]** Information must be relevant, reliable and sufficient. Inadequacy calls for further collection or consideration of its impact; an apparent difference between condition and criteria needs evaluation before becoming a supported finding. Further analysis can require a revised work programme. [1, Standards 14.1–14.2, pp. 104–107]

**[REQ]** Evaluate findings' significance, consider likelihood/impact and prioritise under methodology. Root-cause identification is qualified by “when possible.” Recommendations, requested management plans and collaboratively agreed actions are alternative routes; a known cause and identical five-field template are not unconditional prerequisites. [1, Standards 14.3–14.4, pp. 107–110]

### Conclusions, communication and follow-up
**[REQ]** Conclusions address engagement objectives and aggregate significance. Assurance conclusions additionally address governance, risk-management and control effectiveness. Final communication has specified content, qualities and approval, with assurance-specific additions and appropriate recipients. This is not a universal report layout or a requirement that every engagement report go to the board. [1, Standards 11.2,14.5,15.1, pp. 79,110–114]

**[REQ]** Follow-up confirms implementation through an established methodology including progress inquiries, risk-based assessment and tracking-status updates. Missed dates require explanation and discussion. Management's risk acceptance and CAE escalation for exposure beyond appetite/tolerance are separate from confirmation that a corrective action was implemented. No universal reminder frequency or closed-state taxonomy follows. [1, Standards 11.5,15.2, pp. 84–85,114–115]

## 3. Cross-cutting requirements and meaningful variation
- **Iteration and supervision:** Work may overlap and iterate. Appropriate qualified delegation is permitted, with ultimate CAE accountability retained. Documented supervision and actual approval obligations remain; this does not prescribe a personal digital click or a specific blocking screen. [1, Standard 12.3 and Domain V, pp. 90–92]
- **Evidence and documentation:** Work must be recorded sufficiently for an informed, prudent and competent person to repeat it and reach the same results. Review and retention obligations apply. Templates/software can help, but the standard does not choose a database or record-authority architecture. [1, Standards 14.1,14.6, pp. 104,111–112]
- **Skepticism, information protection and objectivity:** Critically assess reliability and inconsistencies; comply with applicable confidentiality/privacy/security duties; recognise and manage objectivity impairments. The 12-month presumption for assurance concerns prior responsibility, not a blanket prohibition following advisory services. [1, Standards 2.2,4.3,5.1–5.2, pp. 22,33–35]
- **Variation:** Formats, rating scales, review arrangements, tools and procedure depth vary within professional obligations and applicable local requirements. Imported sector examples and one institution's follow-up cadence are not universal practice evidence. [1, Standards 9.3,12.3,15.2][8][9][10][11]

## 4. Current topical and regulatory checks
**[TOPICAL]** IIA's current index lists Cybersecurity effective 5 February 2026 and Third-Party effective 15 September 2026; Organizational Behavior 15 December 2026 and Organizational Resilience 30 April 2027 are future-effective as of this review. The requirements apply to risk-assessed assurance work on the topic, including topics identified during another engagement or unplanned work. Advisory application is recommended, not required. Assess and document relevant requirements/exclusions; do not apply every topic to every engagement. Detailed topical documents have not all been mapped. [3]

**[LOCAL / supervisory guidance, not universal law]** SR 26-2 and OCC Bulletin 2026-13 were issued 17 April 2026, updating prior model-risk guidance. Their joint guidance excludes generative and agentic AI and is nonprescriptive/nonenforceable as guidance. It therefore cannot directly justify a binding universal agentic-AI architecture. The exclusion is not a blanket exemption from governance, applicable law or supervisory consequences for unsafe practices. Relevant regulated entities and activities must still be established. [4][5][6]

## 5. Contrary evidence and corrections
The main contradictions are substantive, not merely differences in labels:
- Report 1 incorrectly cites work-program duties to 13.1 and calls coordination optional; 13.6 and 9.5 supply the correct obligations. [1][7]
- Reports 1/4 sometimes convert conditional findings analysis, templates and practice into universal RCM/five-Cs/root-cause or software-gate mandates. The primary wording does not support those extensions. [1][7][10]
- Report 3's blanket prior-advisory cooling-off conflicts with its later qualification and with 2.2's distinct prior-responsibility provision. [1][9]
- Report 4's sampling norms, external quality assessment (EQA) rankings and adoption percentages lack sufficiently resolved, current evidence for decision use. Standards text cannot establish failure frequency. [10][11]
- The additional report improves authority labelling but still mixes some Essential Conditions, historical statistics and local/guidance claims. It is an input to verify, not a replacement primary authority. [1][11]

## 6. Recommendations for downstream planning
1. **Product requirements document (PRD) workflow/vocabulary input, high confidence in primary foundation:** Describe the complete iterative work and distinguish objectives, risks, criteria, work, evidence, potential/final findings, conclusions, management actions and follow-up. The three-cycle/six-stage arrangement itself remains a design aid. [1][8]
2. **PRD responsibility/approval input, high confidence with contextual limits:** Preserve actual professional authority, timing, delegation and evidence requirements; select software transitions only after methodology and applicable governance are understood. [1]
3. **Architecture input, inference rather than mandate:** Support traceable work and correction across stages, explicit limits and configurable methods. Decide records, schemas, enforcement and retrieval architecture separately; imported research cannot choose them by declaring them “mandatory.” [1][8][10][11]
4. **Validation input:** Use appropriately authorised practitioner examples to test whether this map reflects real work and variation. The current reports do not establish task prevalence, time savings, customer acceptance or willingness to pay. [8][10][11]
5. **Applicability input:** Select the actual organisation/jurisdiction and relevant topical coverage before treating sector assertions as obligations. The checked 2026 model-risk guidance is not a direct agentic-AI mandate. [3][4][5][6]
