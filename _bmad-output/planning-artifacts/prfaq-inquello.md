## Stage 1: Ignition

### Who is the customer?
Confirmed on 5 November 2026: an internal audit team, initially an auditor conducting engagements with supervisors reviewing the work. The auditor's daily experience determines whether the product works. The CAE is a tentative buyer hypothesis, not a validated purchasing decision.

### What concrete problem do they have?
Confirmed by the owner during the 5 November 2026 voice discussion. Agreed wording:

Internal audit teams repeatedly have to reconstruct the context needed to do their work. Strategy, objectives, risk information, past findings, actions, and evidence are scattered across documents, systems, and people rather than connected and reusable. Across planning, field work, reporting, and follow-up, they spend substantial effort finding, reconciling, analyzing, and documenting that information. Context gets lost between stages and reviewers, creating repeated work and making it harder to connect tests and findings back to what matters to the business. The result is less capacity to investigate significant risks, reach well-supported conclusions, and deliver timely assurance and insight that help the organization achieve its objectives.

### Why does this matter to them?
Confirmed within the agreed problem statement: less capacity to investigate significant risks, reach well-supported conclusions, and deliver timely assurance and insight that help the organization achieve its objectives.

The owner confirmed the priority order during the 5 November 2026 voice discussion:
1. Less coverage of significant risks.
2. Quality and relevance of the conclusions.
3. Other consequences, including slower delivery, follow. No finer ranking among the remaining consequences was agreed.

Strategy and business objectives are themselves information the team must find, understand, reconcile and reuse, not merely abstract links to attach after the audit work. The magnitude and frequency of these effects remain unquantified; no quantified savings are claimed.

### What is the initial solution concept?
Confirmed by the owner during the 5 November 2026 voice discussion as the intended product outcome, not a verified description of current capabilities. Agreed wording:

Inquello gives audit teams connected, source backed understanding of their organization and an agent that uses it to perform audit work alongside them, connecting objectives, risks, evidence, judgments, and actions across planning, engagements, and follow up, helping teams cover more significant risks and produce defensible, business relevant conclusions without rebuilding context over and over. Auditors retain direction, judgment, and approval.

Proposed mechanisms to elaborate in the press release and FAQs:
- Retrieve and question organizational knowledge with visible sources.
- Actively perform permitted audit work alongside the team.
- Preserve evidence and reasoning through review, corrections, follow-up and future planning.

The cross-industry direction, flexible methodology and sensible standards-based V1 remain the working context. The three-cycle map covers risk-based audit coverage planning; individual engagements through evidence, findings and report; and management-action follow-up. Delivery scope and performance must be tested before these aspirations become launch claims.

## Press Release
Imagined finished-product announcement for the Working Backwards exercise. The wording below is approved product vision, not a statement of released capabilities or demonstrated results.

### Headline
Inquello helps internal audit teams cover more significant risks with an AI agent that understands their organization and works alongside them.

### Subheadline
Auditors and reviewers work with connected organizational knowledge and hands-on AI assistance to reach defensible, business relevant conclusions with less repeated context building and rework.

Inquello is an AI-powered workbench for internal audit teams. It brings together organizational strategy, objectives, risks, previous audit work, and evidence, then uses that context to help auditors plan coverage, perform procedures, investigate exceptions, and develop supported conclusions. Across engagements, review, and follow-up, it keeps knowledge and reasoning connected while auditors retain direction, judgment, and approval.

Many internal audit teams spend too much time finding, reconnecting, checking, and repackaging information across the audit lifecycle. Inputs are scattered, assumptions get reused without challenge, and evidence and reasoning drift between tools and versions. That limits coverage of significant risks, weakens the quality and relevance of conclusions, and slows delivery while consuming scarce capacity.

### Solution and How It Works
Inquello gives auditors one conversational workspace to direct audit work. They describe what they need, provide files or connect permitted sources, and the agent carries out the task with that information and the organization context. It asks for missing inputs or permission when needed, shows progress, and produces work you can inspect, question, and revise. You steer it as it happens and approve consequential actions. Evidence, analysis, and decisions stay connected so the next task builds on what's already known.

### Leader quote
"We want auditors to cover more significant risks and deliver more continuous, business-relevant assurance. Inquello lets them delegate recurring, information-heavy procedures and scheduled checks to an agent while they focus on investigation, professional judgment, and the implications for the organization. Each engagement strengthens what the team knows for next time, so they can keep up as risks and priorities change."

### Illustrative future-customer quote
"Before, we spent so much time pulling information together that we couldn't examine everything we needed to. With Inquello, I can ask the agent to investigate, examine its work, and steer it further without starting from scratch each time. My team can cover more of the risks that matter and spend more attention on what the evidence means for the business."

### Getting Started
Start by creating your organization. Use guided setup and settings to establish roles and permissions, connections, methodology. Bring in strategy, objectives, audit universe, past work, whatever you already have. The agent helps organize that and flags missing pieces. Then start your first engagement in the conversation workspace, pick model and options there, give instructions, review outputs. If something needs a connection or a setting, Inquello points you there.

### 1. What's the hardest technical problem here?: approved working answer
Delivering rigorous, long-running audit work at scale through a light conversational experience, with verifiable results that auditors can confidently review without specifying every step or repeating the work themselves.

#### Why this leads
- A human can guide the work and judge whether a procedure is appropriate, but cannot feasibly recheck millions of records one by one to establish that the agent executed it dependably.
- Verification that the agreed procedure was faithfully executed is distinct from professional judgment about whether it was the right procedure. Human review does not substitute for evidence of faithful execution.
- Coverage, exclusions and failures must be demonstrable. Recovery after interruption must not silently omit records or duplicate work. The team needs evidence of what completed, what did not and what the result covers, without repeating the entire task.
- Evidence honesty and knowledge integrity remain supporting technical challenges. Claims must stay grounded in the actual work and sources, and accumulated organizational knowledge must remain trustworthy rather than silently propagating unsupported or stale assumptions.

The owner approved this refined formulation as a working answer that can evolve. It retains the execution/verification challenge while making the light conversational experience part of the problem to solve. It does not claim that the current product has solved it. The verification approach, recovery design, test criteria and supporting evidence remain to be worked through.

#### Possible first proof: proposals, not approved scope
- Define the population and agreed scope, including explicit exclusions.
- Reconcile processing coverage against that population, showing completed, excluded, failed and unfinished work. Spot checks alone do not establish full coverage.
- Demonstrate safe interruption recovery without silently omitting records or duplicating work.
- Make the method, processing work, evidence, exceptions and conclusions inspectable without requiring the auditor to repeat the entire task.
- Report completion honestly, distinguishing verified completion from partial work, unresolved failures and uncertainty.
- Use suitable processing tools for substantial data work while keeping the user experience simple. The auditor should be able to direct and review the work without specifying every execution step.

These remain preliminary detailed proof considerations, not an approved implementation plan, selected architecture, benchmark or test dataset. Internal FAQ 9 subsequently records the approved overarching requirements before real engagement use; exact test criteria still need to be specified.

### 4. How will we know the pilot is working?: approved measures
Look for evidence in real audit tasks:
- Risk coverage: Can the team examine more of the relevant population, or address important risks it otherwise lacked capacity to cover?
- Quality: Can auditors trace, challenge and use the evidence and work produced? Are significant gaps and errors caught?
- Experience: Can they direct and review the work comfortably, without having to spell out or repeat every step?
- Value and capacity: Does it give the team more room for judgment and work connected to the organisation’s objectives?

We would establish a baseline with the pilot team and compare actual tasks, rather than promise a percentage improvement now.

The owner agreed to these measurement dimensions. The baseline, measurement methods and numerical targets remain to be determined with the pilot team. No numerical target, improvement percentage or demonstrated result is claimed.

### 6. What is explicitly in V1, and what is outside it?: approved scope boundary
The owner approved the following V1 boundary, including the supported recurring check in the pilot. This is the current agreed scope; exact procedures, connections and the extent of recurring checks remain for the detailed V1 specification.

#### In V1
One organisation, one internal audit team, an end-to-end journey:

1. Set up: Create the organisation and workspace, configure an auditor and manager, with admin responsibilities, permissions and a basic audit methodology. Bring in available strategy, objectives, audit universe and past work.
2. Plan what to audit: Use organisational context, risks, past findings and outstanding actions to help the manager assess priorities and build an audit plan. The manager can review and change the proposed plan.
3. Plan an engagement: Define its objectives, scope, risks, controls and work programme, with the agent helping prepare them for the team to review.
4. Perform the audit: Provide evidence and direct the agent through conversation. It can perform supported procedures, analyse data, prepare workpapers and surface exceptions, gaps and evidence for the auditor to inspect and steer.
5. Review and report: The manager reviews and requests changes; the team develops findings and a report grounded in the work performed. Formal conclusions and sign-off stay with the people.
6. Follow up: Record agreed actions, owners and due dates; review progress and retain unresolved items as context for future planning. Include a supported recurring check where the necessary data and permissions exist, with visible failures and results.

The workspace and conversation experience connect those stages. The auditor should not have to operate our execution machinery to get dependable work.

#### Explicitly outside V1
- External-audit-specific workflows and standards.
- Multiple client organisations and switching between them, as needed by an external audit firm.
- Large-team features such as complex reporting hierarchies, resource scheduling and capacity management across many teams.
- A broad marketplace of integrations or a custom connector for every customer system. V1 still needs practical file intake and the specific connections required for the pilot.
- Automatic migration of every historical audit system. V1 should still let the team bring in useful history; formats and assistance need defining.
- Autonomous issuance of reports or replacement of professional judgment and management sign-off.

#### Deliberately unresolved
The exact supported procedures, connections and extent of recurring checks belong in the detailed V1 specification; they are not assumed to be unlimited. History-import formats and assistance also need defining. This approved journey does not by itself select data-storage architecture or resolve every earlier V1/V2 record-system assumption; reconcile older planning material against this current scope during specification. No delivery date or broader rollout scope is added.

## The Verdict

### Concept strength
Inquello has a clear, coherent direction for detailed PRD work. The user, problem, benefit priorities, intended working experience and end-to-end V1 boundary are specific enough to guide decisions. The strongest idea is not simply an audit chatbot: it is substantial, inspectable audit work carried out with connected organisational context, while auditors retain direction and professional judgment.

The central promise is still unproved. Neither reliable long-running execution nor real-team adoption, permission to use sensitive audit material, willingness to pay or sustainable cost to serve has been demonstrated in this exercise. Completing this PRFAQ establishes a planning foundation, not product readiness or a proven business. The concept needs evidence, not a broader promise.

### Forged in steel
- **Customer and priority:** Internal auditors and their reviewers have a concrete context-reconstruction problem. Significant-risk coverage leads, followed by conclusion quality and business relevance; reduced rework and faster delivery support those outcomes.
- **Experience and responsibility:** The agent performs delegated work rather than only advising. Auditors can inspect, question and steer it, and people retain final conclusions, formal sign-off and report issuance. Setup combines guided settings with conversational assistance.
- **Connected scope:** The approved V1 covers one organisation and team across coverage planning, engagement execution/review/reporting and management-action follow-up, including a supported recurring check. Explicit exclusions keep wider firm, large-team and integration-marketplace demands out.
- **Honest learning sequence:** The owner has distinguished real-use blockers from decisions that can be resolved while building and refinements that follow learning. Unknown pricing, pilot measures and portability details are visible rather than disguised as commitments.

These strengths concern clarity and consistency of the concept. They are not independent evidence of customer demand or achieved performance.

### Needs more heat
- **A testable V1 specification:** Define supported procedures, initial connections/file intake, recurring-check limits, review transitions and onboarding readiness. Specify the full approved journey, with focused proofs along the way; do not substitute a single demonstration for that journey.
- **Verifiable execution with a light interface:** Turn the coverage, exclusions, failure visibility, recovery and inspectability principles into concrete acceptance criteria. The detailed first-proof ideas remain proposals until selected. Preserve an auditor-friendly experience rather than exposing execution machinery as the workflow.
- **Records and portability:** Decide which system holds which authoritative records and how evidence, workpapers, findings and actions stay connected across stages and exports. Reconcile the older existing-system/V1 and native-record/V2 assumptions explicitly with the latest approved scope before relying on that architecture.
- **Pilot and commercial evidence:** Identify an appropriately authorised engagement, relevant decision makers and representative tasks. Establish a baseline, observe accepted work and rework, and measure full cost to serve. Resolve the relevant choices before reliance or purchase; no numerical target or launch date is established.
- **Resource realism:** Convert the preliminary founder-plus-agents picture into sequenced work, dependencies, verification effort and targeted specialist needs before making timing or budget commitments. No delivery estimate is supported yet.

### Cracks in the foundation
- **Plausible work could conceal incomplete execution.** Human judgment cannot prove that millions of records were faithfully processed. Before real use, demonstrate dependable supported execution and recovery, with visible scope, failures and reviewable evidence; define how completion is established rather than relying on reassuring prose or spot checks alone.
- **A successful demonstration may not become an accepted audit workflow.** Test-data use does not establish that auditors will rely on the results, data owners will permit processing or budget holders will buy. Use authorised real engagement work, observe what people accept or redo, and discuss security, procurement and willingness to pay before calling the business case proven.
- **Data-access and privacy promises can fail independently of analytical quality.** The private-work/admin separation, provider processing and revocable access promises need explicit design and verification for the chosen pilot. Agree authorised data and use before real engagement activity; investigate the pilot organisation's applicable security, contractual and legal requirements rather than assuming blanket compliance.
- **Economics and differentiation remain hypotheses.** Heavy or continuous work may be costly, and established tools already advertise lifecycle AI, collaboration and contextual assistance. Measure representative total costs and customer value, then choose a commercial arrangement before purchase. Earn differentiation through dependable, usable outcomes; do not claim exclusivity or superiority from the concept alone.

Use this PRFAQ and `prfaq-inquello-distillate.md` as inputs to create or reconcile the detailed PRD. The latest approved scope and readiness sequence govern; retain unresolved decisions with their evidence needs and decision points. Build planning and research can proceed in parallel without waiting for exhaustive practitioner research, but the agreed real-use gates remain binding.

No new product decision is required merely to close this exercise. Selecting the pilot, concrete supported tasks/connections, record architecture and acceptance criteria is the next substantive planning work. This assessment recommends that work; it does not authorise implementation, external contact, customer-data processing, publication or release.
